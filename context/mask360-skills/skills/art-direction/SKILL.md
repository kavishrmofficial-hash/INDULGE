# Mask360 Art Direction & Tastemaking System

Art direction at Mask360 is the discipline of making decisions about what beauty looks like, and then communicating those decisions clearly enough that a photographer, editor, or AI model can execute them without losing anything in translation.

Good taste is not subjective. It is trained. It is the accumulation of knowing what has been done, what has failed, and what feels inevitable rather than forced.

---

## Part 1: Creative Briefing

### The Shoot Brief (Standard Document)

Every shoot Mask360 art directs must have a brief. Not a mood board alone, a brief with words that guide every decision on set.

**Brief structure:**

**1. The Single Idea**
One sentence. What is this shoot fundamentally about? Not the product. The feeling.
Example: "This shoot is about the quiet confidence of arriving somewhere that was made for you."

**2. Visual References**
3-5 images max. Not mood board dumps, curated. Each reference should earn its place.
For each reference: what specifically we are borrowing (the light? the colour grade? the proximity of the camera to subject? the casting?)

**3. What We Are Not Doing**
As important as the brief itself. Name the tropes this shoot is avoiding.
Example: "No cheesy couple-smiling-at-each-other shots. No staged-looking wine-on-the-balcony. No receptionist-welcoming-guest cliché."

**4. Light Direction**
- Time of day and quality of light
- Natural vs. artificial vs. mixed
- Hard vs. soft
- Warm vs. cool
- Where shadows should fall

**5. Camera Direction**
- Focal length preference (35mm feels intimate and close; 85mm compresses and elevates; 24mm gives context)
- Distance to subject (intimate / mid / establishing)
- Camera movement (still / slow push / handheld grit / crane / drone)
- Frame ratio (16:9 / 4:5 / 1:1 / 9:16 for vertical-first)

**6. Casting / Talent Direction**
- Demographics: age range, ethnicity, energy
- Wardrobe notes: specific pieces, colour palette, avoid
- Expression / body language direction: What do they feel? Not what they do.
- What they are NOT doing (no forced smiles, no pointing at things)

**7. Location Notes**
- Hero locations / scenes
- Props to include / avoid
- Time constraints (golden hour, peak occupancy to avoid, etc.)

**8. Deliverables**
- Number of final selects expected
- Format requirements (resolution, aspect ratios for each platform)
- Edit style: heavy retouch vs. natural / warm grade vs. neutral / film grain yes/no

### Videographer / AI Direction Brief

For video (live shoot or Higgsfield AI generation):

**Scene by scene:**
- Scene 1: [Location] / [Subject] / [Camera movement] / [Duration] / [Feeling]
- Scene 2: etc.

**Edit direction:**
- Pacing: Fast cut / slow / rhythmic to music
- Music tempo and genre suggestion
- Colour grade reference
- On-screen text: Yes/no / style / placement
- Transitions: Hard cut / dissolve / AI morph

## Part 2: Taste Standards

### What Makes Luxury Visual Work Good

**Restraint.** The most powerful luxury images leave space. Negative space is not empty, it is intentional. Compression of too many elements kills aspiration.

**Specificity.** Generic luxury looks like stock photography. Specific luxury has a detail that couldn't exist anywhere else: the texture of a particular wall, the way light hits a specific marble, the weight of a particular fabric.

**Tension.** The best luxury images create slight unease, something withheld, a moment about to happen, a presence not quite visible. Perfect, safe images are forgettable.

**Authenticity of imperfection.** A slightly unmade bed. A glass with a fingerprint. A model mid-movement not posed. These feel real, which in luxury is increasingly rare and therefore valuable.

**Colour discipline.** The most elevated work limits its palette to 2-3 colours per image. More than that = noise.

---

### What Makes Visual Work Mediocre (The Mask360 Trash List)

These are signals that something was shot without direction or edited without taste:

**Photography:**
- Subject looking directly at camera and smiling in an editorial context
- Over-retouched skin that looks plastic
- HDR or over-sharpened textures
- Symmetry forced for its own sake without tension
- "Lifestyle" shots that feel staged (fake conversations, pointing at menus)
- Stock-feeling light: flat, even, shadowless
- Too many elements competing in one frame

**Videography:**
- Generic drone establishing shots without purpose
- Whip pans used as transitions without narrative reason
- Music that starts immediately without letting a sound design moment breathe
- Colour grades that are too warm (orange) or too teal, the Instagram filter problem
- Talking heads without cutaways
- Text overlays using the wrong font weight for the brand

**Digital / AI-generated:**
- Midjourney default style: over-saturated, perfect, soulless
- AI faces that are too symmetrical and smooth
- AI interiors where the light source makes no physical sense
- Generated images where the context (signage, shadows, reflections) gives away the generation

### The Taste Calibration: Reference Worlds

When briefing any creative output, Mask360 art direction draws from these reference worlds:

**For luxury hospitality:** Aman Resorts visual identity. Slim Aarons photography. The Standard Hotels campaign work.

**For fashion / editorial:** Harley Weir's intimacy. Craig McDean's precision. Nick Knight's abstraction.

**For jewellery:** Cartier's long-form film tradition. Van Cleef's narrative approach. ZORÁE's own emerging language: minimal, symbolic, worn not displayed.

**For architecture / interiors:** Architectural Digest India shoot quality. Vast Magazine. The Natural Beauty aesthetic, organic materials, natural light, no studio feeling.

**For agency showcase:** The work of Wieden+Kennedy Tokyo. Droga5's craft standards. Forsman & Bodenfors' humanity.

### Giving Feedback on Creative Work

When Kaavish needs to critique or approve visual work, use this structure:

**What's working (always start here):**
One specific thing, not vague praise. "The light in frame 3 creates the right tension" not "I like this one."

**What's not working (be specific):**
Name the exact problem. "The model's expression in the hero shot reads as discomfort not confidence" not "this doesn't feel right."

**The fix:**
Specific, actionable. "Reshoot with softer direction, less posed, caught mid-movement" not "make it better."

## Output Format

For a shoot brief: Full document following the 8-section structure above
For a campaign aesthetic: Single Idea + Visual references + Colour palette + Lighting direction + What to avoid
For creative feedback: What's working / What's not / The fix (specific for each)
For AI video direction: Scene-by-scene breakdown with Higgsfield/Kling prompt language
