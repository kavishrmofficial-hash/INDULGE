# Mask360 Agency Negotiation System

Negotiation at Mask360 is not about being cheap or being stubborn. It is about knowing what everything is worth, knowing what the agency needs to survive and grow, and never letting desperation drive a decision.

The agency that discounts out of fear trains clients to expect discounts. The agency that holds its price trains clients to respect its value.

**The Mask360 Negotiation Prime Directive: Never lose a deal. But never win a deal at the cost of the agency's dignity, margin, or positioning.**

## Part 2: Margin Architecture

### The Mask360 Margin Rules

**Minimum gross margin on any project: 40%**
Below 40% = the agency is working for the client's rent, not its own growth.

**Retainer margin target: 50-60%**
Retainers are scalable. They should carry higher margin than projects.

**Vendor markup standard:**
- India: 20-30% markup on all vendor costs passed through
- UAE: 15-25% markup (market is more price-aware)
- Never reveal vendor costs to clients. Quote a production fee, not a line-item breakdown.

**How to calculate a project quote:**

1. List all internal hours × team rate
2. List all vendor costs × 1.25 (markup)
3. Add 15% contingency buffer
4. Add agency profit margin (min 40% of total)
5. Round to a clean number, never quote ₹1,73,429

**Example (India):**
- Internal hours: 40hrs × ₹2,500/hr = ₹1,00,000
- Vendor costs: ₹30,000 × 1.25 = ₹37,500
- Contingency (15%): ₹20,625
- Subtotal: ₹1,58,125
- At 40% margin: Quote = ₹2,22,000 → Round to ₹2,25,000

## Part 3: Client-Side Negotiation

### The Pricing Conversation Framework

**Step 1: Anchor high, intentionally.**
Always present a number above your target. The first number sets the psychological anchor. If you want ₹2,00,000, open at ₹2,50,000.

**Step 2: When they push back, don't drop the price. Drop the scope.**
"I can bring this to ₹2,00,000, but that would mean removing the performance marketing component and reducing reels from 4 to 2 per month. Do you want me to restructure the proposal on that basis?"

This does three things: It signals the price is justified by the scope. It makes the client feel like they're choosing, not winning. It protects the margin.

**Step 3: The concession must cost them something.**
Never give a discount for free. If you concede on price, extract something: longer commitment, faster payment terms, a case study right, a referral.

**Step 4: The walk-away line.**
Know it before you enter the conversation. If the client's budget is below Mask360's floor, say this:

> "I want to work with [Brand], the fit is right. But I'd be doing you a disservice by taking this on at a budget that doesn't allow us to do it properly. Let's revisit when the budget is closer to what the brief deserves. In the meantime, I'm happy to suggest what's achievable at your current number with a different scope."

This is not losing the deal. This is leaving the door open without compromising the agency.

### Handling Common Client Tactics

**"Your competitor quoted us half the price."**
Response: "That's worth exploring. The question is what's included, and what happens when it doesn't work. We've seen that before. Happy to walk you through specifically what our number covers and why."

Never ask who the competitor is. Never match their price. Offer specificity instead.

**"Can you do a trial month at a lower rate?"**
Response: "I understand the hesitation, a new agency relationship is a commitment. What I can offer is a 90-day pilot at full rate with a clear set of KPIs we both agree on upfront. If we don't hit them, we'll have a genuine conversation. But starting below rate sets the wrong foundation for both of us."

**"We'll pay more once results come in."**
Response: "I appreciate the intention, but results-based retainers work for performance channels, not for brand building. What I can do is build quarterly review points into the contract where we assess performance together and adjust scope accordingly."

**"We have no budget right now, but this will be big."**
Response: "Tell me more about the timeline." (Qualify before walking away, some become real. Most don't.) If real: "We can structure a deferred payment arrangement for the right client, but it requires a signed contract and a partial upfront." If vague: politely exit.

**Scope creep mid-retainer:**
"Happy to add that, let me send you a brief addendum with the cost for that additional deliverable. The current retainer covers [X]; this would be [Y] on top."

Never do extra work first and charge later. Always quote before executing.

## Part 4: Vendor-Side Negotiation

### The Vendor Negotiation Mindset

Mask360 is a repeat buyer. Vendors should want the relationship. Use that leverage, but never abuse it. Burnt vendors deliver bad work.

**The ask is always specific:**
Don't say "Can you do it cheaper?" Say: "Our production budget for this job is ₹18,000, can you make that work?"

Giving a number first (on vendor side) anchors their counter lower.

**What to trade instead of cash:**
- Credit on deliverables ("We'll tag you in everything we post")
- Faster payment ("We'll pay within 7 days instead of 30")
- Volume commitment ("This is the first of 4 shoots this quarter")
- Portfolio value ("This is Anantara, it's yours to use")

---

### Vendor Negotiation Scripts

**Opening (new vendor):**
"We're bringing on a new vendor for a shoot series, approximately [X] shoots over [timeframe]. Our budget per shoot is [₹/AED]. Is that something you can work with?"

**When vendor quotes high:**
"Appreciate the quote, we're a bit over budget. We can go to [X]. I know that's a gap, but we're looking at this as a longer-term relationship. Is there a way to make this first one work?"

**When vendor won't move:**
"I understand, let me check internally and come back to you." (Never say no in the room. Take time. Either find more budget or find another vendor. Never let desperation show.)

**When vendor delivers late / below standard:**
"We need to talk about the deliverables before we settle the invoice. [Specific issue]. We'd like [specific remedy, reshoot / partial refund / credit toward next job] before we close this out."

## Part 5: Deal Decision Framework

### Should Mask360 Take This Deal?

Run every deal through this filter before saying yes:

| Question | If NO → |
|---|---|
| Is margin above 40%? | Reprice or restructure scope |
| Is the client brand-aligned? | Assess reputational risk |
| Are payment terms acceptable? | Negotiate terms or walk |
| Is the brief executable with current team? | Scope down or timeline out |
| Will this client be a good reference? | Factor into pricing decision |
| Is this relationship likely to grow? | Weight long-term vs. short-term |

**If 4 or more are NO: Walk away. Politely. With the door open.**
