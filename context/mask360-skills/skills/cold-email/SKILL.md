# Mask360 Cold Outreach v2

Trained on what Kaavish actually approved and what he killed with "nah, sucks" and "yuck i cannot send this". The old default (observation + proof + micro-ask on every email) is retired for lists. It survives only in named mode below. When this file and instinct disagree, follow this file.

## the one rule

The email has exactly one job: the reply. The signature, the roster and the credentials link do the selling. Any line that positions, observes, or pitches lowers the reply rate. When in doubt, cut the line.

## two modes

**LIST MODE** (Apollo blasts, category lists, credentials sends to many). Default mode. All lowercase body and signature. Brand names and acronyms keep their caps (Ferrari, PR, H2). 3 to 5 content lines. Question-led. Merge tag is [first name].

**NAMED MODE** (1:1 to a specific senior person at a named company). Proper case. One sharp observation about their world, one proof point, one micro-ask. 6 to 8 line ceiling. Written for an ex-agency reader who detects padding instantly. Research the person and the brand first. Every claim must be publicly citable. No insider information ever.

## the list formula (proven across verticals)

line 1: a direct human question about upcoming briefs. nothing before it.
line 2: one line of category fluency, naming real deliverables in their vocabulary. never a service list, never positioning.
line 3 (when a credentials microsite exists for the category): the link, framed as everything on one page. the link IS the pitch, so the email gets shorter, not longer.
line 4: low-friction close.

signature, always exactly this:

kaavish ramchandani
chief alchemist, mask360
mumbai | delhi | bangalore | dubai | abu dhabi

The approved specimen (real estate, Apr 2026):

> subject: any launches coming up?
>
> hi [first name],
>
> do you have any project launches, campaign briefs, or content requirements lined up for the second half of the year?
>
> we work with real estate brands on launch campaigns, project films, social media, brochures and collateral, and PR. basically the full creative and marketing side of a project.
>
> if something is in the pipeline, happy to understand the brief and see if we can be useful.

That is the register. A person asking a question, not an agency pitching.

## category vocabulary (talk like you are in their room)

- real estate: project launches, launch campaigns, project films, brochures and site collateral, channel partner content, site visits
- auto: model launches, test drive event content, dealership collateral (proof: Ferrari)
- hospitality: property films, F&B launches, occupancy seasons (proof: Anantara, St. Regis)
- beauty and personal care: launches, festive pushes, new collections (proof: L'Oréal, Dior, Dyson)
- conglomerates and corporate: lead with portfolio scale and one flagship credential, never a pitch

One credential per email, maximum. Logo dumping killed drafts. The credential can also live entirely in the link or the signature and nowhere in the body. Curiosity does the work.

## subject lines

Lowercase, 2 to 5 words, question-shaped, reads like an internal email. Proven winners: "any launches coming up?", "quick question", "upcoming projects?", "closing the loop". Always give 2 alternates for a/b.

## hard bans (every one of these killed a real draft)

- category commentary or market observations of any kind ("the category is noisier than ever", "india right now is genuinely exciting", "quick commerce is changing the repurchase game")
- "most [category] brands are sitting on..."
- "we're selectively opening capacity" and any manufactured scarcity paragraph
- opening the email with I or We
- service lists, bullet points, or headers inside the email
- em dashes and en dashes, anywhere, ever
- "hope this finds you well", "looking forward to hearing from you", "worth 15 minutes?" as a list-mode closer
- long HTML credential emails and "We would like to propose an association" (the dead 8K blast format)
- "no decks, just a conversation" (worn out, retire it)

## psychology

Pattern interrupt is the master trigger: everyone is so used to being pitched that a genuine question with zero fluff stops them. Nothing is being sold, so the guard never goes up. Scarcity only ever as a passing clause ("before the calendars fill up"), never as a paragraph. Secondary trigger varies by category: loss aversion for real estate and hospitality, identity and novelty for beauty, authority and scale for conglomerates. Indian clients specifically need peer-to-peer tone and context they recognise, not global agency polish.

## follow-up

- day 4: one line that adds one new thing (a film, a number, the microsite if not yet sent). never repeat the pitch.
- day 10: the soft exit. subject "closing the loop". this touch gets the most replies of the three.
- never more than 3 email touches. then move to LinkedIn.
- clicked-but-no-reply retarget lists go straight to the soft exit.

## LinkedIn DM version

Proper case. 3 to 4 sentences. Same angle as the email, one link or credential maximum. If an email already went out: "Sent you an email last week, shorter version here:"

## output format

1. the email (subject + body, list mode unless a person is named)
2. two alternate subject lines
3. LinkedIn DM variant only when asked or when the play is DM-first

One verdict draft by default. A second variant only when the audience genuinely splits (D2C vs corporate, named vs list). No explanation of why it works unless asked.
