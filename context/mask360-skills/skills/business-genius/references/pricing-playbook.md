# Pricing Playbook

## Core Principle
Price is a signal. High price = high quality in the customer's mind. The first question isn't "what can we charge?", it's "what belief does this price create?"

## Pricing Architecture Templates

### The Three-Tier Stack
Always offer three tiers. Psychology: customers anchor to the middle.
- **Entry**: accessible, limited: creates trial and pipeline
- **Core**: the real product, the target margin, this is what most buy
- **Premium/Bespoke**: high-touch, high-margin: for clients who want the best

Example (Agency):
- Starter Retainer: ₹1.2L/mo (social only)
- Growth Retainer: ₹3.5L/mo (social + content + strategy)
- Full-Service Retainer: ₹8L+/mo (360°, bespoke, leadership access)

### Value-Based Pricing
Don't price based on time/cost. Price based on value delivered.
- "Our campaign drove ₹2Cr in revenue for Client X" → your fee should be a fraction of that, not based on hours
- Frame: "Our retainer is ₹5L/month. The brands we work with typically see [outcome]. That's a 10x return."

### Anchor Pricing
Show the expensive option first so everything else feels reasonable.
- Present the premium tier before the standard tier
- Use round numbers for anchors, specific numbers for real prices (₹10L anchor → ₹3,75,000 actual feels precise and considered)

### Decoy Effect
A slightly worse option at a similar price makes the preferred option look better.
- Option A: ₹2L/mo (10 posts/month)
- Option B (decoy): ₹2.8L/mo (10 posts + 2 reels, not great value)
- Option C: ₹3.2L/mo (10 posts + 5 reels + strategy, much better value)
- C gets chosen most often because B makes C look like a deal

## Pricing Reframe Scripts

### Cost → Investment
"Our retainer is ₹3.5L/month."
→ "Our retainer is ₹3.5L/month. Most of our clients see that returned within the first campaign."

### Monthly → Daily
"₹3.5L/month sounds like a lot."
→ "That's ₹11,600 a day for a full marketing team, strategy, content, and execution."

### Compare to Cost of Inaction
"Why should I pay this?"
→ "What's the cost of not having a consistent brand presence for another 12 months? Lost leads, lost positioning, competitors gaining ground. This fee is insurance against that."

### The Expertise Premium
"Other agencies charge less."
→ "Yes, and they work with clients at that level. We work with Ferrari and Dior. You're not paying for execution; you're paying for judgment."

## When to Hold Price
- When the client is pushing back on price but not on value
- When losing this client wouldn't hurt you financially or reputationally
- When discounting would signal weakness and undermine future negotiations
- Offer to reduce scope, never reduce rate (rate is your brand)

## When to Discount
- Strategic client (opens a door, category, or region)
- First engagement (trial pricing with clear escalation clause)
- Volume/duration (annual retainer vs. monthly)
- Never discount just because they asked, that's a negotiation failure
