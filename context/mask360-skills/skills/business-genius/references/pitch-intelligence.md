# Pitch Intelligence

## The Anatomy of a Winning Pitch

A pitch is not a presentation of capabilities. It's a story about the client's future.

Structure:
1. **Mirror**: Show them you understand their world better than they do
2. **Diagnosis**: Name the problem (even if they didn't ask you to)
3. **Vision**: Paint the world where the problem is solved
4. **Proof**: Show you've done this before (or something adjacent)
5. **Plan**: Make it feel specific and executable
6. **Ask**: Clear, confident, no hedging

## Reading the Room

### Before the Pitch
- Research: what are they proud of? What are they embarrassed about? What's changed recently for them?
- Find the real buyer vs. the gatekeeper (they are often different people)
- Discover their internal pressure points: is this pitch about revenue, or about the CMO needing a win?
- LinkedIn-stalk the decision maker: what do they post about? What do they care about?

### In the Pitch
- First 90 seconds: establish that you understand their business, not your credentials
- Watch for "lean-in" moments: when someone sits forward, you've hit something real
- Silence is powerful: don't rush to fill it after making a strong point
- Match their energy: a conservative CFO in the room means you slow down, get precise
- The best pitch feels like a conversation, not a presentation

### Questions to Ask (That Make You Look Brilliant)
- "What's the one thing that has to be true for this to be a success for you?"
- "If we were sitting here 12 months from now and everything had gone perfectly, what does that look like?"
- "What's been tried before and why didn't it work?"
- "Who else is involved in this decision?" (Discover the real power map)
- "What's your timeline, and what's driving it?"

## Handling Objections

### "Your price is too high"
Don't justify. Anchor to value.
"I understand. Let's talk about what the right outcome looks like for you, and then we can work backward to what makes sense."
Or: "Compared to what? What's the cost of not solving this?"

### "We're talking to other agencies"
"That's smart. You should. Here's the one question I'd ask all of them: [insert differentiating question that only you can answer well]."

### "We need to think about it"
"Of course. What would make you more confident? Is there something we haven't addressed?"
Or: "What would need to be true for this to be a clear yes?"

### "Can you do it for less?"
Never reduce rate, reduce scope.
"We can make this work within your budget, here's what we'd prioritize and what we'd defer. Does that still solve the core problem?"

## Closing Mechanics

### The Assumptive Close
Behave as if the deal is done: "When we onboard you, we'll start with a discovery session..."
This shifts the mental frame from "if" to "when."

### The Urgency Close (Only When Real)
"We have two other clients we're in final conversations with and our Q2 capacity is limited. I'd hate for timing to get in the way."
Only use if true. Manufactured urgency erodes trust.

### The Summary Close
"Let me summarize what we've agreed: [restate everything]. Does that feel right to you?"
Gets micro-commitments throughout → closing is just the last one.

## Pitch Mistakes

1. **Starting with "About Us"**: Nobody cares until they believe you understand them
2. **Too many capabilities, not enough empathy**: Feature dump kills emotional engagement
3. **Vague language**: "We create impactful content" means nothing. "We grew Ferrari India's engagement 240% in 90 days" means everything.
4. **Not knowing who the decision maker is**: Pitching to someone who can't say yes
5. **No clear ask**: Ending without "so, shall we get started?" leaves deals in limbo
6. **Defending instead of reframing**: When challenged, never defend; reframe around value
7. **Matching their pessimism**: If they're skeptical, don't become cautious; stay confident and curious

## Agency-Specific Pitch Intelligence

### The Pre-Pitch Audit
Send a 1-page audit of their current social/brand presence before the meeting.
Shows: you already did the work, you see things they don't, you're invested.

### The Campaign Idea Close
End every pitch with one original campaign idea, not a generic one.
Something specific to their brand, executed to a creative level that makes them feel:
"They get us. And they made this before we even hired them."

### The Follow-Up That Gets Replied To
Not "Thanks for the meeting, please find attached..."
Instead: "One more thing I forgot to mention that I think could change the numbers significantly for you, [specific insight or idea]. Happy to walk through it quickly."
Gives them a reason to respond that isn't just "yes or no on the pitch."
