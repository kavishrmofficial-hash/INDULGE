# Brand Strategy Reference

## What a Brand Actually Is
A brand is not a logo or a color palette. A brand is a set of associations stored in someone's memory. It's the sum of every experience, piece of content, interaction, and word-of-mouth they've ever had with you.

Strong brand = predictable feelings. Weak brand = no feelings.

## The Brand Identity Stack

### 1. Purpose (Why)
Why does this brand exist beyond making money? The reason that makes people feel something.
- Not "we sell great jewelry" → "we believe beauty shouldn't cost the earth"
- Not "we're a marketing agency" → "we exist to make brands impossible to ignore"

### 2. Positioning (Who, Against What)
Two questions: "Who is this for?" and "Who is this NOT for?"
- Great brands repel as powerfully as they attract
- The sharper the audience definition, the stronger the brand
- Positioning = category + differentiation: "The [something] for [someone] who [belief/behavior]"

### 3. Personality (How)
If this brand were a person at a dinner party, who would they be?
- Define 3-4 personality traits that guide all communication
- Anti-traits (what we are NOT) are equally important, prevents drift
- Example: ZORÁE = quietly confident, intentional, unshowy, modern, ethical. NOT: flashy, accessible, trend-chasing, loud

### 4. Promise (What)
The one thing this brand consistently delivers. The expectation they set and must always meet.
- Volvo: safe
- Apple: beautiful simplicity
- Hermès: timelessness
- What is the brand's one-word promise?

## Positioning Tools

### The Perceptual Map
Plot the category on two axes (e.g., Traditional ↔ Modern / Mass ↔ Exclusive).
Where are competitors clustered? Find the white space.
Most categories have crowded middle ground and open premium space.

### The Empathy Map
Four quadrants: What does the target customer think? Feel? Say? Do?
Uncovers the emotional truth under rational behavior.

### The Brand Onion
- **Core**: Values and beliefs (never changes)
- **Middle**: Personality and communication style (evolves slowly)
- **Outer**: Products, campaigns, visuals (changes with time)
Start from the core, not the outer.

## Common Brand Mistakes

1. **Trying to be for everyone**: dies in the middle, stands for nothing
2. **Visual before verbal**: logo before story, design before positioning
3. **Brand ≠ Branding**: investing in assets without building associations
4. **Inconsistency**: different tone on each channel, confused identity
5. **Chasing trends**: brands that follow culture lose; brands that shape culture win
6. **Competing on features**: customers don't buy features, they buy feelings

## Premium Brand Principles (Luxury / High-End)

- **Scarcity of access**, not scarcity of product (the brand decides who gets in)
- **Heritage or intentionality** replaces mass market credentials
- **Restraint in communication**: say less, mean more
- **Tactile over digital**: physical touchpoints (packaging, paper, texture) signal quality
- **The brand is the experience**: from first Instagram scroll to product in hand
- **Price anchors identity**: dropping price damages the brand more than losing a sale
- **Silence is a strategy**: not always on, not always selling
