# Growth Models Reference

## Revenue Model Teardowns

### Subscription / Retainer
- Predictable MRR; churn is the silent killer
- Net Revenue Retention (NRR) > 100% = expansion revenue (customers pay more over time)
- Key metric: MRR, Churn Rate, LTV:CAC ratio (healthy = 3:1 or better)
- Playbook: Land small → prove value → expand → lock in annual contracts

### Transactional (E-commerce, Project Work)
- AOV (Average Order Value) and Purchase Frequency are the dials
- Formula: Revenue = Traffic × Conversion Rate × AOV × Repeat Purchase Rate
- Improve any one of these to grow
- Highest leverage: repeat purchase rate (cheapest customer = existing customer)

### Marketplace / Platform
- Two-sided: must grow supply AND demand simultaneously
- Chicken-and-egg problem at launch → always seed one side artificially first
- Network effects are the moat; without them, it's just a listing site
- Take rate (commission %) is the business model; don't compete on rate, compete on value

### Licensing / IP
- High margin, low effort once built
- Requires strong brand or proprietary technology
- Playbook: build in one market, license to others (geographic, vertical, demographic)

### Lead Generation / Media
- Audience is the asset; monetization is secondary
- Build an audience of buyers → sell access (ads, sponsored content, affiliate)
- The pivot: content brand → product brand (works in reverse too)

## Growth Mechanics

### Viral Loops
- Inherent virality: using the product creates exposure (Zoom link, Canva watermark)
- Incentivized virality: referral programs, affiliate commissions
- Social virality: content people want to share (status, humor, utility)
- Key metric: viral coefficient K = invitations sent × conversion rate (K > 1 = exponential growth)

### Flywheel vs. Funnel
- Funnel: linear, one-time momentum
- Flywheel: circular, momentum compounds
- Amazon flywheel: more sellers → more selection → better prices → more customers → more sellers
- Build flywheels, not funnels

### The Hook Model (Nir Eyal)
Trigger → Action → Variable Reward → Investment
- Design products that become habits
- Variable reward = why gambling, social media, and great products are addictive
- Investment (data, reputation, relationships) = switching cost

### Cohort Analysis
- Don't look at aggregate retention, look at retention by cohort (month acquired)
- If newer cohorts retain better, the product is improving
- If retention flattens above 0% at any period, you have a core retained audience to build on
