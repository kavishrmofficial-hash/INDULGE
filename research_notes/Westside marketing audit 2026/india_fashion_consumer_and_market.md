## 1. Market: size, growth, structure, online vs offline, malls and footfall, tier 2/3

### Takeaway
India's apparel market is roughly USD 70-90bn and growing at about 8-12% a year. It is still overwhelmingly bought offline: about 80-85% of fashion spend is offline, and fashion e-commerce penetration is about 19% in FY25. Growth is concentrated in value price points (under ₹800 ASP) and in mall-based stores. Mall fashion stores get about 50% more footfall than high-street stores, and mall traffic grew about 15% YoY in FY26. Westside itself is about 94% store-driven, so footfall is the right currency for a Westside pitch.

### Inferences
- Westside's growth is coming from new stores (52 net in FY26), not from more shoppers per existing store, given low-single-digit to marginally negative LFL. A campaign judged on footfall at existing stores answers exactly the gap in Trent's numbers: LFL.
- About 94% of Westside's sales are in store, and fashion is about 80-85% offline. Social's main job for Westside is therefore to drive store visits rather than e-commerce clicks, which supports the pitch's footfall-based KPI.
- Value fashion (under ₹800 ASP, led by Zudio inside Trent itself) is taking the growth, and mid-premium is only tracking the category. Westside cannot win on price, so it has to justify its premium over Zudio with experience, styling, quality and occasion. This is a squeeze from below, not only competition from Zara above.
- Mall fashion stores get about 50% more footfall and about 40% higher weekend traffic, and 5-8 PM is about 46% of daily footfall. Experiential activations should cluster on mall weekends and weekday evenings. The first-two-minutes staff interaction finding supports a "styled welcome" service concept.
- Tier-3/4 footfall growth is slower (about 10%) but engagement is stronger. Westside's new-store expansion into smaller cities is where launch-moment experiential marketing could make the most difference.

### Gaps
- No Redseer or Technopak 2025-26 headline figure for the organised share of total apparel was found. The latest is Technopak 2023, men's apparel only.
- No dedicated 2025-26 national mall footfall report from Anarock, Knight Frank, CBRE or JLL was found. Only Enalytix (fashion stores) and single-mall operator claims are available. Knight Frank did not surface at all.
- No conversion-rate data by tier or day-part from Enalytix was found.
- No Westside-specific footfall, conversion or customer age data. Trent does not disclose it in the sources found.
- The share of Westside sales coming from family vs women's vs men's vs kids' categories was not found.

## 2. Consumer behaviour: discovery on social, research online / buy offline, creators, Gen Z, women, occasion dressing, styling help, in-store service, price sensitivity, quick commerce

### Takeaway
Discovery for Indian shoppers now starts on social, and mostly on Meta: the Meta + RAI whitepaper (Feb 2026) puts social's influence at 77%, with Meta driving 96% of social discovery. The purchase still mostly happens in store. Shoppers who mix channels spend about 2.5x as much, and creator content converts fast: 71% buy within a couple of days. KPMG finds Empathy is the biggest driver of apparel customer experience, and value for money the biggest attribute. Gen Z is promo-code driven. Women lead family and festive buying. There is no rigorous India data on demand for in-store styling help, so it is a gap to own rather than a proven need.

## 5. Creator economy in India: counts, costs by tier, regional creators, effectiveness

### Takeaway
India has about 2-4.5 million creators, depending on definition (BCG counts 2-2.5 mn with more than 1,000 followers; Kofluence counts 4.0-4.4 mn active, 61% nano). Creators reportedly influence more than 30% of consumer decisions, worth USD 350-400bn (BCG 2025). Only 8-10% monetise well. Nano Reels cost roughly ₹500-8,000, micro ₹8,000-80,000 (fashion and beauty micros with 50K followers about ₹25-60K), and macro roughly ₹84K to ₹7.9L per Reel. Most creators sit in Tier 2/3 cities, which supports a regional-language micro-creator strategy.

### Cited Findings
- BCG "From Content to Commerce: Mapping India's Creator Economy" (released at WAVES, 3 May 2025; 1,900+ consumers and 60+ brands): 2-2.5 mn active creators (more than 1,000 followers), only 8-10% of whom monetise effectively. Creators influence USD 350-400bn of annual consumer spend, projected to exceed USD 1 trn by 2030 — [BCG](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2025/india-from-content-to-commerce-mapping-indias-creator-economy); [PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2126106&reg=48&lang=2)
- BCG: creators influence "over 30% of consumer decisions". Outlets differ on the base of this 30% figure — [Buzzincontent](https://www.buzzincontent.com/influencer-marketing/creators-now-influence-over-30percent-of-consumer-decisions-driving-400-billion-in-spending-bcg-9026182); contrast [Patralok](https://www.patralok.com/bcg-creators-power-indias-next-economy/), which says "30% of these users make purchases influenced by" creators
- BCG: short-form video dominates, especially in comedy, fashion and entertainment. Brands are expected to raise creator-marketing investment 1.5-3x — [Indian Broadcasting World](https://www.indianbroadcastingworld.com/indias-creator-economy-influence/); [DD News](https://ddnews.gov.in/en/waves-2025-indias-creator-economy-set-to-influence-over-1-trillion-in-consumer-spending-by-2030-says-bcg-report/)

- Effectiveness (Meta-sourced): 71% buy within a couple of days of creator content (Meta-RAI 2026). Nearly half of festive shoppers follow creators for guidance, and more than 30% rely on creator reviews and buying guides (Meta festive 2025) — [APN News](https://www.apnnews.com/ai-creators-messaging-ignite-indias-omnichannel-shopping-boom-meta-retailers-association-of-india-whitepaper/); [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/technology/news/story/meta-study-reveals-how-ai-reels-and-creators-are-reshaping-festive-shopping-in-india-495472-2025-09-24)

### Inferences
- A tiered model of many fashion micro-creators (₹25-60K each) local to store catchments, plus a few macro anchors, is cost-efficient. It also fits the evidence that micro-influencers sway Gen Z and that creators are concentrated in Tier 2/3.

## 6. Tensions and gaps a brand could own (including Westside perception)

### Takeaway
The evidence supports several tensions: social inspiration vs in-store execution; a value squeeze that leaves mid-premium growing only with the category; Empathy-led CX expectations vs store service; and Zara as aspirational but pricey. There is no published evidence that Gen Z sees Westside as "my mom's store". It should be framed as a hypothesis to test with primary research or social listening, not as a finding.

### Cited Findings
- **Inspiration and execution:** Meta's own framing is "AI for inspiration, creators for credibility, and Reels for discovery". Purchase still happens mostly offline (fashion e-commerce penetration about 19%) — [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/special-coverage/metas-arun-srinivas-on-how-gen-z-short-form-video-micro-dramas-and-ai-are-reshaping-indias-media-landscape-82131.htm); [Meesho IPO industry report (Redseer)](https://investor.meesho.com/investor-web/_next/docs/ipo/industry-report-e-com.pdf)

"How to style" is a top Google search framing for 2025 (UK or global list) — [ELLE India](https://elle.in/fashion/2025s-most-googled-style-trends-and-how-to-wear-them-10900838)
- **Service matters:** Empathy is weighted 22%, the largest pillar in apparel CX, and a shopper says they would not buy if not respected — [KPMG](https://kpmg.com/in/en/insights/2025/03/india-cx-report-2025/retail/apparel.html). ### Inferences
Candidate customer truths and tensions for the pitch. All are inferences from the findings above, and those marked "hypothesis" lack direct evidence.
1. **"I see it on Reels but don't know how to wear it."** Discovery is on social (77% / 96% Meta, per Meta-RAI), guidance-seeking is high (about half follow creators for guidance), and fusion dressing (kurti with jeans, belted kurta) is inherently a styling problem. Westside could own "the store where Reels become outfits" with in-store styling, which social shows and the store fulfils.
2. **"Stores sell clothes; nobody helps me put them together."** KPMG puts Empathy first, and Enalytix ties two-minute staff engagement to dwell time. No India survey measures demand for styling help, so demand is a hypothesis while the CX levers are evidenced. Ownable idea: "Styled by Westside" associates, trial-room styling and WhatsApp-booked styling appointments, which fits Meta's WhatsApp recommendation.
3. **"Zudio is cheap, Zara is classy but costly; what's in between?"** The value squeeze and Zara's premium perception leave a mid-premium "considered style at fair value" space. Value for money is KPMG's top apparel attribute, so Westside should frame value as cost per wear or complete outfits, not discounts.
4. **"Westside is my mom's store" (hypothesis, unsupported).** No evidence was found. Westside's family positioning and women-led family shopping (2023 data) suggest an "intergenerational" angle (mother and daughter styling, the family festive wardrobe) could turn the perceived weakness into a strength. Test with social listening and Gen Z primary research before using it in the pitch.
5. **"I want it now."** Quick commerce (M-Now in 10+ cities) raises immediacy expectations, which Westside could answer with "reserve on Instagram or WhatsApp, try tonight" for evening peaks (5-8 PM is 46% of daily footfall).
6. **"Big occasions, many outfits, one family."** Wedding (46 lakh weddings in 2025, about 10% of spend on apparel) and festive (about ₹59,400 cr garments and textiles in 2025) moments are where women plan whole-family wardrobes. Westside's family format suits these occasions, with experiential "wedding wardrobe edit" events in malls on weekends, when footfall is about 40% higher.

### Gaps
- No primary or secondary research was found on Westside brand perception among Gen Z or millennials, or on "my mom's store" sentiment. This is the biggest evidence gap for the pitch. Recommend social listening (Instagram and Reddit r/india and r/IndianFashionAddicts comments) and a quick Gen Z survey.
- No India data on trial-room wait times, staff-to-shopper ratios, or satisfaction with in-store styling at Indian fashion chains.
- No data on Westside's social media following or engagement vs Zara, H&M or Zudio India.
- No India data on how many shoppers arrive in store with a screenshot or Reel, a classic "show-the-store-a-Reel" metric. This could be measured as a pitch KPI.
