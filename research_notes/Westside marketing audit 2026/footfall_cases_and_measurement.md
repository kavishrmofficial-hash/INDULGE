## 1. Case studies: social, creator, digital or experiential work that demonstrably moved store visits or sales

### Takeaway
Published, numbers-backed evidence that marketing raised *store visits* comes mainly from platform measurement (Meta, Google, Snap, Pinterest) and measurement vendors. Fashion brands' own disclosures are rare. Even well-known experiential cases (Glossier, Gentle Monster, Jacquemus, H&M x Sabyasachi) publish buzz, sell-out or conversion anecdotes, not controlled footfall lift. The strongest India-relevant proof points are Tata-group siblings (Tanishq, Taneira) using Google and Meta store-sales measurement, plus Meta–RAI aggregate claims. All of them are platform-claimed.

### Inferences
- The honest pitch line is not "creators fill stores". It is that **digital and social demonstrably drive in-store sales, which is where most of the effect lands**. Lewis & Reiley put 84% offline, and Tanishq found 26% of store sales had a Google touchpoint. Brands rarely prove it because they do not measure at store level. Mask360 can position the pilot as the measurement Westside does not yet have.
- The two Tata-group precedents (Tanishq with Google store sales and Meta offline CAPI; Taneira with Meta omnichannel) are the most persuasive for a Trent audience. They show that sister companies already accept platform-measured offline attribution. They are platform-claimed, so pair them with an independent footfall read.
- Experiential icons (Glossier, Gentle Monster, Jacquemus) work as *creative* references only. None published controlled footfall lift, and Glossier's own staff say its stores are not about transactions. Do not present them as proof of measurable uplift.
- Drops and collaborations (H&M x Sabyasachi) show demand spikes but also the main operational risk: stock-outs and backlash. A "one-drop" pilot needs a stock depth plan; see section 5.
- Mall-level data (Nexus +5% portfolio footfall, Dubai 10% across a festival) suggests a realistic single-activation footfall lift is **single digits to low double digits** over a multi-week window, with very large peaks on one day (IKEA, LuLu openings are launch extremes). That is an inference from aggregates, not a measured activation benchmark.

### Gaps
- No verified, numbers-backed footfall-lift case was found for Uniqlo (store launches or UT events), Zara openings, Primark, Nike or adidas store events, Target style programmes, Sephora or Nykaa in-store events, or Lenskart. The search budget ran out before these could be queried individually. Do not cite them with numbers until verified.
- No Effie India 2024/2025 fashion entry with a footfall metric surfaced. The [Effie India 2024 results](https://theadvertisingclub.net/award/effie-india-2024-results/) and [Effie case library](https://effie.org/cases/page/6/?partner=india) should be checked by hand.

- No Placer.ai-style footfall panel analysis exists for India; Placer.ai is US-focused.

## 2. Measurement methods and a credible one-store / one-drop pilot design

### Takeaway
A single-store pilot can be credible only if it uses (1) hardware or CCTV people counters as the primary outcome, (2) a matched-control or synthetic-control comparison drawn from Westside's own store network, (3) at least 10–12 weeks of daily pre-period baseline (ideally a year, to model festive seasonality), and (4) a pre-agreed minimum detectable effect. At realistic noise levels, **one store can reliably detect only lifts of about 8–15%+** over a 4–6 week window. Smaller effects need three or more treated stores or a longer window. Platform tools (Meta Conversion Lift with offline CAPI, Google store sales and visits) add a person-level, ID-based read, using WestStyle Club phone numbers as the join key.

**Experimental designs: store tests, geo tests, synthetic control**
- **Typical number of test stores:** retailers usually test in **5–25 stores** because testing is costly. — [Marketing Science 2000, UCLA Anderson PDF](https://www.anderson.ucla.edu/documents/areas/fac/dotm/bio/pdf_KR04.pdf)
- **MarketDial guidance:**
  - Using **more than 5 control stores per test store reduced accuracy**.
  - **1:3 matching cut noise by about 10%** compared with 1:1.
  - A 50/50 split maximises power for a fixed sample.
  - Most in-store tests need **4–8 weeks minimum** to detect moderate effects. — [MarketDial, multiple control matches](https://marketdial.com/resources/implications-of-multiple-control-matches-on-a-b-testing-accuracy-and-frequency/); [MarketDial, control vs test](https://marketdial.com/education-center/control-vs-test-groups-explained-the-foundation-of-every-good-experiment/); [MarketDial, in-store vs digital testing](https://marketdial.com/education-center/in-store-testing-vs-digital-testing-key-differences-every-retailer-should-know/)
- **Bain:** randomisation alone is insufficient with small numbers of units, which is typical of store and market tests (under 100). Sample size depends on the confidence level (90% vs 95%) and the target effect (10% vs 20% uplift), which needs a proper power analysis. — [Bain](https://www.bain.com/insights/successful-a-b-tests-in-retail-hinge-on-these-design-considerations/)
- **Meta GeoLift (open source, R):** builds the counterfactual with synthetic control and runs **power simulations before launch** to choose treatment markets. A larger treatment set and a longer test both raise power and lower the MDE. — [GeoLift walkthrough (GitHub)](https://github.com/facebookincubator/GeoLift/blob/main/vignettes/GeoLift_Walkthrough.md); [Bell Statistics](https://www.bellstatistics.com/glossary/geolift)
- **Baseline length:** power analysis inputs are a historical baseline "**ideally 10 to 12 weeks of daily data**", pre-period variance across candidate units, a target MDE that is commercially meaningful, and the confidence and power levels. Before launch, check **L2 imbalance**, run **placebo-in-time** tests and run **leave-one-out** donor checks. — [Popjam geo-lift guide](https://popjam.io/blog/geo-lift-test); [Medium, synthetic DiD power analysis](https://medium.com/@jeffrey.m.allard/planning-a-geo-lift-experiment-power-analysis-using-synthetic-difference-in-difference-2e824c06eca6)
- **Single unit vs pooled units:** in one public geo-experiment build, **placebo DiD error was 5.1% for a single state but 1.8% when pooling 6 states**. A real **2% lift returned p = 0.76** because the test was underpowered, which is not evidence of "no effect". — [GitHub: FCWVivian/geo-lift-incrementality](https://github.com/FCWVivian/geo-lift-incrementality)
- **Academic designs:** experiments designed around synthetic controls — [Doudchenko et al., Wharton](https://mackinstitute.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2020/03/Wernerfelt-Nils-Doudchenko-Nick-Gilinson-David-and-Taylor-Sean_Designing-Experiments-with-Synthetic-Controls.pdf). Supergeo matching for geo experiments — [arXiv 2301.12044](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.12044); [arXiv 2506.20499](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.20499)
- **Underpowered retail ad tests are the norm:** the Lewis & Reiley field experiment needed more than 3 million users to detect a 3.6% sales lift. — [INFORMS](https://www.informs.org/News-Room/INFORMS-Releases/News-Releases/Over-80-of-online-ad-effect-is-on-offline-sales)

**Platform and ID-based attribution**
- **Meta:** Conversion Lift supports offline conversion sources. Meta also has a separate **offline sales lift** product built on matched transaction data. — [Triple Whale](https://www.triplewhale.com/blog/meta-conversion-lift-test); [Haus, "Is Meta incremental?"](https://www.haus.io/blog/is-meta-incremental)
- **Meta in India:** Meta promotes **offline CAPI** (uploading in-store sales), **Omnichannel Optimization** and **Click-to-WhatsApp** as the bridges to offline sales; see the Tanishq, Taneira and Meta–RAI findings in section 1. — [Meta Newsroom, Feb 2026](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/02/ai-fuels-indias-omnichannel-shopping-surge-meta-retailers-association-of-india/)
- **Google store visits and store sales:** these require linked Google Ads and Google Business Profile accounts, with **at least 90% of linked locations verified**. Google's India retail page promotes store-visit goals and Performance Max for store goals (as Tanishq used). — [Odolena Kostova](https://www.odolena.com/index.php/2019/05/27/how-to-track-in-store-visits-in-google-ads/); [Google for Retail India](https://business.google.com/in/merchant-center/goals/instore-sales/); [Think with Google APAC](https://business.google.com/in/think/consumer-insights/omnichannel-retail-strategy-indian-shoppers/)
- **Snap to Store** and **Pinterest/Oracle/IRI/Nielsen** use exposed-vs-control designs based on location panels or loyalty and POS data (section 1). Pinterest's IRI coverage was limited to categories with loyalty-card data (food, health, beauty), which shows that **loyalty-ID data is what makes offline lift measurable**. — [Marketing Land](https://marketingland.com/pinterest-taps-market-research-firm-iri-to-measure-offline-sales-lift-274601)
- **Location panels:** Placer.ai-type data is a **modelled estimate from a device panel** of tens of millions of US devices. Lift claims connect exposure to movement statistically, not deterministically. — [Placer.ai](https://www.placer.ai/anchor/reports/retail-trends-to-watch-2026); [AI Digital](https://www.aidigital.com/blog/footfall-attribution)
- **Westside's loyalty join key:** WestStyle Club has **19M+ members** (Q2 FY26). In FY23, members contributed **over 84% of Westside sales**. — [Trent Q2 FY26 investor presentation](https://docs.trent-tata.com/Investor_Presentation_Q2_FY26.pdf); [Motilal Oswal note](https://images.assettype.com/bloombergquint/2024-06/a535fe3a-922b-4a48-a428-1aa5a62e0d32/Motilal_Oswal_Trent_Annual_Report_Update.pdf)

**Privacy: DPDP Act 2023 and DPDP Rules 2025**
- **Timeline:** the Rules were notified in **November 2025**. Sources differ on 13 vs 14 November. They phase in over about **18 months**, with **full compliance by 13 May 2027**. — [Seclore](https://www.seclore.com/fundamentals/dpdp-rules-2025-compliance-guide/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_Personal_Data_Protection_Rules,_2025); [EY India](https://www.ey.com/en_in/insights/cybersecurity/decoding-the-digital-personal-data-protection-act-2023)
- **What counts as personal data:** location data can make data personal. Device and advertising identifiers linked to an individual are personal data and need a lawful basis, so adtech falls under the Act. — [AM Legals, DPDPA for AdTech](https://amlegalsdpdpa.com/dpdpa-impact-decoded/dpdpa-for-advertising-adtech); [Legalkart](https://www.legalkart.com/en/legal-blog/a-comprehensive-guide-to-the-dpdp-act-2023-and-dpdp-rules-2025)
- **Consent (Section 6):** must be free, specific, informed, unconditional and unambiguous, given by a clear affirmative act, and limited to the data needed for the stated purpose. Withdrawing must be as easy as giving it. **Pre-ticked boxes and dark patterns are invalid.** — [dcomply DPDP FAQ](https://dpdpa.dcomply.in/faq/); [Digital Hangover, DPDP for marketers](https://digitalhangover.in/blog/dpdp-act-for-marketers/)
- **Children:** no tracking, monitoring, profiling or behavioural targeting of children, and no targeted ads to them. — [AM Legals](https://amlegalsdpdpa.com/dpdpa-impact-decoded/dpdpa-for-advertising-adtech); [dcomply](https://dpdpa.dcomply.in/faq/)
- **Reach:** the Act applies extraterritorially when goods or services are offered to people in India. — [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/?t=law&c=IN); [Cyril Amarchand FAQ PDF](https://www.cyrilshroff.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/FAQs-DPDPA.pdf)

### Inferences

**Recommended pilot design for one store and one drop. This is Mask360's proposal, synthesised from the sources above.**
1. **Primary KPI:** daily **entries per store**, cleaned of staff and repeat entries, from a 3D counter or CCTV analytics. Secondary KPIs: transactions, conversion (bills ÷ entries), average bill value (ABV), units per bill, new WestStyle Club enrolments, and member reactivations (lapsed members who return).
2. **Counter validation:** if the treated and control stores lack counters, install them at least 8–12 weeks before launch. Run a 1–2 day manual-count calibration and report the accuracy figure. Mixing counter types between the test store and control stores biases the comparison.
3. **Comparators:** choose **3–5 control Westside stores** matched on city tier, format (mall vs high street), size, pre-period footfall level, trend and seasonality. Better still, build a **synthetic control** from the wider store network (about 300 stores) with GeoLift-style code. Check fit on the pre-period (placebo-in-time, leave-one-out) before go-live. MarketDial's evidence argues against using more than 5 controls per test store.
4. **Baseline:** at least **12 weeks of daily data** before launch, plus the **same weeks last year** for every store to model festive and EOSS (end-of-season sale) seasonality. Because test and control stores share the festival calendar, a difference-in-differences or synthetic-control estimate nets out most festive effects, provided the controls are in the same region and festival mix (Durga Puja in the East, Onam in Kerala, Navratri in Gujarat and Maharashtra, and so on).
5. **Pre-registered MDE.** Illustrative calculation: 80% power, two-sided α = 0.05, weekly residual noise σ of the store-minus-control gap.
   - Formula: MDE ≈ 2.8 × σ × √(1/post-weeks + 1/pre-weeks) ÷ √(number of treated stores)
   - Results:

   | Weekly gap noise σ | Pre-weeks | Post-weeks | 1 treated store | 3 treated stores |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | 5% | 12 | 4 | **~8%** | ~5% |
   | 8% | 12 | 4 | **~13%** | ~7.5% |
   | 8% | 26 | 6 | **~10%** | ~6% |
   | 12% | 12 | 6 | **~17%** | ~10% |
   | 8% | 52 | 8 | ~8.5% | ~5% |

   The σ values are assumptions; Westside's own counter data should replace them. The GitHub benchmark (5.1% single unit vs 1.8% for 6 pooled units) suggests this scaling is about right. **Implication:** a single store should be pitched as able to detect roughly a **10%+ footfall lift** over 4–6 weeks. A smaller effect from a "one drop" may be real and still read as "not significant", so state that limit up front and pre-agree what happens in that case.
6. **Contamination control:** geo-fence paid social and creator boosts to the test store's catchment (for example a 5–8 km radius) and exclude control-store catchments. Ask creators to name the specific store. Use a store-specific landing page, WhatsApp click-to-chat number, QR code and offer code for the test store only.
7. **ID-based second read (person-level):**
   - Upload hashed WestStyle Club phone numbers and POS transactions via **Meta offline CAPI**, and run **Meta Conversion Lift** with a holdout among people in the catchment.
   - Run **Google store sales or visits** if the account meets the thresholds.
   - Track redemptions of **store-specific codes** and **new-member sign-ups at the till**.
   - This gives an independent check on the counter-based estimate, as Yves Rocher did by validating geo-lift against Conversion Lift.
8. **Drop-specific reads:** sell-through of the drop SKUs at the test store against the same SKUs (or a matched pseudo-drop) in control stores. Also compare halo spend: non-drop items in baskets that contain the drop.
9. **Privacy:** prefer aggregate counters, which count heads and store no identities. CCTV analytics that process faces, and any Wi-Fi or beacon MAC tracking, likely touch personal data under DPDP and need notice and consent. Loyalty-based matching should rely on WestStyle Club consent terms, which should be checked for a marketing-measurement purpose. Exclude minors from targeting. Full Rules compliance is due by May 2027, so the pilot runs during the phase-in.
10. **Report** the point estimate *and* the 90% or 95% interval, the pre-period fit, and placebo results, so the client cannot dismiss a positive result as luck.

### Gaps
- Westside's actual daily footfall per store, its variance, and whether people counters are already installed are unknown. These drive the real MDE.
- No source documented Meta Conversion Lift or Google store-visit **eligibility thresholds in India**, such as minimum conversions or audience size. Confirm with platform reps.
- No India-specific location-panel provider (a Placer.ai equivalent) was identified, and none with a DPDP-compliant consent basis.
- Lewis & Rao, "The Unfavorable Economics of Measuring the Returns to Advertising" (QJE 2015), is the standard reference on why ad-effect tests are underpowered. It could not be verified in this session; the writer should check it before citing.
- Exact Indian festive dates for the pilot window (Navratri, Dussehra and Diwali in Oct–Nov 2026) were not verified here.

## 4. Commercial models for performance-linked agency fees

### Takeaway
Pure pay-for-performance is rare and, among large advertisers, declining. The ANA reports performance-incentive use fell from **61% (2013) to 15% (2025)**, and fixed and labour-based fees dominate. Indian agency practice converges on **hybrid models: a modest base plus a performance kicker**. A footfall-linked pilot is therefore differentiating, but it needs a tightly defined, pre-agreed, independently measured outcome and a cap on payouts.

### Cited Findings
- **ANA, Forward, Oct 2026:** performance incentives fell from **61% in 2013 to 15% in 2025**, the lowest since the ANA began measuring in 1991. This is a secondary summary of ANA data. — [ANA Forward / Decideware](https://www.ana.net/magazines/show/id/forward-2026-10-decideware-article-2)
- **Earlier ANA editions:** labour-based fees remained the dominant form of agency compensation. An older edition reported that nearly half of marketers used performance incentives with at least one agency, and that use was higher among larger advertisers. — [ANA, labour-based fees](https://www.ana.net/content/show/id/76981); [ANA, compensation models study](https://www.ana.net/content/show/id/440); [ANA Trends in Agency Compensation, 18th ed., 2022](https://www.ana.net/miccontent/show/id/rr-2022-11-ana-trends-in-agency-compensation)
- **4A's/ANA, April 2024:** **fixed fee** was the most-used model among agencies polled, and most advertisers and agencies were "only starting to consider" approaches beyond traditional arrangements. — [4A's/ANA, Decoding Compensation Models (PDF)](https://www.aaaa.org/wp-content/uploads/4As-ANA-Decoding-Compensation-Models-and-Implementing-the-Right-Model.pdf?access_pid=118450); [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/395188/4as-ana-issue-report-on-agency-compensation-mode.html); [Adweek](https://www.adweek.com/agencies/ana-and-4as-explain-how-brands-and-agencies-can-switch-compensation-models/)
- **Indian agency pricing guides (2025–26; agency self-published, so treat as rough benchmarks).** The search extracts did not tie each figure to a single page, so all are cited together:
  - "Hybrid — modest base plus a percentage or performance kicker — aligns best" for most growing brands.
  - Pure performance deals are rare because they need enough historical data to set a fair target.
  - Fees are commonly **10–20% of media spend**, a fixed retainer, or a hybrid.
  - Example structures: **₹60,000 base + ₹20,000–50,000 bonus** if an agreed CAC is hit; **₹30,000–75,000 base + bonus per qualified lead or ROAS slab**; **₹20–40K retainer + 5–10% bonus**.
  - In PR, a common model is a smaller base plus a bonus for specified high-value coverage.
  - Risks: pure performance pay creates perverse incentives (agencies take only sure bets, or push spend that inflates commission), and "leads" must be defined as *qualified* leads the sales team accepts.
  - — [Upgrowth](https://upgrowth.in/performance-marketing-retainer-pricing-india-2026/); [The Shizz](https://theshizz.in/blog/agency-pricing-models-india); [CreatorCity](https://creatorcity.in/blog/performance-marketing-agency-cost-india/); [ITD Growth Labs](https://itdgrowthlabs.com/services/performance-marketing-packages.php); [Performance Marketing Guru](https://performancemarketingguru.com/pricing/); [Social101](https://social101.in/performance-marketing-agency-cost-india/); [Blue Buzz, PR](https://bluebuzz.in/pr-cost-in-india-2025-retainers-agency-rates/); [Madchatter, PR](https://madchatter.in/how-much-does-a-pr-agency-cost-in-india-honest-pr-services-pricing/)
- **Decideware in ANA Forward (2026):** AI "hasn't changed the agency compensation model — it just exposed the problem", meaning time-based fees are misaligned with value. — [ANA Forward](https://www.ana.net/magazines/show/id/forward-2026-10-decideware-article-2)

### Inferences

**Suggested pilot commercial structures for Mask360. These are design proposals, not sourced practice.**
- **Option A, "you lose nothing" (risk-reversal):**
  - Westside pays only pass-through costs (media at cost, creator fees, production) or nothing at all.
  - Mask360's fee is **100% contingent** on a pre-registered result: the lower bound of the 90% confidence interval of footfall lift must exceed 0, or the point estimate must clear the pre-agreed MDE, for example 10%.
  - The fee is payable as a **fixed success fee**, or **₹X per incremental visitor** (estimated lift × baseline footfall × weeks), **capped**.
  - This matches the "if numbers don't move, you lose nothing" framing. It is credible only if Mask360 can absorb the cost of a null result, which is likely given that a single store is underpowered (section 2).
- **Option B, hybrid (closest to Indian market norms):**
  - A modest base covers strategy, measurement set-up and creative.
  - A tiered bonus applies at, for example, +5%, +10% and +15% verified footfall lift, or is tied to incremental store revenue (footfall × conversion × ABV), with a cap.
  - This mirrors the "base + kicker" pattern in the Indian guides.
- **Option C, scale-up rights:** the pilot fee is waived or contingent in exchange for a pre-agreed **roll-out contract** to N more stores if the lift threshold is met. This is common reasoning, not a sourced practice.
- **Contract hygiene, drawn from the risks the guides flag:**
  - Define the outcome precisely: counter entries, cleaned of staff, compared against the agreed control stores.
  - Name the measurement owner. A neutral third party or the client's own counters is preferable to the agency's own data.
  - Fix the analysis method and the baseline window **before** launch.
  - List exclusion events (store closures, mall-level disruptions, stock-outs not caused by the agency, competing Westside promotions).
  - Cap the bonus, and agree what happens if the result is inconclusive rather than negative.
- **Pitch framing:** the decline in performance incentives (ANA: 15%) means many CMOs have not seen a well-measured outcome deal. Presenting the measurement design (section 2) as the foundation of the fee makes it credible.

### Gaps
- No India-specific survey of advertiser–agency compensation (an Indian equivalent of the ANA or WFA studies) was found.
- No documented Indian case of an agency fee tied to *store footfall* specifically was found. Outcome deals found are tied to CAC, leads or ROAS.
- The full ANA 2025 Trends in Agency Compensation report could not be opened to check definitions behind "15%".
- The WFA and other global agency-compensation benchmarks were not searched before the budget ran out.

## 5. Risks and how to de-risk a one-store / one-drop pilot

### Takeaway
The main ways such a pilot fails are statistical (one store is noisy, so real effects read as null), operational (stock-outs, untrained staff, an unprepared store), and comparator- or contamination-related (bad control stores, a festive or mall event coinciding, paid media spilling into control catchments). Each has a known mitigation drawn from retail test-and-learn practice.

### Cited Findings
- **Underpowering:** a real 2% lift showed p = 0.76, so it was underpowered rather than absent. A single unit's placebo error was about 3x a pooled set of six units (5.1% vs 1.8%). — [GitHub: FCWVivian/geo-lift-incrementality](https://github.com/FCWVivian/geo-lift-incrementality)
- **Small-sample comparators:** randomisation is insufficient below about 100 units, so matched or synthetic controls are needed. — [Bain](https://www.bain.com/insights/successful-a-b-tests-in-retail-hinge-on-these-design-considerations/). Between 1 and 5 control stores per test store is optimal, with 1:3 about 10% less noisy than 1:1. — [MarketDial](https://marketdial.com/resources/implications-of-multiple-control-matches-on-a-b-testing-accuracy-and-frequency/)
- **Duration:** in-store tests need 4–8 weeks minimum. — [MarketDial](https://marketdial.com/education-center/in-store-testing-vs-digital-testing-key-differences-every-retailer-should-know/). The baseline should be 10–12+ weeks of daily data, with placebo-in-time and leave-one-out checks. — [Popjam](https://popjam.io/blog/geo-lift-test)
- **Stock and demand spikes:** the H&M x Sabyasachi collection sold out within minutes and the site crashed, causing backlash over stock availability. The designer said demand "cannot be anticipated". — [The Quint](https://www.thequint.com/neon/social-buzz/sabyasachi-hm-collection-twitter-memes-myntra); [Apparel Resources](https://apparelresources.com/business-news/retail/indian-designer-sabyasachi-apologises-following-backlash-collaboration-hm/)
- **Capacity and crowd control:** IKEA Hyderabad (capacity 9,000 against 40,000 visitors on day one) faced long queues and police traffic management. — [The News Minute](https://www.thenewsminute.com/article/ikea-store-hyderabad-attracts-40000-customers-first-day-86312); [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/amp/story/world%2Fasia%2Findia%2Fstampede-like-situation-at-ikea-store-on-first-day-1.2264183)
- **Staff as a channel:** a US apparel ambassador programme that turned store associates into creators reported a 168% ROI **[vendor-claimed]**. — [Later](https://later.com/case-studies/fashion-retailer/)
- **Measurement data quality:** most Indian retailers cannot reliably compute conversion because footfall measurement is weak. — [PPMS](https://ppms.in/blog/what-is-conversion-rate-in-retail/). Retailers also struggle to count visitors accurately. — [IndiaRetailing / Tango Eye](https://www.indiaretailing.com/?p=41335)
- **Pop-up and event effects are short-lived** and do not follow normal seasonality. — [Placer.ai](https://www.placer.ai/anchor/reports/retail-trends-to-watch-2026)
- **Platform-claimed numbers are not independent:** Meta–RAI uplift figures have no published methodology. — [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/amp/brand-makers/ai-reels-and-whatsapp-driving-indias-omnichannel-retail-growth-shows-meta-rai-report-90694.htm). Measured's footwear case found online-only metrics *understated* Meta because the payoff showed up in stores. — [Measured](https://www.measured.com/blog/the-hidden-power-of-retail-what-2-omnichannel-brands-discovered-with-incrementality-measurement/)
- **Privacy:** location and device identifiers are personal data under DPDP; consent must be specific and affirmative; children must not be targeted. — [AM Legals](https://amlegalsdpdpa.com/dpdpa-impact-decoded/dpdpa-for-advertising-adtech); [dcomply](https://dpdpa.dcomply.in/faq/)

### Inferences

**De-risking checklist for the pitch, synthesised from the above.**
1. **Store selection:**
   - Choose a store with stable, *above-median* baseline footfall and conversion, and clean counter data.
   - It should sit in a catchment with strong creator and social reach, such as a metro mall flagship in Mumbai, Bengaluru or Delhi NCR.
   - It should have **no refurbishment, mall works or new competitor openings** planned in the window.
   - Avoid the chain's very best store; ceiling effects and capacity limits mute lift.
2. **Comparator selection:** pre-agree 3–5 matched control stores in the same region and festival calendar, plus a synthetic-control cross-check. Freeze the list before launch and run placebo-in-time tests on the pre-period.
3. **Seasonality and festive confounds:**
   - Avoid launching in the exact peak festive or EOSS week.
   - If the client wants a festive drop, rely on DiD against same-region controls *and* YoY comparisons.
   - Report results excluding the top 1–2 peak days as a robustness check.
   - The pitch date (15 Oct 2026) sits in the run-up to Navratri, Dussehra and Diwali. Either launch before the festive peak with a clean pre-period, or wait until after it.
4. **Stock readiness:**
   - Allocate drop depth for the test store from a forecast based on expected footfall lift plus a buffer.
   - Agree rapid replenishment.
   - Treat a stock-out as a pre-defined exclusion event or an extension trigger in the contract.
   - Keep the drop exclusive to the test store, or measure it in both arms; if the drop also goes to control stores, the test measures the marketing rather than the product.
5. **Staff readiness:**
   - Brief and incentivise test-store staff: clientelling via WhatsApp to WestStyle Club members, and capturing member IDs at the till.
   - Train staff on the drop story.
   - Consider staff-as-creators (Later precedent).
   - Watch for a Hawthorne effect, where extra attention to the test store lifts performance on its own; brief control-store staff normally.
6. **Contamination:** geo-fence paid and boosted creator content to the test catchment, use store-specific CTAs, codes and QR codes, and keep Westside's national campaigns identical across test and control stores during the window.
7. **Measurement integrity:** install or validate counters 8–12 weeks before launch, use the same hardware in test and control stores, and name an independent or client-owned data source. Pre-register the analysis plan, MDE and success thresholds.
8. **Expectation management:** state the single-store MDE up front (about 10%+). Offer a **3-store variant** that can detect about 5–7% for clients who want a decisive read, and define "inconclusive" separately from "failed".
9. **Privacy:** use aggregate counters, consented WestStyle Club data and hashed uploads for platform matching. Do not use passive device tracking without DPDP-compliant notice and consent, and exclude minors.

### Gaps
- No published post-mortem of a failed fashion store-traffic pilot (one with stock-out, staff or comparator failure) was found to cite.
- Whether Westside's existing store CCTV or counters are suitable for analytics, and whether the WestStyle Club terms permit measurement-purpose sharing with Meta or Google, could not be verified.
- No India-specific guidance was found on whether pure head-count footfall (no identifiers) falls outside the DPDP Act. This is likely, but it needs legal confirmation.
