## 1. Platform footprint: handles, audience sizes, growth, cadence, engagement

### Takeaway
Westside's main handle is @westsidestores. It runs on Instagram, YouTube, X, Pinterest and Threads, and in the past on Facebook. I could not get current (2026) follower counts for any of these. The best sourced indications are a stale snippet of about 1M Instagram followers and a brand claim of about 1.5M followers across all platforms combined, both from roughly 2024. I found no reported engagement rate, growth rate or posting cadence for Westside. A Westside-specific HypeAuditor/Socialblade pull, or a direct profile check, is needed before pitch numbers are quoted.

### Inferences

- Westside has about 300 stores and plans 60 to 100 openings a year, so each new store is a recurring local content moment. That is a natural hook for city-specific and store-specific content.
- The ~1M Instagram figure is old, and the 1.5M "all platforms" figure is roughly two years old. For 2026, assume Instagram is somewhere around or above 1M but **do not quote a number** until it is checked directly.

## 2. Content pillars, formats, tone of voice, store content, and what performs

### Takeaway
The sourced evidence shows four recurring Westside content pillars: seasonal and festive collection drops (e.g. the A/W'25 festive "burnt orange" Diwali edit), in-house label storytelling (Bombay Paisley, ETA, the W Edit), talent and culture platforms (YNG, Front Row, dance-offs), and long-form IP in the past (the Limitless podcast, 2024). The stated tone, per agency LinkedIn copy, "celebrates imperfections" and "real conversations", and uses a mascot-like character called "Hi Cutie" for topical days. I found **no evidence** of store-specific or city-specific content, store-staff stylist content, live shopping, or "shop in store / find your nearest store" social pushes in the window. Meanwhile, third-party YouTube haul videos of Westside stores show organic demand for store walk-through content that the brand does not appear to own.

## 3. Creator programmes: YNG, FRONT ROW, celebrities, hashtags, reach

### Takeaway
Westside's creator strategy in the window centres on two owned platforms. **YNG (Young New Game-changer)** launched in Sep 2025 with NIFT; by Q4 FY26 Trent described it as a "self-sustaining creator ecosystem, strengthening Westside's content sourcing and talent". **FRONT ROW** launched on 1 Oct 2026 as a fashion and culture platform with about 150 micro and macro "culture-shapers", 80 looks, three national dance-off challenges, and 30 "New Faces" for 2026/27. I could not verify the briefed figures of "~4,000 YNG creators / 6M views in Q4 FY26" in any indexed source. I found no named celebrity brand ambassador for Westside in the window.

## 7. Content IP opportunities and gaps vs best-in-class (store-driving content)

### Takeaway
Westside already owns raw material for IP: YNG (talent), FRONT ROW (culture and runway), 30 New Faces (models), the W Edit and in-house labels (Bombay Paisley, ETA), a 300-store estate growing by 60 to 100 a year, 9.7M WestStyleClub members (stale figure), and a past long-form podcast (Limitless). What is missing, based on all the evidence found, is a **recurring, store-anchored, local-language content layer**: store and city content, staff or stylist faces, occasion edits shot in stores, live shopping, and creator-to-store mechanics like those Uniqlo, Shoppers Stop and Snitch use.

### Cited Findings (evidence base for the opportunities)
- Store estate: 300 Westside stores at 31 Mar 2026, 60 opened in FY26 — [Trent Q4 FY26 press release](https://docs.trent-tata.com/Press_Release_Q4_FY26.pdf)

### Inferences (pitch-ready opportunity hypotheses; these are recommendations, not findings)
- **"New In, In Store" weekly series:** a store-floor walk-through of new arrivals, presented by YNG stylists or New Faces, naming the store and city, with an "available at Westside [city]" CTA. This takes back the haul format that third parties already run.
- **Stylist network IP:** turn store staff and YNG stylists into recurring on-camera "Westside Stylists", modelled on personal-shopper programmes such as Shoppers Stop's. Pair it with a book-a-styling-session-in-store CTA tied to WestStyleClub.
- **Occasion edits shot in stores:** wedding guest, office, college, festive and travel edits, each a recurring franchise filmed in a different city's store. This works as the store-anchored counterpart to the editorial W Edit.
- **FRONT ROW on tour, store edition:** stage the three national dance-offs, and New Faces casting calls, at or beside flagship stores, with creator-hosted "Front Row Store Days" and a store-exclusive drop. This applies the Uniqlo and H&M culture-plus-store model.
- **Store-opening playbook:** with 60 to 100 openings a year, give every launch a "People of [City]" local-creator film plus a launch-day creator meetup. This copies Uniqlo's mechanic at much larger scale.
- **Vernacular and regional content:** regional creator rosters and Hindi or regional-language captions or voiceovers for tier-2 store markets. This follows Uniqlo's regional creators and Myntra's small-town creators. I found no Westside evidence of vernacular content.
- **Revive long-form IP:** a Limitless-style podcast or video series with YNG and New Faces talent, cut into Reels.
- **Editorial art-direction upgrade:** use the 80-look seasonal line-ups for a Zara-like editorial grid, while a separate store and local layer carries the footfall CTAs.
- **Measurement gap:** propose store-visit metrics (Meta store-visit objectives, WestStyleClub code redemptions, store-specific links). The only social metric Trent discloses is qualitative ("self-sustaining creator ecosystem"), and the briefed 4,000/6M figures are unverified.
