## Summary (10 lines)

1. **Cart pilot:** 3 Westside-designed carts (Mumbai, Bengaluru, Delhi NCR), Thursday to Sunday for 6 weeks: 72 cart-days and about 21,600 cups at 300 a day. Estimated cost is **Rs 35-85 lakh before space fees, Rs 40 lakh to 1.4 crore with them**. All inputs are unverified, and space fees are the weakest.
2. **Operator model:** a specialty-coffee operator runs the cart under its own FSSAI licence and supplies baristas, machine and beans. Westside owns the design, menu names and look-code sleeves. Free coffee is still a "food business" (FSS Act, as recalled: "whether for profit or not") (unverified).
3. **FSSAI:** a temporary stall with turnover up to Rs 12 lakh a year needs only registration, at Rs 100 a year (2011 regulations, as recalled; unverified). A cart selling 300 cups at Rs 200 crosses Rs 12 lakh in **20 days**, so plan on the operator's State licence instead. Check for amendments.
4. **Public streets are the hard part.** The Street Vendors Act 2014 gives vending rights to certified vendors in vending zones, not to brand activations (unverified). Use private land instead: inside Westside stores, mall atriums, store forecourts, campuses and tech parks.
5. **Likely blocker:** a cart inside a Westside store in a mall still needs landlord consent (the lease's permitted use, fire rules, any food-court exclusivity). Check three leases before promising locations.
6. **Throughput caps the cart:** a 2-group machine with 2 baristas makes roughly 80-120 drinks an hour at peak (unverified rule of thumb). Plan for 200-400 cups a day. The machine needs about 3-5 kW of mains power, so it cannot run on batteries all day (unverified).
7. **No published results** (views, footfall, queues) for an Indian fashion or lifestyle brand coffee cart, café or pop-up could be checked this session. **Put no comparable result on a slide.**
8. **Story windows:** plan for about **470 windows** (311 stores × ~1.5 each, an assumption) plus 311 in-store "story zones". That is **Rs 1.25-4.1 crore per cycle (Rs 40,000 to 1.33 lakh per store)** and **9-11 weeks** from brief to all stores live with central kits (unverified inputs).
9. **Evidence:** academic studies link windows to store entry (Sen, Block & Chandran 2002; Oh & Petrie 2012; Lange, Rosengren & Blom 2016; citations unverified), but no effect sizes were retrieved. Prove it with Westside's own door counts: 20 test stores against 20 matched controls.
10. **"Rahul and Anjali":** first names alone are generic and low risk. As a pair they point to Dharma's *Kuch Kuch Hota Hai* (1998) and *K3G* (2001). Add film cues and the risks become copyright, passing off or false endorsement, and personality rights. **Use other names**, or get legal clearance or a licence.

**Consumables per cup (derived; every input unverified):**
- Specialty beans cost about Rs 900-1,800/kg wholesale (Rs 2,000-3,200/kg retail). An 18 g double shot gives about 55 shots per kg, so coffee costs Rs 16-33 per cup.
- Milk costs about Rs 66-72 per litre in metros. At 150-200 ml a drink, that is Rs 10-14. Oat milk is roughly 4-5 times as expensive.
- A double-wall cup and lid cost Rs 5-12. A printed sleeve costs Rs 3-12.
- Add sugar, napkins, stirrers, water and wastage.
- **Rs 40-80 per milk drink. Masala chai and batched cold brew cost less per cup.**

**Barista pay (unverified):** about Rs 15,000-30,000 a month salaried, and Rs 1,500-5,000 a day for event baristas. This is already inside the operator fee.

### A4. Licences and permissions (all unverified; confirm with the operator's compliance team and a local consultant)

**FSSAI (applies everywhere)**
- The FSS Act 2006, s.3(1)(n), defines "food business" to include any undertaking "whether for profit or not". **Free sampling therefore still needs FSSAI cover** (as recalled).
- The FSS (Licensing and Registration of Food Businesses) Regulations 2011 cover three tiers (as recalled):
  - **Registration (Form A, Rs 100 a year):** for a "petty food business operator", which includes a "temporary stall holder", "hawker" and "itinerant vendor", with turnover up to Rs 12 lakh a year.
  - **State licence:** turnover of Rs 12 lakh to Rs 20 crore, about Rs 2,000-5,000 a year depending on category.
  - **Central licence:** above Rs 20 crore, and for certain premises such as airports and railways.
  - Applications go through FoSCoS (foscos.fssai.gov.in).
- **Practical route:** the operator lists the cart under its existing State licence, or obtains a registration or licence for the cart as a separate unit. Whether a mobile cart needs its own registration per city depends on the State Food Safety office. Ask the operator.
- **Arithmetic check:** 300 cups × Rs 200 = Rs 60,000 a day. Rs 12 lakh ÷ Rs 60,000 = **20 trading days**. A selling cart running beyond a short pilot outgrows the petty-operator registration.
- **Hygiene and training (as recalled):** Schedule 4 of the regulations sets hygiene conditions (Part I covers petty and street vendors). Licensed businesses need a FoSTaC-trained food safety supervisor. Baristas need medical fitness certificates.
- **Check:** whether FSSAI amended the Rs 12 lakh threshold or the fee after 2022. I have no reliable recollection either way.

**Malls**
- Mall placement is a commercial agreement with the mall's marketing or "specialty leasing" team: space fee, power point, water, waste, fire NOC, insurance and working hours.
- A cart **inside a Westside store** saves the space fee. It still needs (a) landlord consent if the lease restricts use to apparel retail, (b) the mall's fire and electrical sign-off, and (c) confirmation that the mall has not granted coffee exclusivity to a food-court tenant.

**High streets (public footpath)**
- The Street Vendors (Protection of Livelihood and Regulation of Street Vending) Act 2014 gives vending rights through a certificate of vending from a Town Vending Committee, within designated vending zones (as recalled). A corporate activation does not fit that scheme.
- Use the **private forecourt of a stand-alone Westside store**, which is private land. Then a municipal health or trade permission for the premises and the FSSAI cover are the main needs.

**Colleges and office parks**
- On private campuses, permission comes from college administration or the fest committee, or from the park's facility manager. Often it is sold as sponsorship.
- FSSAI still applies. Campuses may already have exclusive food vendors.

**City-specific (all unverified, low confidence; confirm locally)**
- **Mumbai:**
  - BMC hawking rules and Bombay High Court orders restrict hawking near railway stations, schools, hospitals and places of worship (a 150 m buffer is recalled for stations).
  - Eating houses need a BMC health licence.
  - Temporary stalls at events are permitted through the ward office.
  - A BMC food-truck policy has been discussed. Its status is unknown.
- **Bengaluru:** civic administration was restructured in 2025 under the Greater Bengaluru Authority, which replaced BBMP. Confirm which city corporation now issues temporary trade or event permissions.
- **Delhi:**
  - Jurisdiction is split between MCD, NDMC (Connaught Place, Khan Market) and the Cantonment Board.
  - Delhi Police licensing of eating houses was reportedly withdrawn in 2025 (low confidence).
  - A Delhi food-truck policy was announced earlier (status unknown).
- **Recommendation for all three cities:** avoid public footpaths in the pilot.

**Tax:** restaurant service in India is generally taxed at 5% GST without input tax credit (unverified). The operator bills this if coffee is sold.

### A5. Cups per day (all unverified; the most important number to verify)
- **Capacity:**
  - One barista on a 2-group machine makes roughly 40-60 milk drinks an hour; two baristas make roughly 80-120 at peak (industry rule of thumb).
  - Over a 10-hour mall day with 3-4 peak hours, a realistic ceiling is **400-600 cups**.
  - Pre-batched cold brew (Nuon) and chai (Utsa) from dispensers raise the ceiling, because they need no espresso.
- **Demand:**
  - **Paid:** a busy paid specialty kiosk in a metro mall plausibly serves **150-400 cups a day**. This is the weakest figure in this section, with no source.
  - **Free:** free cups at a college fest or a launch can reach the capacity ceiling.
- **Rough anchor (low confidence):**
  - Tata Starbucks revenue was about Rs 1,218 crore in FY24 across about 420-460 stores (both recalled, unverified).
  - That gives about Rs 72,000-80,000 a store a day. At a Rs 300-350 average ticket, that is **roughly 205-265 transactions a store a day**.
  - Transactions are not cups, and revenue includes food and merchandise.
- **Planning figure: 300 cups per cart-day**, within a 200-400 range.

### A6. Locations and run lengths (judgement, not sourced)
- **Best, in this order:**
  1. Inside or at the mall entrance of a high-footfall Westside store (no space fee; walking distance to the looks on the sleeve).
  2. The private forecourt of a stand-alone Westside high-street store.
  3. A mall atrium next to Westside's mall entrance.
  4. College fests (the peak fest season is roughly Dec-Feb, unverified).
  5. Tech-park plazas at lunch hour (Bengaluru ORR and Whitefield, Gurugram Cyber City, Mumbai BKC and Powai), on weekdays only.
- **Run lengths:**
  - Mall or in-store: 2 weekends to 2 weeks.
  - College fests: 2-4 days.
  - Office parks: 1-3 days per park, rotating.
  - The pilot below uses 2-week blocks per location type.
- **Power:** a 2-group machine draws about 3-5 kW and uses roughly 10-20 kWh over a day (rough estimate). Every site needs a dedicated 16-32 A mains point. Batteries alone are impractical.

## B. Scenario-based (story) windows and displays

### B1. Windows and display zones per store (estimate, unverified)
- **No public count** of Westside windows or VM zones was available. The local notes have no Westside VM organisation detail.
- **Working assumption** for a 20,000-30,000 sq ft Indian fashion store:
  - A mall-facing store has about 1-4 façade windows (often 1-2 at a mall entrance).
  - A stand-alone high-street store has about 3-8 window bays.
  - Inside, there are about 10-25 focal points: the entry table, escalator or staircase heads, department focal walls and mannequin platforms.
- Planning figure: **~1.5 story windows per store (≈470 windows across 311 stores) plus 1 in-store story zone per store (311).**
- **Replace this with a store survey:** each store team sends photos and measurements in one form, which takes about 1-2 weeks.
- **Westside VM organisation:** nothing public was available. The usual Indian chain structure is a central VM team at head office, regional VM managers and a store VM executive (unverified as Westside's). Ask Westside in the pitch Q&A.

### B2. Change cycles and costs (all unverified, weak)
- **Change cycle:**
  - Indian fashion chains change major windows roughly 8-12 times a year, following the calendar: end-of-season sales in Jan and Jul, summer, monsoon, back-to-college, Independence Day, festive (Navratri, Durga Puja, Diwali), wedding season and year-end.
  - Fast-fashion internationals change windows every 2-4 weeks.
  - A story programme fits a **6-8 week** cycle, with 6-8 stories a year.
- **Print:** flex or vinyl about Rs 40-120 per sq ft; backlit fabric (SEG) about Rs 100-300 per sq ft.
- **Standard central window kit** (graphics, modular props, styling card, planogram): about **Rs 15,000-50,000 per window**.
- **Hero custom window:** about **Rs 2-5 lakh**.
- **Mannequins:** fibreglass, about Rs 5,000-25,000 each. Reuse store stock.
- **Styling:** uses Westside's own product, so the cost is shrinkage and handling only.
- **Installation:** done by store VM staff in-house (no incremental cost), or by a vendor at about Rs 3,000-8,000 per store.
- **Logistics:** about Rs 3,000-8,000 per store across 97 cities.

### B3. Research on windows and store entry (citations as recalled; effect sizes not retrieved)
- **Sen, Block & Chandran (2002)**, "Window displays and consumer shopping decisions", *Journal of Retailing and Consumer Services* 9(5). Survey evidence that the merchandise and fashion information in windows relates to store entry.
- **Oh & Petrie (2012)**, "How do storefront window displays influence entering decisions of clothing stores?", *JRCS* 19(1).
- **Lange, Rosengren & Blom (2016)**, "Store-window creativity's impact on shopper behavior", *Journal of Business Research* 69(3). Experimental evidence that creative windows raise attention and entry intentions or behaviour.
- **Cornelius, Natter & Faure (2010)**, "How storefront displays influence retail store image", *JRCS* 17(2).
- **Edwards & Shackley (1992)**, "Measuring the effectiveness of retail window display as an element of the marketing mix", *International Journal of Advertising* 11(3).
- **Mower, Kim & Childs (2012)**, "Exterior atmospherics and consumer behavior: influence of landscaping and window display", *Journal of Fashion Marketing and Management* 16(4).
- **India:** Mehta & Chugan (2013), visual merchandising and impulse buying, a mall study in Ahmedabad (journal and figures unverified).
- **No numeric effect sizes are stated here.** I could not open the papers, and I will not guess the numbers.
- **The pitch should say "academic studies link window displays to store entry"** and promise Westside-specific proof: the capture rate (entries ÷ passers-by) from door and passer-by counters at 20 test and 20 matched control stores, 2 weeks before and 4 weeks after.

### B5. Turning displays into social content (approach; unverified as Westside practice)
- **Store content champion:**
  - Name one per store.
  - Give a 3-shot list: a 9:16 walk-past reveal, a mannequin detail with the story caption, and "shop the look" at the rail with the look code.
  - Uploads go to a central folder. The central team cuts city edits.
- **Local creators:**
  - Westside's own YNG community is reported at **4,000 members, 6 million views, 50,000+ engagements and 12,000+ shares** (Trent Q4 FY26 deck, company-reported) [local: westside_campaigns_pr_events_stores.md].
  - Brief 30-50 YNG creators in 15-20 cities to "live the story" outside the window.
- **Store listings:** post each store's story photo on its Google Business Profile for "near me" discovery (general practice, unverified for Westside).
- **Compliance (as recalled):**
  - ASCI's influencer advertising guidelines (2021) require upfront disclosure labels for paid or material-connection posts.
  - The Department of Consumer Affairs issued "Endorsement Know-hows!" (Jan 2023).
  - Staff posting about their employer is a material connection, so they disclose.
  - Get model releases for anyone filmed, and mall permission to film in common areas.

### B6. The names Rahul and Anjali (legal view as recalled; not legal advice; unverified)
- **Association:**
  - In *Kuch Kuch Hota Hai* (1998, Dharma Productions, directed by Karan Johar), Shah Rukh Khan plays Rahul and Kajol plays Anjali.
  - The same pair of names returns in *Kabhi Khushi Kabhie Gham* (2001, Dharma).
  - "Rahul" is also a recurring Shah Rukh Khan screen name, so **the pairing, not either name alone, carries the association**.
- **Generic first names in an ad display:**
  - Common first names are weak as trademarks and cannot be monopolised for ordinary descriptive use.
  - A display using "Rahul" or "Anjali" alone, with no film cues, is low risk.
- **Trademark and passing off:**
  - Passing off needs goodwill, misrepresentation and damage (the classic trinity, followed by Indian courts).
  - Using "Rahul & Anjali" together with cues (a famous line, a song, stills, lookalike styling, the "Cool" pendant, the film's typeface, campus and friendship-band imagery) could suggest Dharma endorsement or take unfair advantage of its goodwill.
  - ASCI's Code also bars taking unfair advantage of another's goodwill (as recalled; chapter and clause unverified).
  - Run a check on the IP India public trade-mark search for "KUCH KUCH HOTA HAI" and "RAHUL ANJALI" before use.
- **Copyright:**
  - A film title alone is not protected by copyright in India (*Krishika Lulla v. Shyam Vithalrao Devkatta*, Supreme Court, 2015, as recalled).
  - Dialogue, music, lyrics and stills are protected.
- **Character evocation:** in *Star India v. Leo Burnett* (Bombay High Court, 2003, as recalled), the court refused to stop an ad that parodied TV-serial characters. It found that character-merchandising protection needs the characters to be recognised beyond the work itself, and the ad was not a copy. This helps, but it is not a safe harbour.
- **Personality rights:**
  - Indian courts have recently granted broad personality-rights orders to film figures.
  - Karan Johar reportedly obtained Bombay High Court relief in 2024 against a film title using his name, and a Delhi High Court personality-rights order in 2025 (both recalled, medium-low confidence).
  - Lookalikes of Shah Rukh Khan or Kajol would create the highest risk.
- **Recommendation:**
  - Use other names, or city-specific names that change per store, which suits the per-store-story idea anyway.
  - If the team wants a deliberate film nod, treat it as a licensed collaboration with Dharma, cleared by counsel, not as an unlicensed wink.
