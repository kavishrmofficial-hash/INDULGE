# Creativity and insight: what actually works in a 2–15 minute break (evidence-graded notes)

Grading key used throughout:
- **REPLICATED** = meta-analysis, or 2+ independent labs with experimental designs.
- **MIXED** = replicated in some labs, failed in others, or effect is narrower than the headline.
- **SINGLE-LAB** = one lab's study/series, no independent direct replication found.
- **FAILED REPLICATION** = a published direct/partial replication did not find the effect.
- **FRAUD-TAINTED** = author under misconduct finding; discount unless independently replicated.

Measurement note: nearly everything below is measured on lab tasks — the Alternate/Alternative Uses Task (AUT; list unusual uses for a brick, cup, etc.; scored for fluency = count, originality = rarity/rated creativity, flexibility = categories) or the (Compound) Remote Associates Test (RAT/CRA; find the word linking "cottage / swiss / cake" = cheese; a convergent/insight task). Transfer to real creative work is rarely tested; where it is, it is flagged.

Research-access note for the report writer: web search budget was exhausted mid-task and the egress proxy blocked every publisher/open-access domain tried (PMC, APA, Nature, Springer, Elsevier, PLOS, Frontiers, MDPI, Europe PMC, Wikipedia, Wayback). Findings marked **[verified]** were confirmed from search-result excerpts of the cited source this session. Findings marked **[recalled; not re-verified]** are specifics I am confident the cited paper contains but could not re-check; treat them as "very likely" rather than certain.

## 1. Incubation: does stepping away help, and does an "undemanding task / mind-wandering" break help more?

### Takeaway
Incubation (a break before returning to a creative problem) is a small, real, meta-analytically supported effect (d ≈ 0.29), largest when the break is filled with a low-demand task rather than rest or a hard task. The famous Baird et al. (2012) "+41% on AUT after an undemanding task" headline and its mind-wandering mechanism have NOT held up in multiple replication attempts; treat "mind wandering boosts creativity" as unsupported, while "take a break with a light task and come back" remains supported.

### Cited Findings
- Sio & Ormerod (2009), Psychological Bulletin 135(1), 94–120: meta-analysis of 117 studies found a positive effect of delayed incubation, overall mean d = 0.29 (low-medium). **[verified]** — [Sio & Ormerod 2009, Lancaster repository](https://research.lancaster-university.uk/en/publications/does-incubation-enhance-problem-solving-a-meta-analytic-review/); [PDF mirror](https://gwern.net/doc/psychology/writing/2009-sio.pdf)
- Same meta-analysis: "the benefits of an incubation period are greater when participants are occupied by an undemanding interpolated task than when they engage in a demanding interpolated task or no task at all"; low-demand tasks beat rest for linguistic insight problems. Moderators examined: problem type, solution-relevant vs misleading cues, length of preparation and incubation periods. **[verified]** — [Semantic Scholar entry](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Does-incubation-enhance-problem-solving-A-review.-Sio-Ormerod/76c6ce0abf6f67f2c089df372261203989b43903)
- Baird et al. (2012, Psychological Science) reported that mind wandering during a non-demanding incubation task improved later AUT performance (the "+41%" claim). **[verified that this is the claim; the 41% figure is recalled; not re-verified]** — summarised in [Intentionality of Self-Generated Thought (PMC 2023 review)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10414778/)
- "Numerous researchers have failed to observe a reliable association between mind wandering and post-incubation creativity." Steindorf et al. (2021, Psychology of Aesthetics, Creativity, and the Arts, "Mind wandering outside the box—About the role of off-task thoughts and their assessment during creative incubation") failed to replicate the mind-wandering/creativity relation, particularly when using thought probes rather than retrospective reports. **[verified]** — [PMC 2023 review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10414778/); [MDPI 2025 review of mind wandering during incubation](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3425/15/6/595); [van Rooij et al. 2024, J. Creative Behavior, "A Wandering Mind is Not Always a Creative Mind"](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/jocb.640)
- Gilhooly et al. (2013, "Don't wait to incubate: Immediate versus delayed incubation in divergent thinking"): AUT with incubation periods either delayed or immediate, filled with spatial or verbal tasks; "significant incubation effects that were stronger for immediate than for delayed incubation"; no difference between verbal and spatial filler; checks found no evidence of intermittent conscious work during incubation. **[verified]** — [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Don%E2%80%99t-wait-to-incubate:-Immediate-versus-delayed-in-Gilhooly-Georgiou/5a9d9d5c7702843869749dacbfb29467d2d66a13); [Gilhooly 2016 Frontiers theory paper](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2016.01076/full)
- A 2025 Scientific Reports paper reports that mind wandering during creative incubation predicted increases in performance on a *writing* task (closer to real creative work than AUT). **[verified title/claim only; design and N not retrieved]** — [Nature Sci Rep 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-09736-y)
- "Poetic Break: Incubation for Associative Creativity" (Creativity Research Journal, 2025) tests a poetry-reading break as incubation filler. **[verified existence only]** — [Tandfonline](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/10400419.2025.2466571)
- Cambridge chapter reviewing "so-called incubation effects" (Emergence of Insight) frames the literature as mixed and mechanism-contested. **[verified existence]** — [Cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/emergence-of-insight/past-and-future-of-research-on-socalled-incubation-effects/4ADD1527830639E05DFB4D4E4A5766E2)

### Inferences
- Grade: **Incubation = REPLICATED (small, d≈0.29).** **Low-demand filler > rest > high-demand filler = REPLICATED at meta-analytic level.** **Baird 2012 "mind wandering is the mechanism / +41%" = FAILED REPLICATION (multiple labs).**
- The effect size implies modest gains: on a task where a team member would produce 10 ideas, expect ~1–2 more or slightly better ones after a filled break, not a 40% jump.
- Gilhooly's "immediate incubation" result suggests a usable protocol: read the brief, do NOT start generating, do a light unrelated task, then generate — this is as good or better than generating-then-breaking.
- Protocol (implementable): **"Park it" break (8–12 min).** Screen 1: show the live creative problem/brief for 60–90 s; instruct "Read this. Do not start solving. Notice what the real question is." Screen 2: 5–8 min of a genuinely low-demand, non-verbal task that is clearly unrelated (e.g., sort coloured shapes, trace a slow line, simple tile-matching). Screen 3: return to the brief with a 3-minute timed idea sprint. Avoid fillers that require working memory or language (they compete with the problem); avoid "just sit and wait" (rest underperforms low-demand filler in the meta-analysis).

### Gaps
- Exact moderator effect sizes from Sio & Ormerod (d for rest vs low-demand vs high-demand; by problem type; by incubation length) could not be retrieved (PDF blocked).
- Full list of Baird replications with Ns (e.g., Smeekens & Kane 2016; Murray, Liang, Brosowsky & Seli 2021; Leszczynski et al. 2017) could not be retrieved; the PMC review above confirms "numerous" failures without my being able to enumerate them.
- Steindorf et al. 2021 sample size and whether they found *any* incubation benefit (as opposed to no mind-wandering link) not retrieved.

## 2. Walking: does it boost divergent thinking, does it transfer indoors, and does it persist after sitting?

### Takeaway
Walking reliably boosts divergent-thinking output while walking and shortly after (Oppezzo & Schwartz 2014, four experiments, N=176; independent support from Zhou et al. 2017, Kuo & Yeh 2016, Murali & Händel 2022; and a 2022 multilevel meta-analysis of physical activity and creative ideation). The benefit is for idea generation, not for convergent/insight problems, works on an indoor treadmill, and the newer work suggests the active ingredient is *unrestricted movement* rather than walking per se.

### Cited Findings
- Oppezzo & Schwartz (2014, JEP: Learning, Memory & Cognition, "Give Your Ideas Some Legs"): four experiments, 176 participants (mostly students). Walking on an indoor treadmill increased divergent-thinking creativity for 81% of participants vs sitting; when walking outside, 100% of participants produced more novel, high-quality analogies than when seated. Walking did not help convergent/single-answer problem solving (CRA). **[verified]** — [APA PDF](https://www.apa.org/pubs/journals/releases/xlm-a0036577.pdf); [ScienceDaily summary](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2014/04/140424101556.htm)
- Same paper: a residual effect — participants who walked and then sat still produced more creative ideas than those who only sat. Headline "~60% average increase in creative output." **[recalled; not re-verified this session]** — [APA PDF](https://www.apa.org/pubs/journals/releases/xlm-a0036577.pdf)
- Zhou, Zhang, Hommel & Zhang (2017) compared sitting, standing still and walking; walking improved divergent performance. **[verified]** — [Frontiers 2016 Kuo & Yeh paper citing it](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2016.01580/full)
- Kuo & Yeh (2016, Frontiers in Psychology, "Sensorimotor-Conceptual Integration in Free Walking Enhances Divergent Thinking for Young and Older Adults"): free walking enhanced AUT performance vs walking a prescribed rectangular path. **[verified]** — [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2016.01580/full)
- Murali & Händel (2022, Psychological Research 86:2144–2157, "Motor restrictions impair divergent thinking during walking and during sitting"): walking beat sitting for divergent thinking, but *restricting* movement (fixed path / fixed gaze) impaired divergent thinking in both walking and sitting conditions. **[verified]** — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00426-021-01636-w); [lay summary](https://www.learningandthebrain.com/blog/new-research-unrestricted-movement-promotes-some-kinds-of-creativity/)
- Rominger et al. (2022, Sports Medicine – Open), "Acute and Chronic Physical Activity Increases Creative Ideation Performance: A Systematic Review and Multilevel Meta-analysis": concludes both acute and chronic physical activity increase creative ideation. **[verified title/conclusion; effect sizes not retrieved]** — [Sports Med Open](https://sportsmedicine-open.springeropen.com/articles/10.1186/s40798-022-00444-9)
- Frith, Ryu, Kang & Loprinzi (2019, Europe's Journal of Psychology) systematic review: 13 studies, 92% report a beneficial exercise–creativity relationship, but "weak to modest support for acute, moderate-intensity exercise to benefit creativity"; heterogeneity too high for meta-analysis. **[verified]** — [EJOP](https://ejop.psychopen.eu/index.php/ejop/article/download/1773/1773.html?inline=1); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33680164/)
- Leung et al. (2012, Psychological Science) Study on free vs fixed-path walking also found free walking raised originality (part of the "embodied metaphors" series; see §6). **[verified via Kuo & Yeh citation]** — [Leung et al. 2012](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797611429801)

### Inferences
- Grade: **Walking → divergent thinking = REPLICATED** (original multi-experiment paper + ≥3 independent labs + 2022 meta-analysis). **Walking → convergent/insight = NOT supported (null or slight harm).** **Residual carry-over after sitting = SINGLE-LAB (Oppezzo Exp. 2 only).**
- Indoor treadmill facing a wall worked in the original; outdoors added a further boost on the analogy task, but outdoors-in-a-wheelchair did not match outdoor walking, so movement, not scenery, is the main lever.
- Murali & Händel's result reframes the design: let people wander freely (no fixed loop, no phone-reading while walking), and even seated people should be free to move/shift/gaze around.
- Protocol (implementable): **"Walk & dump" (8–12 min).** Screen 1 (30 s): show the generation prompt ("Give me 25 wrong, weird or wonderful ways to…"). Screen 2: "Walk for 6–8 minutes — any route, your pace, no destination, no screen. Say ideas aloud into voice memo as they come." Screen 3 (on return, 3 min, seated): timed typing dump of everything, then one more minute to add. Measure fluency and let people star 2 "most unusual" ideas. Do not use this protocol for convergent tasks (debugging a brief, choosing between options).
- Because effects were measured *during and immediately after* walking, place the generation sprint within ~5 minutes of stopping.

### Gaps
- Exact treadmill speed, walking duration per experiment, and per-experiment Ns/effect sizes from Oppezzo & Schwartz not retrieved (PDF blocked). I recall "comfortable self-selected pace" and sessions of ~4–16 min but cannot confirm.
- Rominger 2022 Hedges g values and moderators (intensity, duration, measurement timing) not retrieved.
- Duration of the residual effect after sitting is unknown beyond "shortly after."
- Transfer to real creative work (not AUT/analogy tasks) has not been tested in any study found.

## 3. Sleep and hypnagogia: N1 sleep onset (Lacaux 2021), overnight sleep (Wagner 2004), targeted dream incubation (Horowitz 2023) — and office feasibility

### Takeaway
Overnight sleep roughly triples the chance of discovering a hidden rule (Wagner et al. 2004, Nature; ~60% vs ~22%); a single minute of N1 sleep onset in a 20-minute reclined break also roughly tripled insight versus staying awake (Lacaux et al. 2021, N=103), but falling into deeper N2 sleep wiped the benefit; targeted dream incubation at sleep onset raised post-nap creativity on cue-related tasks (Horowitz et al. 2023). All three are single-lab findings on specific tasks; the N1 effect is the most office-feasible but hard to dose without EEG.

### Cited Findings
- Lacaux et al. (2021, Science Advances 7(50), eabj5866, "Sleep onset is a creative sweet spot"): N = 103; participants solved mathematical problems (Number Reduction Task) with an unnoticed hidden rule allowing near-instant solution; after a break, those who spent at least one minute in N1 were ~2.7× more likely to discover the rule than the wake group and ~5.8× more likely than those who entered N2. "A single minute of sleep onset is sufficient to boost the probability of insight by a factor of three compared to wake." **[verified]** — [HAL record](https://hal.science/hal-03474706); [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8654287); [Psychiatry Advisor summary](https://www.psychiatryadvisor.com/news/immediately-after-waking-up-from-a-light-n1-sleep-is-a-creative-sweet-spot/)
- Same study: insight rates of ~83% (N1 group) vs ~30% (wake) vs ~14% (N2); the 20-minute break was spent reclined in a dark room, eyes closed, holding a bottle in the hand (the "Edison technique"); participants were EEG-monitored. **[recalled; ratios above are consistent with these percentages; not re-verified]** — [HAL record](https://hal.science/hal-03474706)
- Wagner, Gais, Haider, Verleger & Born (2004, Nature, "Sleep inspires insight"): with the Number Reduction Task, subjects whose two training sessions were separated by nocturnal sleep were almost three times more likely to discover the hidden rule — ~60% (sleep) vs ~22% (wake). **[verified]** — [ResearchGate record](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/8908590_Sleep_inspires_insight); [follow-up review "Insights into sleep's role for insight"](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24605175/)
- Horowitz, Esfahany, Vega Gálvez, Maes & Stickgold (2023, Scientific Reports 13, "Targeted dream incubation at sleep onset increases post-sleep creative performance"): first controlled experiment on incubating dream content; the Dormio glove tracks muscle tone, heart rate and skin conductance, plays a pre-recorded cue at sleep onset, and prompts a spoken dream report; incubating dreams on a topic significantly increased post-sleep creativity on tasks related to that topic. **[verified]** — [Nature Sci Rep](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-31361-w); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37188795/); [MIT Media Lab](https://www.media.mit.edu/posts/dreams-and-creativity/)
- Counter-evidence on sleep and *classical* insight: "Sleep Does Not Promote Solving Classical Insight Problems and Magic Tricks" (PMC, 2018). **[verified existence]** — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5834438/)
- Newer: "N2 sleep promotes the occurrence of 'aha' moments in a perceptual insight task" (PLoS Biology, 2025) — i.e., which sleep stage helps may depend on task. **[verified existence]** — [PLoS Biology](https://journals.plos.org/plosbiology/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pbio.3003185)
- A commentary argues the "creative sweet spot may not be a sleep stage at all" (i.e., it may be the hypnagogic state/phenomenology rather than N1 per se). **[opinion piece]** — [Substack commentary](https://healthhippiemd.substack.com/p/zettelnaut-the-bottle)

### Inferences
- Grade: **Overnight sleep → rule insight (Wagner) = SINGLE-LAB origin but conceptually supported by later NRT studies from the same group; MIXED across insight task types** (null for classical insight problems/magic tricks). **N1 sweet spot (Lacaux) = SINGLE-STUDY (N=103, pre-registered-quality lab study).** **Targeted dream incubation (Horowitz) = SINGLE-STUDY, same lab that built the device.**
- Office feasibility: the Lacaux protocol is a 20-minute reclined, eyes-closed break — feasible with a quiet room and a recliner. The dosing problem: the benefit vanished if people slipped into N2, and without EEG you cannot see the N1/N2 boundary. The Edison-style object-drop is a crude proxy (muscle tone drops in N1), which is also what Dormio automates.
- Protocol (implementable, flagged as "experimental"): **"Edison nap" (15–20 min, max once per day, after lunch).** Screen 1: show the problem for 2 min; instruct "Hold this question loosely." Screen 2: "Recline, dim the lights, close your eyes, hold a light object (keys, a pen) over the edge of the chair. Let yourself drift. When it drops or you notice dream-like images, open your eyes and immediately write for 3 minutes." Hard timer at 20 min with a gentle alarm to prevent N2/N3. Screen 3: 3-min write-up, then return to problem. Expected: a few people will get a flash; most will get nothing — present it as a low-cost lottery, not a routine.
- Targeted dream incubation requires the Dormio device or an app approximating it; not practical for a 2–15 min break.

### Gaps
- Horowitz 2023 sample size (I recall N=49, four groups), nap length, cue wording ("think of a tree") and effect sizes could not be retrieved.
- Lacaux: whether the object actually dropping mattered, and the exact NRT trial counts/break timing, not retrieved.
- No study tests N1/hypnagogic breaks on open-ended creative work (ad concepts, copy); all evidence is on hidden-rule or cue-related lab tasks.

## 4. Mood and music: positive affect (Baas 2008; Davis 2009), happy music (Ritter & Ferguson 2017), and the counter-evidence (Threadgold 2019)

### Takeaway
Positive, *activating* mood (happy/excited, not relaxed) has a small-to-moderate, meta-analytically robust effect on creativity versus neutral mood (Baas et al. 2008: r = .15 across 102 effect sizes; Davis 2009: d = .52 vs neutral). Happy, high-arousal music during an AUT raised divergent-thinking scores vs silence in one study, but background music impaired compound-remote-associate (insight) solving in another lab. So: music or mood induction for generation, silence for convergent/insight work.

### Cited Findings
- Baas, De Dreu & Nijstad (2008, Psychological Bulletin 134, 779–806): meta-analysis synthesising 102 effect sizes; positive moods produce more creativity than neutral controls (r = .15); creativity is enhanced most by positive mood states that are activating and approach/promotion-focused (e.g., happiness) rather than deactivating (e.g., relaxed); negative deactivating moods (sadness) were unrelated; negative activating/avoidance moods (fear, anxiety) were associated with lower creativity, especially cognitive flexibility. **[verified]** — [RUG portal](https://research.rug.nl/en/publications/a-meta-analysis-of-25-years-of-mood-creativity-research-hedonic-t/); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18954157/); [author PDF](https://www.uva.nl/binaries/content/documents/personalpages/b/a/m.baas/profile-en/profile-en/uva:foldout/cpitem%5B2%5D/asset)
- Davis (2009, Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes 108, 25–38): with neutral mood as referent, sample-weighted mean d = .52 (δ = .55 after correcting for unreliability); non-experimental positive-affect/creativity studies mean r = .26; positive-mood effects "noticeably robust in relation to neutral mood states" but shrink against negative mood. **[verified]** — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S074959780800054X); [ERIC](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ821960)
- Ritter & Ferguson (2017, PLoS ONE 12(9), "Happy creativity: Listening to happy music facilitates divergent thinking"): five groups listened to calm, happy, sad or anxious classical music, or silence, while doing a divergent task; creativity was higher in the "happy music" (high arousal, positive valence) group than silence; no effect on convergent creativity. **[verified]** — [PLoS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article/file?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0182210&type=printable); [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5587106)
- Happy music piece used was Vivaldi's "Spring" (Four Seasons); N ≈ 155; divergent task was an AUT (brick). **[recalled; not re-verified]** — [PLoS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article/file?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0182210&type=printable)
- Threadgold, Marsh, McLatchie & Ball (2019, Applied Cognitive Psychology, "Background music stints creativity"): background music (instrumental and with lyrics) significantly impaired Compound Remote Associate Task performance versus quiet; authors say results "challenge the popular view that music enhances creativity." **[verified]** — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/acp.3532); [UCLan open PDF](http://clok.uclan.ac.uk/25989/8/25989%20Threadgold_et_al-2019-Applied_Cognitive_Psychology.pdf)
- Follow-up: "The susceptibility of compound remote associate problems to disruption by irrelevant sound" (J. Cognitive Psychology 2021) continues this line. **[verified existence]** — [Tandfonline](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/20445911.2021.1900201)
- A workplace study "Happy music and employee creativity in the workplace" (Social Behavior and Personality) exists. **[verified existence only; results not retrieved]** — [SBP](https://www.sbp-journal.com/index.php/sbp/article/view/11390/6938)
- Subramaniam, Kounios, Parrish & Jung-Beeman (2009, J. Cognitive Neuroscience): participants in more positive mood solved more CRA puzzles *by insight* and showed greater anterior cingulate activity when preparing for problems; anxiety predicted more analytic solving. **[verified]** — [author PDF](https://tiffaning.com/files/Subramaniam%20et%20al_2009_A%20brain%20mechanism%20for%20facilitation%20of%20insight%20by%20positive%20affect.pdf)
- Experience-sampling study of mood and everyday creativity (2019) exists, suggesting the within-person link is weaker/more complex than lab inductions. **[verified existence]** — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31379699/)

### Inferences
- Grade: **Positive activating mood → creativity = REPLICATED (two independent meta-analyses).** **Happy music during AUT = SINGLE-STUDY (Ritter & Ferguson); MIXED once insight tasks are included (Threadgold shows harm for CRAT).** **Positive mood → insight (Subramaniam) = SINGLE-STUDY with convergent neuroimaging support.**
- Mood inductions in the meta-analyses were typically short (film clips, music, recalled events, small gifts), so a 2–5 minute induction is within the evidence base.
- Protocol (implementable): **"Lift then list" (5–7 min).** Screen 1 (2–3 min): a short, genuinely funny/uplifting video clip or an upbeat, high-arousal instrumental track (the evidence favours high-arousal positive, not calming); or "write 3 things that went well this week." Screen 2 (3 min): AUT-style generation sprint on the live problem. Keep music OFF during any convergent/insight step (copy-editing, picking a route, puzzle-type problems).
- Avoid anxiety-inducing framing (deadlines, scoreboards) immediately before generation; fear/anxiety reliably lowers flexibility.

### Gaps
- Baas 2008 effect sizes for activating-positive vs deactivating-positive specifically, and for fluency vs originality, not retrieved.
- Independent direct replication of Ritter & Ferguson not found; a 2024 University of Reading paper on "divergent thinking, personality, and listening to music" exists but results not retrieved — [Reading CentAUR](https://centaur.reading.ac.uk/114326/1/114326%20VoR.pdf).
- No studies found on mood induction transferring to rated quality of real creative deliverables.

## 5. Noise and physical environment: 70 dB ambient noise (Mehta, Zhu & Cheema 2012), dim light (Steidle & Werth 2013), ceiling height (Meyers-Levy & Zhu 2007), nature/greenery, colour (Mehta & Zhu 2009)

### Takeaway
Most "environment hacks" rest on single-lab consumer-research studies. The 70 dB coffee-shop-noise effect has no published independent replication that I could find; the blue-vs-red colour effect from the same lab has a published failed replication and "no studies since have replicated" it; ceiling height is a single paper with no replications found. Dim light (150 lux) is a six-experiment series from one lab. Nature exposure has the strongest and most independent support (multi-day immersion raised RAT 50%; brief nature videos/backgrounds raise divergent originality in several newer studies).

### Cited Findings
- Mehta, Zhu & Cheema (2012, Journal of Consumer Research, "Is Noise Always Bad?"): five experiments; moderate ambient noise (70 dB) vs low (50 dB) enhanced creative-task performance (including RAT with coffee-shop recordings) and increased buying likelihood of innovative products; high noise (85 dB) hurt; proposed mechanism is distraction → higher-level construal. **[verified]** — [JSTOR](https://www.jstor.org/stable/10.1086/665048); [Illinois news](https://news.illinois.edu/research-too-much-too-little-noise-turns-off-consumers-creativity/)
- My searches found no published direct replication (successful or failed) of Mehta et al. 2012. **[absence noted]** — [search summary via ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2012/05/120514134332.htm)
- Mehta & Zhu (2009, Science, "Blue or Red?"): six studies; red improved detail-oriented tasks, blue improved creative tasks. Steele (2014, Psychonomic Bulletin & Review): "Failure to replicate the Mehta and Zhu (2009) color-priming effect on anagram solution times"; "no studies since have replicated their effects." **[verified]** — [Steele 2014](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13423-013-0548-3); [Mehta & Zhu 2009](https://www.science.org/doi/abs/10.1126/science.1169144)
- Meyers-Levy & Zhu (2007, JCR): three experiments; 10-ft vs 8-ft ceilings (with lanterns to emphasise height) → more relational/abstract vs item-specific processing (the "cathedral effect"). No replications found. **[verified; absence of replications noted]** — [Experts@Minnesota](https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/the-influence-of-ceiling-height-the-effect-of-priming-on-the-type/); [Wikipedia "Cathedral effect"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathedral_effect)
- Steidle & Werth (2013, J. Environmental Psychology 35, 67–80, "Freedom from constraints: Darkness and dim illumination promote creativity"): six experiments; key study N=114 German undergraduates in a simulated office at 150 lux (dim), 500 lux (standard office) or 1,500 lux (bright); dim light → "feeling of freedom, self-determination, and reduced inhibition" → better idea generation; authors caution dim light helps generation, not analysis/implementation. **[verified]** — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/242023319_Freedom_from_constraints_Darkness_and_dim_illumination_promote_creativity); [Salon summary](https://www.salon.com/2013/06/20/dim_lighting_sparks_creativity/)
- Later illuminance/colour-temperature studies on mood and creativity exist (Building Simulation 2020; Buildings 2025), i.e., the question is being re-tested, but results were not retrieved. **[verified existence]** — [Building Simulation 2020](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12273-020-0652-z); [Buildings 2025](https://doi.org/10.3390/buildings15101738)
- Atchley, Strayer & Atchley (2012, PLoS ONE, "Creativity in the Wild"): 56 Outward Bound participants; those tested after four days of backcountry hiking without devices scored ~50% higher on the RAT than those tested before. **[verified]** — [PLoS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0051474)
- Nature video vs urban video (J. Environmental Psychology 2025, "Fascination moderates the effects of nature video exposure on creative thinking"): online experiment; nature video enhanced creativity/originality of verbal divergent thinking and creativity of visual divergent thinking; effect strongest for people who found the nature fascinating. **[verified]** — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0272494425001823)
- "Effects of nature virtual backgrounds on creativity during videoconferencing" (Thinking Skills and Creativity 2021): a nature background image for a few minutes may enhance creative thinking. **[verified summary]** — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1871187121001917)
- "The effect of nature on creativity through mental imagery" (PLoS ONE) and VR spatial-openness/divergent-thinking (PMC 2023) extend this. **[verified existence]** — [PLoS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0315141); [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10042331/)
- "Nature versus non-natural environments: Effects on stress and creativity" (J. Environmental Psychology 2026). **[verified existence]** — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0272494426000307?via=ihub)

### Inferences
- Grade: **70 dB noise = SINGLE-LAB (no replication found).** **Blue colour = FAILED REPLICATION.** **Ceiling height = SINGLE-LAB.** **Dim light = SINGLE-LAB (six experiments, one group).** **Nature exposure → creativity = REPLICATED across labs and formats (immersion, video, virtual background), with moderate effects and a fascination moderator.**
- Note the cluster: noise, colour and ceiling-height effects all come from the Rui (Juliet) Zhu consumer-research group, and the one that was directly tested failed; weight the others accordingly.
- Protocol (implementable, low cost): **"Window break" (3–5 min).** Screen: full-screen slow nature footage (forest canopy, coastline, moving water) with natural sound, 3 minutes, instruction "Just look. Let your eyes wander; notice what catches you." Then a 2-min idea sprint. Offer as a default break screen; cheap and the only environment lever with multi-lab support. Give people the option to take the break at a window or outside if available.
- Not recommended as a built feature: coffee-shop noise generators, blue UI themes for ideation, or "dim the lights" prompts — label these "folklore / unreplicated" if mentioned.

### Gaps
- Sample sizes and effect sizes for the nature-video studies not retrieved.
- Whether any conceptual replication of 70 dB noise exists in environmental psychology (rather than consumer research) is unknown to me; none surfaced.
- Indoor plants/greenery: I found no controlled experiment on plants and creative output in this session (only mentions in nature reviews); treat as gap.

## 6. Priming and mindset: "be creative" instructions (Nusbaum, Silvia & Beaty 2014), creative-stereotype priming (Dumas & Dunbar 2016), psychological distance / construal (Förster), embodied metaphors (Leung et al. 2012)

### Takeaway
Simply telling people to be creative (defined as unusual, clever, uncommon) is one of the oldest, largest and best-replicated effects in creativity research (β ≈ .47 on rated creativity in Nusbaum et al. 2014; a 2023 meta-analysis confirms). Role-priming ("think like an eccentric poet") also works in one PLoS ONE study. Psychological-distance/construal effects on creativity are fraud-tainted at source (Förster) and need independent evidence; embodied-metaphor effects ("literally outside the box") are a single-lab series with conceptual but no direct independent replication.

### Cited Findings
- Nusbaum, Silvia & Beaty (2014, Psychology of Aesthetics, Creativity, and the Arts, "Ready, set, create"): the "be creative effect" is "one of the oldest findings in creativity science"; told to focus on creative quality, responses received higher creativity ratings, standardized β = .47 (large), while fluency dropped (~2.1 fewer uses, β = −.32). **[verified]** — [author PDF](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/sites.psu.edu/dist/c/122043/files/2020/08/2014b_Nusbaum_PACA.pdf); [Penn State record](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/ready-set-create-what-instructing-people-to-be-creative-reveals-a/)
- The "be creative" condition's wording defines creativity for participants as uses that are unusual, clever, uncommon, humorous, innovative or different, and explicitly says quality matters more than quantity; the "be fluent" condition says to generate as many uses as possible. **[recalled paraphrase; not re-verified]** — [author PDF](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/sites.psu.edu/dist/c/122043/files/2020/08/2014b_Nusbaum_PACA.pdf)
- "The power of the 'be creative' instruction: A meta-analytical evaluation" (2023, Elsevier journal) exists and confirms the effect across studies. **[verified existence; effect size not retrieved]** — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0023969023000760)
- Explicit "be creative" instructions also work across domains and cultures (Niu & Liu / "Effects of Explicit Instruction to 'Be Creative' Across Domains and Cultures"). **[verified existence]** — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/233401798_Effects_of_Explicit_Instruction_to_Be_Creative_Across_Domains_and_Cultures)
- Dumas & Dunbar (2016, PLoS ONE 11(2): e0142567, "The Creative Stereotype Effect"): participants primed to think like a high-creativity stereotype ("an eccentric poet") produced higher fluency and originality on divergent thinking than those primed with a low-creativity stereotype ("a rigid/stubborn librarian"). **[verified]** — [PLoS ONE PDF](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article/file?type=printable&id=10.1371/journal.pone.0142567)
- Förster: the Dutch National Board for Scientific Integrity (LOWI) concluded data in Förster's work were manipulated and recommended retraction; "Sense Creative!" (global/local sensory processing and creativity) is retracted. Förster et al. (2004) six experiments on temporal distance and creativity were foundational to the construal-level/creativity link. **[verified]** — [retracted paper record](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/258189205_RETRACTED_Sense_Creative_The_Impact_of_Global_and_Local_Vision_Hearing_Touching_Tasting_and_Smelling_on_Creative_and_Analytic_Thought)
- Independent work on distance/construal and creativity continues (e.g., "Distant Temporal Distance and Creative Thinking", Frontiers 2020; "Greater psychological distance, better creative-idea selection", PMC 2025; Mueller & Wakslak "Construing creativity"). **[verified existence; results not retrieved]** — [Frontiers 2020](https://public-pages-files-2025.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.576835/pdf); [PMC 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11837695/); [Mueller & Wakslak](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022103113001935)
- Leung et al. (2012, Psychological Science, "Embodied Metaphors and Creative 'Acts'"): five studies; sitting outside vs inside a 5×5-ft cardboard box → better RAT; joining halves of coasters ("putting two and two together") → more convergent thinking; gesturing "on one hand… on the other" → more ideas; free walking vs fixed rectangular path → more original ideas. Reviewers note "these experimental studies typically suffer from small sample sizes and lack of replication." **[verified]** — [SAGE](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797611429801); [APS release](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/news/releases/to-think-outside-the-box-think-outside-the-box.html); [4E creativity review (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6818493)
- Conceptual extension: Wang, Lu, Runco & Hao (2018, Consciousness and Cognition, "Break the 'wall' and become creative"): breaking virtual walls in VR before an AUT increased originality and fluency vs a no-wall condition; effect mediated by flexibility and persistence. **[verified]** — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1053810017305780); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29566975/)

### Inferences
- Grade: **"Be creative" instruction = REPLICATED (decades of studies + 2023 meta-analysis).** **Creative-stereotype priming = SINGLE-STUDY (two experiments, one lab).** **Psychological distance → creativity = FRAUD-TAINTED at origin; independent evidence exists but was not evaluated here.** **Embodied metaphors = SINGLE-LAB; conceptual VR replication only.**
- The "be creative" effect trades fluency for originality; for a break exercise, run two phases: fluency first ("as many as possible"), then "now be creative: unusual, clever, uncommon."
- Protocol (implementable): **"Two-gear sprint" (6 min).** Screen 1 (3 min): "List as MANY uses/angles/ideas as you can. Quantity only. Don't judge." Screen 2 (3 min): show the explicit definition: "Now give ideas that are *unusual, clever, uncommon, surprising*. Quality over quantity — fewer is fine." Optional priming line before Screen 2: "For the next three minutes, think as an eccentric poet would." Score originality by peer "most surprising" votes.

### Gaps
- Nusbaum 2014 sample size and verbatim instruction text not retrieved.
- Effect size and k from the 2023 "be creative" meta-analysis not retrieved.
- Dumas & Dunbar sample sizes/effect sizes not retrieved; no independent replication found.
- No direct replication (success or failure) of Leung 2012's box study found.

## 7. Structured techniques: brainstorming vs nominal groups vs brainwriting vs electronic brainstorming; constraints (Haught-Tromp 2017); SCAMPER; quantity-first; problem construction; analogy; "worst possible idea"; Design Thinking

### Takeaway
Face-to-face Osborn brainstorming is reliably worse than the same people working alone (nominal groups) on both quantity and quality (Mullen et al. 1991 meta-analysis, 20 studies, 800+ teams), mainly because of production blocking; brainwriting (silent, written, round-robin) and electronic brainstorming remove the blocking, with brainwriting beating nominal groups in Paulus & Yang (2000) and hybrid 6-3-5 with sketches beating nominal groups. Imposed constraints raised rated creativity of rhymes and the benefit carried over to unconstrained work (Haught-Tromp 2017). SCAMPER has mostly education-sector quasi-experiments with mixed originality effects; "worst possible idea" and Design Thinking have no controlled evidence I could find.

### Cited Findings
- Mullen, Johnson & Salas (1991) meta-analysis: 20 studies, >800 teams; "brainstorming groups are significantly less productive than nominal groups, in terms of both quantity and quality of ideas." **[verified]** — [ERIC document citing it](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED357399.pdf); [Purdue review "Questioning the Assumptions of Osborn's Brainstorming"](https://docs.lib.purdue.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1093&context=jps)
- Diehl & Stroebe (1987, 1991): production blocking (only one person can speak at a time; ideas are lost or suppressed while waiting) is the main cause of group productivity loss. **[verified]** — [ScienceDirect "Production blocking and idea generation"](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0022103103000404); [Wikipedia "Production blocking"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Production_blocking)
- Paulus & Yang (2000): brainwriting — writing ideas and passing them round-robin — enhanced idea generation relative to nominal groups; "the first and only known experimental evidence that brainwriting enhances idea-generation performance, relative to nominal groups" (as of the citing paper). **[verified]** — [Better than brainstorming? (ResearchGate)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228210918_Better_than_brainstorming_Potential_contextual_boundary_conditions_to_brainwriting_for_idea_generation_in_organizations)
- Hybrid 6-3-5 brainwriting with rotational viewing and words + sketches outperformed nominal groups on number of ideas with equal quality. **[verified]** — [Springer chapter "Effectiveness of Brainwriting Techniques"](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/978-0-85729-224-7_22.pdf); [6-3-5 method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/6-3-5_Brainwriting)
- DeRosa, Smith & Hantula (2007, Computers in Human Behavior 23, 1549–1581) meta-analysis of electronic brainstorming (EBS): EBS groups are more productive and more satisfied than face-to-face groups; EBS vs nominal groups showed no overall difference; nominal groups match or beat EBS below ~8 members, EBS wins for large groups (12+). **[verified]** — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0747563205000506); [MSU summary](https://msu.edu/user/kerr/p840/Sum.DeRosaSmithHantula.2007.htm)
- Dennis & Williams meta-analysis of group-size effects in EBS: "More heads are better than one" for electronic groups. **[verified existence]** — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/220474993_A_Meta-Analysis_of_Group_Size_Effects_in_Electronic_Brainstorming_More_Heads_are_Better_than_One)
- Haught-Tromp (2017, Psychology of Aesthetics, Creativity, and the Arts 11(1), 10–17, "The Green Eggs and Ham Hypothesis"): Study 1 — write a 2-line rhyme for an occasion that must include a given noun; Study 2 — participants chose their own nouns to include; both showed a main effect of constraint on rated creativity plus an order interaction indicating carryover: after practising with constraints, people stayed more creative on unconstrained rhymes. **[verified]** — [PsycNET](https://psycnet.apa.org/psycinfo/2016-17972-001/); [open PDF](https://www.cct.umb.edu/630/files/HaughtTromp2017-GreenEggsandHam.pdf); [Tromp 2023 integrated-constraints model](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/10892680211060027)
- SCAMPER: multiple experimental/control-group studies (mostly students/children) report gains in fluency, flexibility, originality and elaboration; but at least one found increased fluency and *not* originality; SCAMPER + group brainstorming produced more original business ideas than brainstorming alone in an entrepreneurship course. **[verified]** — [SCAMPER effectiveness study](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/297743527_The_Effectiveness_of_SCAMPER_Technique_on_Creative_Thinking_Skills); [SCAMPER in entrepreneurship brainstorming](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/373453665_Sparking_creativity_in_Entrepreneurship_courses_The_effect_of_using_the_SCAMPER_technique_in_Brainstorming_sessions); [SCAMPER product design, TSC 2023](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1871187123000524)
- Six Thinking Hats: a 2022 Thinking Skills and Creativity experiment tested de Bono's green hat / green-red combination in individual and dyad brainstorming. **[verified existence; results not retrieved]** — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S187118712200178X)
- Computer-supported scaffolds (prompts, group awareness) improved hybrid brainstorming outcomes (Computers & Education 2024). **[verified existence]** — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0360131524002434)
- Paulus's cognitive-social-motivational review of brainstorming (2007) summarises what improves group ideation (alternating individual and group phases, facilitation, exposure to others' ideas). **[verified existence]** — [Wiley](https://compass.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1751-9004.2007.00006.x)

### Inferences
- Grade: **Nominal > face-to-face brainstorming = REPLICATED (meta-analysis).** **Brainwriting > nominal = SINGLE-STUDY for the pure form (Paulus & Yang) plus hybrid 6-3-5 evidence; robustly ≥ nominal.** **EBS ≥ FTF = REPLICATED (meta-analysis); EBS > nominal only for large groups.** **Constraints → creativity = SINGLE-LAB (two studies) but consistent with a large theoretical literature (Stokes).** **SCAMPER = MIXED, low-quality education studies.** **"Worst possible idea", Six Thinking Hats as a whole, Design Thinking as a method = NO controlled evidence found.**
- The practical rule for an agency: never open a group ideation with talking. Generate silently first (brainwriting or individual), then share, then build.
- "Quantity first" is implicitly supported by the fluency-vs-originality trade-off in the "be creative" literature and by Osborn's rule tests, but I did not retrieve a direct test of Simonton's equal-odds rule in a session context (gap).
- Protocol (implementable): **"Silent 6-3-5 lite" (12–15 min, 3–6 people, async-friendly).** Round 1 (3 min): each person writes 3 ideas on their own card/screen — no talking. Rounds 2–4 (3 min each): cards rotate; add 3 new ideas that build on or twist what's there; sketching allowed. Final 2 min: each person stars the 2 they'd least expect. Show a per-round countdown and the "no talking until the end" rule on screen.
- Protocol (implementable): **"Green Eggs" constraint sprint (5 min, solo).** Screen: the live brief + a random constraint ("must include the word 'hinge'", "no colour", "must fit in a tweet", "for someone who hates us"). 3 min to write 3 ideas honouring the constraint, then 2 min unconstrained — the carryover is the point.
- Protocol: **"Random spark"** (forced connection with a random word/image) is widely used; it is best read as a cousin of the constraint effect and of SCAMPER prompts. Evidence is weak-to-mixed; label it "common practice" not "evidence-based."

### Gaps
- Diehl & Stroebe 1987 exact productivity ratios (I recall nominal groups producing roughly twice as many ideas) not verified.
- Paulus & Yang 2000 N and effect sizes not retrieved.
- Haught-Tromp 2017 sample sizes and effect sizes not retrieved.
- No controlled evidence located for "worst possible idea," problem-construction training as a break activity (Reiter-Palmon/Mumford work exists but was not retrieved), analogical-thinking prompts (Gick & Holyoak's hint-dependence is classic but not retrieved), or Design Thinking outcomes.

## 8. Training that works: creativity-training meta-analyses (Scott, Leritz & Mumford 2004; Ma 2006), divergent-thinking practice, RAT/CRA practice

### Takeaway
Creativity training has a moderate-to-large meta-analytic effect (Scott et al. 2004: 70 studies, overall d ≈ 0.64–0.68; transfer tasks d ≈ 0.51), with the biggest gains on divergent thinking and problem solving, from cognitively framed, longer programmes. Ten days of intensive RAT practice raised RAT scores in one study; whether that transfers beyond the test is unknown. For a break product, the realistic claim is "practice makes the lab task easier," not "practice makes your campaigns better."

### Cited Findings
- Scott, Leritz & Mumford (2004, Creativity Research Journal 16(4), 361–388): meta-analysis of 70 creativity-training studies; overall effect ≈ 0.68 (another report of the same work gives d = 0.64); the average trained participant outperformed ~75% of untrained peers; strongest effects on divergent thinking and problem-solving measures, weaker on attitudes and product generation; transfer tasks weaker but moderate (0.51); cognitively framed and longer training produced larger effects. **[verified]** — [Open PDF](http://www.psychologie.uni-mannheim.de/cip/tut/seminare_wittmann/meta_fribourg/sources/Scott_etal_2004_Effectiveness_of_creativity_training.pdf); [Research Nebraska](https://experts.nebraska.edu/en/publications/the-effectiveness-of-creativity-training-a-quantitative-review); companion paper [Types of creativity training (JCB 2004)](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/j.2162-6057.2004.tb01238.x)
- A newer "meta-analytic review and critical evaluation of 5 decades of creativity training" (White Rose repository, 2024) exists. **[verified existence; results not retrieved]** — [White Rose PDF](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/209749/1/Meta_Creativity_Training_accepted_pre_format_version.pdf)
- RAT training: "all participant groups' creativity performance and RAT scores improved significantly after 10 days of intensive RAT training," using an online RAT training system that presents items as text and images (Frontiers in Psychology 2020). **[verified]** — [Frontiers 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.00026/full)
- RAT validation and variants: "Remote associates test: An empirical proof of concept" (Behavior Research Methods 2019); systematic review of RAT studies 2000–2019 (PMC); visual RAT (PMC). **[verified existence]** — [BRM](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13428-018-1131-7); [PMC review](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7644781/); [visual RAT](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6997336/)
- Haught-Tromp's carryover finding (above) is small-scale evidence that even a single constrained practice bout lifts subsequent unconstrained performance. — [PsycNET](https://psycnet.apa.org/psycinfo/2016-17972-001/)

### Inferences
- Grade: **Creativity training (multi-session, cognitive-technique based) = REPLICATED (meta-analysis).** **RAT practice → RAT gains = SINGLE-STUDY; transfer untested.** **Daily AUT practice = no direct study retrieved (gap).**
- Ma (2006) is also a meta-analysis of creativity training with a similar-or-larger mean effect; I could not retrieve it this session (gap).
- Protocol (implementable): **"Daily rep" (3–5 min).** One CRA/RAT-style triad (gamified: streaks, hints) + one 2-minute AUT prompt with a rotating object and the "be creative" definition shown. Frame as warm-up; show personal fluency/originality trend lines. Be honest in-product: "this trains the exercise; evidence that it transfers to client work is thin."

### Gaps
- Ma 2006 effect size; 2024 meta-analysis effect size and quality critique not retrieved.
- No study found on brief daily divergent-thinking practice in working adults with real-work outcomes.

## 9. Mindfulness: open-monitoring vs focused-attention meditation (Colzato et al. 2012), meta-analyses (Lebuda et al. 2016; Hughes et al. 2023)

### Takeaway
A single ~35-minute open-monitoring (OM) meditation session improved divergent thinking while focused-attention (FA) meditation did not, in a small within-subjects study (Colzato et al. 2012); meta-analytically, mindfulness and creativity correlate modestly (r = .22 across 20 samples, Lebuda et al. 2016) with high heterogeneity, and a 2023 meta-analytic review again finds a positive but moderated link. For a short break, a brief OM-style "notice everything, hold nothing" exercise is the defensible choice; breath-focus (FA) is not expected to help idea generation.

### Cited Findings
- Colzato, Ozturk & Hommel (2012, Frontiers in Psychology, "Meditate to Create"): OM meditation (receptive to all thoughts/sensations, no fixed object) vs FA meditation (focus on one object); divergent thinking (AUT) was significantly enhanced after OM, convergent thinking (RAT) tended to improve after FA; authors argue the two creativity modes need different cognitive-control states. **[verified]** — [Frontiers PDF](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2012.00116/pdf); [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2012/04/120419102317.htm)
- Design details: N ≈ 19 experienced meditators, within-subjects, ~35-minute guided sessions. **[recalled; not re-verified]** — [Frontiers PDF](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2012.00116/pdf)
- Colzato et al. (2014): prior meditation practice modulates performance and strategy on creativity tasks (practitioners vs novices). **[verified existence]** — [author PDF](https://scottbarrykaufman.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/02/Colzato-et-al.-2014.pdf)
- Lebuda, Zabelina & Karwowski (2016, Personality and Individual Differences 93, 22–26, "Mind full of ideas"): 20 independent samples; mindfulness–creativity r = .22 (r = .18 before attenuation correction); significant but heterogeneous, with more between-study than within-study variance (moderators likely). **[verified]** — [MOCCA lab publications](https://mocca.uark.edu/home/publications/)
- Hughes et al. (2023, Psychonomic Bulletin & Review, "A meta-analytical review of the impact of mindfulness on creativity: Framing current lines of research and defining moderator variables"). **[verified existence; effect size and moderator results not retrieved]** — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13423-023-02327-w)
- "Mindful creativity: the influence of mindfulness meditation on creative thinking" (Frontiers 2014) and a mini-review on meditation and creativity exist. **[verified existence]** — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24454303); [Frontiers 2014 mini review](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2014.01083/pdf)

### Inferences
- Grade: **Mindfulness ↔ creativity = REPLICATED but small and heterogeneous (two meta-analyses).** **OM > FA for divergent thinking = SINGLE-STUDY (small N), with supportive follow-ups from the same lab.**
- The OM/FA distinction matters for product design: most consumer meditation apps default to breath-focus (FA), which the evidence does not link to divergent thinking.
- Protocol (implementable): **"Open sky" (5–8 min).** Audio-guided OM: "Sit or stand. Let attention be wide. Notice sounds, sensations, thoughts as they arise — label each lightly ('sound', 'thought', 'itch') and let it pass. Don't follow anything, don't push anything away." No breath anchor. Then a 3-min AUT-style sprint. Show a simple expanding/contracting ambient visual, not a breathing pacer.

### Gaps
- Hughes 2023 overall effect and whether OM vs FA was a significant moderator not retrieved.
- Colzato 2012 N and session length not re-verified; no independent direct replication found.

## 10. Boredom and creativity: Mann & Cadman 2014; Park, Lim & Oh 2019; replication status

### Takeaway
"Being bored first makes you more creative" (copying phone numbers for 15 minutes, then AUT with cups; Mann & Cadman 2014) has a published partial replication that "clearly failed" to find any positive relation between state or trait boredom and creativity when the control condition induced interest rather than nothing. Park et al. (2019) found a boredom boost in one study but only for certain personality profiles in another. Grade: MIXED/FAILED; do not build a "boring task" break on this.

### Cited Findings
- Mann & Cadman (2014, Creativity Research Journal, "Does Being Bored Make Us More Creative?"): participants who spent 15 minutes copying numbers from a phone directory later generated more creative uses for polystyrene cups than those who skipped the boring task. **[verified]** — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/271991375_Does_Being_Bored_Make_Us_More_Creative); [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Does-Being-Bored-Make-Us-More-Creative-Mann-Cadman/13cd2f72f5a669887c3af6df260ba217708859b1)
- Nettinga et al. (University of Waterloo thesis and journal article, "Exploring the Relation between State and Trait Boredom and Various Measures of Creativity"): "the attempted partial replication of findings from Mann and Cadman (2014) clearly failed to show any positive relation between boredom, boredom proneness and any measure of creativity"; the replication used a control group with a distinct induced mood (interest) rather than no induction. **[verified]** — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/371242526_Exploring_the_Relation_between_State_and_Trait_Boredom_and_Various_Measures_of_Creativity); [UWaterloo thesis](https://uwspace.uwaterloo.ca/bitstreams/200f342a-a6ea-4ef6-91df-dd8bbc187fb0/download)
- Park, Lim & Oh (2019, Academy of Management Discoveries 5(1), "Why Being Bored Might Not Be a Bad Thing after All"): boredom induced by sorting red and green beans one-handed for 30 minutes; Study 1 — bored participants produced more unique ideas on an idea-generation task, controlling for positive/negative mood; Study 3 — on a product-development task boredom helped only those high in learning-goal orientation, need for cognition, openness and internal locus of control. **[verified]** — [AOM](https://journals.aom.org/doi/10.5465/amd.2017.0033); [open PDF](https://ink.library.smu.edu.sg/context/studentpub/article/1015/viewcontent/Why_boredom_might_not_be_a_bad_thing_after_all__1_.pdf)
- A 2024 scoping review of boredom and creativity in educational contexts summarises inconsistent findings. **[verified existence]** — [Review of Education](https://bera-journals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/rev3.3470)

### Inferences
- Grade: **Boredom → creativity = MIXED with a FAILED partial replication.** The Mann & Cadman effect may have been a "doing a low-demand task before the AUT" (incubation-like) effect rather than boredom per se; the failed replication's design (interest control) is consistent with that reading.
- Do not ship a "copy phone numbers" exercise. If a low-demand filler is wanted, use the incubation framing (§1) and keep it pleasant.

### Gaps
- Nettinga et al. sample size, year and journal details not retrieved.
- Mann & Cadman Study 2 (passive reading vs writing) details not verified.

## 11. Physiological levers: acute aerobic exercise (Frith; Colzato 2013; Rominger 2022), caffeine (Zabelina & Silvia 2020), alcohol (Jarosz et al. 2012; Benedek et al. 2017)

### Takeaway
Acute moderate exercise has modest, heterogeneous support for creativity (walking being the best-supported form; see §2). Caffeine (200 mg) improved convergent/insight problem solving (CRA) but not divergent thinking in one placebo-controlled study (N=88). Mild alcohol (BAC ≈ .075, N=40; and ≈ .03 in a conceptual replication) improved RAT solving and insight-feeling but not divergent thinking. None of these is a sensible "break feature" beyond "coffee before a convergent task; not before brainstorming."

### Cited Findings
- Frith et al. (2019) systematic review: 13 studies, 92% positive, "weak to modest support" for acute moderate-intensity exercise benefiting creativity; no meta-analysis due to heterogeneity. Frith's 2019 dissertation found moderate treadmill exercise plus anagram priming improved subsequent RAT vs priming alone. **[verified]** — [EJOP](https://ejop.psychopen.eu/index.php/ejop/article/download/1773/1773.html?inline=1); [dissertation](https://egrove.olemiss.edu/etd/1586/)
- Rominger et al. (2022) multilevel meta-analysis: acute and chronic physical activity increase creative ideation. **[verified conclusion; sizes not retrieved]** — [Sports Med Open](https://sportsmedicine-open.springeropen.com/articles/10.1186/s40798-022-00444-9)
- "Effects of Acute Exercise on Verbal, Mathematical, and Spatial Insight Creativity" (J. Science in Sport and Exercise 2021) exists. **[verified existence]** — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s42978-021-00158-6)
- Colzato et al. (2013, Frontiers in Human Neuroscience): acute moderate exercise affected convergent thinking differently in athletes vs non-athletes (benefit for trained, impairment for untrained). **[recalled; not re-verified]** — [Frontiers listing](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2013.01020/full)
- Zabelina & Silvia (2020, Consciousness and Cognition, "Percolating ideas"): 88 volunteers, 200 mg caffeine vs placebo (double-blind); caffeine group solved significantly more CRA problems; no effect on divergent thinking or working memory; caffeine group reported feeling less sad. **[verified]** — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/339442376_Percolating_ideas_The_effects_of_caffeine_on_creative_thinking_and_problem_solving); [EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/861179)
- Jarosz, Colflesh & Wiley (2012, Consciousness and Cognition 21, 487–493, "Uncorking the muse"): 40 social drinkers brought to BAC ≈ .075; intoxicated participants solved more RAT items, faster, and more often reported solutions as sudden insight. **[verified]** — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/221785003_Uncorking_the_muse_Alcohol_intoxication_facilitates_creative_problem_solving); [BPS digest](https://www.bps.org.uk/research-digest/mild-intoxication-aids-creative-problem-solving)
- Benedek et al. (2017, Consciousness and Cognition, "Creativity on tap? Effects of alcohol intoxication on creative cognition"): conceptual replication at a lower dose; alcohol improved RAT performance while divergent thinking was unaffected and executive control decreased. **[existence verified; results recalled, not re-verified]** — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1053810016303713)
- Wiley & Jarosz (2012) review: lower working-memory capacity / attentional focus can help creative problem solving — the theoretical basis for the alcohol and caffeine dissociation. **[verified existence]** — [SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/0963721412447622)

### Inferences
- Grade: **Acute exercise → creative ideation = REPLICATED (meta-analysis) but modest and heterogeneous.** **Caffeine → CRA (not DT) = SINGLE-STUDY.** **Mild alcohol → RAT/insight (not DT) = REPLICATED across 2 labs (small Ns), obviously not an office intervention.**
- The consistent pattern across caffeine (helps convergent), alcohol (helps convergent), walking and happy music (help divergent) supports a two-mode design: a "generate" break set and a "crack it" break set.
- Protocol note (not a feature): tell people when to have their coffee — before a convergent/solve session, not as a brainstorming aid.

### Gaps
- Rominger 2022 effect sizes; Colzato 2013 exact design and numbers; Benedek 2017 N and statistics not retrieved.

## 12. Insight specifically: aha moments (Kounios & Beeman), mood and insight (Subramaniam 2009), insight accuracy (Salvi 2016), CRA as a trainable, gamifiable format

### Takeaway
Insight ("aha") solutions are measurably different from analytic ones: they are more often correct (Salvi et al. 2016, four experiments across CRA, anagrams, rebuses, line drawings, magic tricks), more likely in positive mood (Subramaniam 2009), and preceded by distinct brain states (Kounios & Beeman). Compound remote associates are the workhorse task and are easy to gamify; the evidence that CRA practice transfers to real insight is absent.

### Cited Findings
- Kounios & Beeman (2014, Annual Review of Psychology, "The Cognitive Neuroscience of Insight") and their earlier "The Aha! Moment" review summarise the neural signature of insight (right anterior temporal activity, preparatory alpha) and its sensitivity to mood and attention. **[verified existence]** — [Annual Reviews](https://www.annualreviews.org/content/journals/10.1146/annurev-psych-010213-115154); [Aha! review PDF](https://cpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/sites.northwestern.edu/dist/a/699/files/2015/11/Aha-The-cognitive-neuroscience-of-insight-2895xqe.pdf)
- Subramaniam et al. (2009): more positive mood → more CRA problems solved by insight; anxiety → more analytic solving; ACC activity during preparation tracked both mood and solving style. **[verified]** — [author PDF](https://tiffaning.com/files/Subramaniam%20et%20al_2009_A%20brain%20mechanism%20for%20facilitation%20of%20insight%20by%20positive%20affect.pdf)
- Salvi, Bricolo, Kounios, Bowden & Beeman (2016, Thinking & Reasoning 22(4), 443–460): insight solutions were more accurate than analytic solutions across four experiments and five task types. **[verified]** — [Salvi lab related record](https://doi.org/10.1080/17470218.2015.1136338); [2025 "Moment of Truth: Why Aha! Experiences are Correct" (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13036825/)
- Caution: artificially induced aha feelings make false statements feel true (Laukkonen et al. 2020) and false insights can be elicited with semantic priming (2021) — the aha feeling is a fallible signal. **[verified existence]** — [Laukkonen 2020 PDF](https://gwern.net/doc/psychology/cognitive-bias/illusion-of-depth/2020-laukkonen.pdf); [false insights, PB&R 2021](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13423-021-02049-x)
- Background music impairs CRA solving (Threadgold 2019) — see §4. — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/acp.3532)
- A perceptual-insight task shows aha moments are promoted by N2 sleep (PLoS Biology 2025) while classical insight problems are not helped by sleep (2018). — [PLoS Biology](https://journals.plos.org/plosbiology/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pbio.3003185); [PMC 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5834438/)
- RAT/CRA validation, item banks and training systems exist (BRM 2019; Frontiers 2020). — [BRM](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13428-018-1131-7); [Frontiers 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.00026/full)

### Inferences
- Grade: **Insight > analysis accuracy = REPLICATED within the Beeman group across tasks; independent replications not checked.** **Positive mood → insight = SINGLE-STUDY plus consistent meta-analytic mood evidence.**
- Design implications for a "crack it" break: quiet (no music), positive/low-anxiety framing, short CRA puzzles with a "did it come as a flash or did you work it out?" tag; use the flash-tag only as a self-awareness feature, not as a truth signal.
- Protocol (implementable): **"Three-word puzzle" (2–3 min).** Show one CRA triad at a time (e.g., "cottage / swiss / cake"), 30-s timer, hint after 20 s, ask "flash or worked out?" afterwards; silence enforced (mute music). Daily streak. Good as a warm-up before a convergent work block.

### Gaps
- Kounios & Beeman's practical recommendations (e.g., eyes-closed/looking-away before insight, time-of-day effects) not retrieved from primary sources this session.
- Independent (non-Beeman-lab) replication of the insight-accuracy effect not checked.

## 13. Flow and creative output

### Takeaway
Flow correlates with rated creativity in expert improvisation (Rosen et al. 2024: 32 jazz guitarists, 192 improvisations, EEG), and is itself a product of expertise plus "letting go" of control; a 2026 study finds the absorption component links to more ideas but lower quality on hard prompts. There is no experimental evidence that a short break can induce flow on demand, so flow should be treated as an outcome to protect (uninterrupted work blocks) rather than a break technique.

### Cited Findings
- Drexel (Rosen, Kounios et al., 2024): high-density EEG from 32 jazz guitarists improvising six pieces each (192 recordings) rated by four experts; more flow was related to greater rated creativity; experienced players showed more intense flow and reduced frontal (superior frontal gyrus) activity with increased left auditory/somatosensory activity — "practice hard, then let go." **[verified]** — [Drexel news](https://drexel.edu/news/archive/2024/March/New-Neuroimaging-Study-Reveals-How-the-Brain-Achieves-a-Creative-Flow-State)
- "Experiences of absorption and smooth performance during flow are linked to different aspects of creative thinking" (Consciousness and Cognition 2026): flow linked to more ideas but lower creative quality on prompts with scarce semantic neighbourhoods. **[verified]** — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1053810026000681)
- Qualitative work on flow in group creativity exists (Plymouth). **[verified existence]** — [Plymouth PEARL](https://pearl.plymouth.ac.uk/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1357&context=psy-research)

### Inferences
- Grade: **Flow ↔ creativity = correlational/EEG, SINGLE-LAB for the causal-direction claims.** No break-sized intervention evidence.
- For the team OS: protect 60–90-minute no-notification blocks for experienced practitioners rather than trying to "trigger flow" in a break.

### Gaps
- No RCT of any brief intervention inducing flow and raising creative output was found.

## 14. Which items are concrete enough to implement in a 2–15 minute "Break" section, and how should each be labelled?

### Takeaway
Six exercises have enough support to ship with an honest evidence label; three more can ship as "experimental"; and several popular ideas should be excluded or labelled folklore.

### Cited Findings
- Ship, labelled "well-supported": (1) **Walk & dump** (free walking, 6–8 min, generate during/immediately after; divergent only) — [Oppezzo & Schwartz 2014](https://www.apa.org/pubs/journals/releases/xlm-a0036577.pdf); [Murali & Händel 2022](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00426-021-01636-w); [Rominger 2022 meta](https://sportsmedicine-open.springeropen.com/articles/10.1186/s40798-022-00444-9). (2) **Park it** (read brief, low-demand unrelated filler 5–8 min, return) — [Sio & Ormerod 2009](https://research.lancaster-university.uk/en/publications/does-incubation-enhance-problem-solving-a-meta-analytic-review/); [Gilhooly 2013](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Don%E2%80%99t-wait-to-incubate:-Immediate-versus-delayed-in-Gilhooly-Georgiou/5a9d9d5c7702843869749dacbfb29467d2d66a13). (3) **Two-gear sprint** (fluency 3 min, then explicit "be creative" definition 3 min) — [Nusbaum et al. 2014](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/ready-set-create-what-instructing-people-to-be-creative-reveals-a/); [2023 meta-analysis](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0023969023000760). (4) **Silent 6-3-5 lite** (brainwriting before any talking) — [Mullen et al. 1991 via Purdue review](https://docs.lib.purdue.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1093&context=jps); [Paulus & Yang 2000 via boundary-conditions paper](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228210918_Better_than_brainstorming_Potential_contextual_boundary_conditions_to_brainwriting_for_idea_generation_in_organizations). (5) **Lift then list** (2–3 min high-arousal positive clip/track, then generate; music off for convergent steps) — [Baas et al. 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18954157/); [Davis 2009](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S074959780800054X); [Ritter & Ferguson 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5587106); contradicted for insight tasks by [Threadgold 2019](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/acp.3532). (6) **Window break** (3 min slow nature footage, then sprint) — [Atchley 2012](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0051474); [nature video 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0272494425001823); [virtual backgrounds 2021](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1871187121001917).
- Ship, labelled "experimental / single-study": (7) **Open sky** OM meditation 5–8 min — [Colzato 2012](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2012.00116/pdf); [Lebuda 2016](https://mocca.uark.edu/home/publications/). (8) **Green Eggs constraint sprint** — [Haught-Tromp 2017](https://psycnet.apa.org/psycinfo/2016-17972-001/). (9) **Edison nap** 15–20 min reclined with object-in-hand, hard timer — [Lacaux 2021](https://hal.science/hal-03474706); [Wagner 2004](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24605175/). (10) **Three-word puzzle** CRA warm-up for convergent blocks — [Zabelina & Silvia 2020](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/861179); [Salvi 2016 via 2025 PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13036825/).
- Exclude or label "folklore/unreplicated": mind-wandering-as-mechanism — [PMC 2023 review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10414778/); boredom induction — [Nettinga et al.](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/371242526_Exploring_the_Relation_between_State_and_Trait_Boredom_and_Various_Measures_of_Creativity); blue colour themes — [Steele 2014](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13423-013-0548-3); 70 dB noise generators — [Mehta 2012, no replication found](https://www.jstor.org/stable/10.1086/665048); dim-light prompts — [Steidle & Werth 2013](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/242023319_Freedom_from_constraints_Darkness_and_dim_illumination_promote_creativity); construal/"imagine it's a year away" prompts sourced to Förster — [retraction record](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/258189205_RETRACTED_Sense_Creative_The_Impact_of_Global_and_Local_Vision_Hearing_Touching_Tasting_and_Smelling_on_Creative_and_Analytic_Thought); "literally step outside a box" — [Leung 2012, no direct replication](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797611429801); "worst possible idea," Six Hats, Design Thinking as methods — no controlled evidence found.

### Inferences
- Expected magnitudes are modest: meta-analytic effects of d ≈ 0.3 (incubation), r ≈ .15–.26 (mood), d ≈ 0.5 (mood vs neutral in Davis), d ≈ 0.6 (multi-session training). Walking's "60%" and Lacaux's "3×" are single-paper headline numbers on lab tasks; present them as such.
- Everything is measured on AUT/RAT-type tasks within minutes of the manipulation; the product should measure its own effect (e.g., idea counts and peer-rated originality before/after each break type) rather than promise transfer.
- A two-mode taxonomy ("Generate" breaks: walk, mood, nature, two-gear, brainwriting; "Crack it" breaks: quiet, caffeine timing, CRA warm-up, Edison nap) maps cleanly onto the divergent/convergent dissociation that recurs across the literature.

### Gaps
- No study was found that measures any of these breaks against real agency deliverables (campaign concepts, copy quality, client ratings).
- Dose-response for break length (2 vs 5 vs 15 min) is not established for any of the interventions above; the incubation meta-analysis examined incubation length but the specific numbers could not be retrieved.
