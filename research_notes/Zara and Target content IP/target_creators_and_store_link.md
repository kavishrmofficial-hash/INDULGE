# Target: creator programmes and the content-to-store link, 2023-2026

Research date: 15 October 2026. Replaces the earlier unverified draft of this file.

**How this was researched**
- 25 WebSearch calls (standard mode).
- WebFetch failed with `getaddrinfo ENOTFOUND` on retailtouchpoints.com and modernretail.co, so no article page was opened in full.
- **Every finding below comes from search-result text, not from the full page.** Target's own announcement (corporate.target.com) appeared in results, but its full text was never read. Quoted wording is as reported in results and should be checked on the page before it goes into a client deck.

Labels:
- **[C]** cited. It has a source URL and a date.
- **[C?]** cited, but the only sources are secondary or blogs, or sources disagree. Treat as unconfirmed.
- **[I]** my inference, not a fact.

## 1. Takeaway

1. **The brief is mostly confirmed.**
   - On 6 May 2026 Target announced two creator programmes. **Club Target** is a gamified, tiered programme for everyday creators and superfans; thousands of creators took part in its pilot. **Target Ambassadors, powered by LTK** is an invite-only programme for established creators, with two tiers. [C]
   - They replaced a commission-based **Target Creator Program** with creator storefronts. Target started it in 2023 (reported as November 2023), told creators in April 2026 that it would close, and closed it in May 2026 (reported final date 7 May 2026). [C]/[C?]
2. **Why Target replaced it.** Sarah Travis, Target's Chief Digital and Revenue Officer, said creators "don't all operate the same way" and that one model no longer reflected how creators engage or how guests shop. [C]
   - Trade press frames the wider shift as fixing the weak spots of affiliate programmes: tracking gaps, problems at scale, and creator frustration with flat commission. [C]
   - The change was not smooth. Travis called it "particularly disruptive" for some creators. [C]
3. **Cash is now earned, not given.**
   - Club Target opens at 500 followers. Creators first earn points, gift cards, features on Target's own social accounts and event access. Commission comes only at the highest tiers. [C]
   - Ambassadors get higher commission and monthly bonuses, through a platform (LTK) where they already run their business. [C]
4. **The store and loyalty layers do the rest. Target has not linked them to the creator programmes in public.**
   - **Loyalty.** Target Circle was relaunched on 7 April 2024 with more than 100 million members and three options: free, card and paid. [C]
   - **Fulfilment.** Same-day services (Order Pickup, Drive Up, and same-day delivery for Circle 360 members) brought in more than $14bn in FY2025, about two-thirds of digital sales. Stores fulfil more than 96% of merchandise sales. [C?]
   - **In store.** The app has store maps, aisle locations and a Wallet that combines payment with Circle in one scan. [C]
   - **Results.** No creator-programme results (sales, traffic or new members) were found. Q1 2026 results (comps +5.6%, traffic +4.4%) say nothing about creators. [C]
5. **What this means for Westside (311 stores, 11.85M loyalty members per the brief).** The transferable design has four parts. [I]
   - A two-track programme.
   - Non-cash, status-based rewards for the many creators, with commission only for the few.
   - The loyalty ID as the creator's identity and attribution key.
   - Every challenge ending in a store action.
   - Target's own record shows the gaps to plan for: online-only attribution, a disruptive switchover, and no published results.

## 3. Inferences [I]

1. **Two tracks follow two economics.**
   - The many creators (Club Target, "thousands") are paid mostly in things Target owns or makes cheaply: features on its channels, gift cards, events and status.
   - The few (Ambassadors, "a few hundred") are paid in cash and bonuses, on a platform they already use.
   - This controls cost per creator at scale and keeps flat commission from going to low performers.
2. **The gamified tiers do two jobs.**
   - **Briefing.** Weekly challenges tell thousands of creators what to post: a Target run, a haul. That is a central creative brief in disguise.
   - **Selection.** Points and tiers show which creators drive sales before Target pays them commission.
3. **Attribution is still online.**
   - Every reward route found (affiliate links, referred revenue, storefronts, LTK) measures online sales.
   - Target's money is in stores (S3) and same-day pickup (S1), yet no source shows creator content being credited for in-store or Drive Up sales.
   - For a store-led retailer this is the main gap to design around.
4. **Target's identity layer is ready but not connected in public.**
   - Circle (L1) plus Wallet's single scan (L5) could in principle tie a creator referral to an in-store bill.
   - Whether Target does this is **not found**.
5. **Changing over is costly.** Raptive's wind-down (Jan 2026), the April notice and the May close, then a "particularly disruptive" switchover (R6, R7), show that creator trust is an asset that a programme redesign can damage.
6. **Social, then a store CTA.** Target's own social reach (about 3.3-3.4M on TikTok, 8.4M on Instagram) is smaller than its loyalty base (more than 100M). Creators widen discovery, and loyalty plus stores close the sale. The ratio matters more than the follower count.
