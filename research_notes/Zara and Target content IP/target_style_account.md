# @TargetStyle: Target's dedicated style account, and what Westside should build from it

Research date: 15 October 2026. For the Mask360 x Westside pitch (the Westside style account).

## How this note was made

- It builds on `target_style_and_ip.md` and `target_creators_and_store_link.md` and does not redo what their verification passes settled.
- Four research angles (anatomy, formats, commerce, comparables) were each researched and then re-checked by a separate verifier. **This note uses only claims the verifier marked verified or partly.** For a partly claim, the unconfirmed part is stated in the row. Claims marked unverifiable or refuted are listed in section 8 and are not used anywhere else.
- **No page was opened in this run, by any agent.** My own WebFetch of https://www.instagram.com/targetstyle/ returned `getaddrinfo ENOTFOUND www.instagram.com`. Every fact below rests on search-result text that quotes or paraphrases the page at the URL given. Check the wording on the page before it goes into a client deck.
- This pass used 4 WebSearch calls of its own (handle check, Front Row handle, Indian style accounts, Brides of Sabyasachi).
- Status key:
  - **verified**: seen in search text of the cited source by the verifier.
  - **partly**: the core was seen; a stated detail was not.
  - **NOT FOUND**: not seen in any source. An absence, not proof that the thing does not exist.
  - **[I]**: Mask360 inference or recommendation, not a fact.

## 6. What Westside should take

Each item names the verified practice it borrows. Where nothing at Target fits, it says "Westside's own".

1. **A separate, permanent style title beside the master brand** (S1, S3). Target has run one since at least 2014; in 2020 it was about half the size of @Target. Plan the Westside handle as a standing title, not a campaign account. [I]
2. **A name that reads as the title.** @TargetStyle is master brand plus "Style" (A1). Westside's lean, @seenatwestside, keeps the brand in the name but reads as the IP; @westsidestyle is the literal copy of Target's construction. Both need an availability check (I5). [I]
3. **One link, and every post pointing to it** (A3). Target's 2014 rule was that every post's caption said it was shoppable and sent people to the one profile link. Westside's link goes to a store picker that opens that store's WhatsApp with BOOK, HOLD or RSVP. The WhatsApp step is Westside's own.
4. **A narrow scope** (A2, S3): fashion and beauty only. Food and home stay on @westsidestores.
5. **A dated, hosted premiere**: announce on a Friday, debut the next day, live behind-the-scenes with a comic host (S6, S7). Westside does it in one store, not at a celebrity event, and ends it with an RSVP. [I]
6. **A short, fixed list of formats** (F1-F3), turned into named, numbered series with a store action, which Target does not show (F5):
   - Mausam = trend spotlight with a transitional outfit, under one named season (F1; "Forever Fall" in the earlier file).
   - The Window = curated trend edit plus a rotating showcase on a published clock (F3, B5, B6).
   - The Trial Room = stylist tips plus a named house stylist (F3, A9, F10).
   - Seen In = street-style snapshots plus city-local creators on real streets (F2, F3, F6).
   - First Look = the hosted live premiere, and the launch-night-then-store-day pattern (F4, F7).
   - The Regulars = city micro-creators, with tagging and disclosure (F2, A8, B10).
   - Dehleez = a named seasonal re-theme of the store and the event-giveaway-store-day order (B7, B8). Not a @TargetStyle series.
   - Five Functions = Westside's own; Tanishq has a function-by-function precedent in India (I1).
7. **City-local micro-creators shot on real streets by an in-house crew, with social as the master edit** (F2, F6).
8. **A tag-to-be-featured promise** (A7): tag the style handle and your store to be Seen In.
9. **A named stylist on screen who can be booked in store** (A9, F10). Indian demand for in-store styling is real: Shoppers Stop's personal shoppers bring in about a quarter of its sales (I2).
10. **The store as a content set with a visible clock** (B5-B7): one photo spot and one Window rail per series store, changed on a published day.
11. **Staff as talent, then a ladder outwards** (C3, C5, C6). M&S says staff content outperforms external influencers (its own claim).
12. **Editorial ownership inside the client.** Target runs creative in-house (S9) and has put "curation" in senior job titles (F11). Mask360 can work as an embedded style desk inside Trent's team. [I]

## 7. What Westside should not copy

1. **The online-only conversion route** (B1-B4). Target sends people to a product page or checkout. Westside's action is the store, and its measure is footfall per store.
2. **Dependence on a platform's shopping features** (A5). Meta changed the rules twice in a year. A store WhatsApp number in every caption and in the bio is Westside's to control. [I]
3. **The promise that a style account lifts apparel** (B11-B13). Target's apparel was still below expectations a year after the relaunch. Pitch a measured test with matched stores, not a formula. [I]
4. **The celebrity fashion-week scale.** The Tailgate is a US NYFW event. Westside premieres in one of its own stores.
5. **Anything Target-specific that is unsourced.** Do not describe Target's bio, highlights, grid, cadence or voice (2.2). Do not quote "as discoverable as it is shoppable" (unverifiable). Use only dated follower figures: 300,000+ each (Aug 2014), 2.2M (May 2020), 8.4M combined (Nov 2024).
6. **A staff-series analogy to "Teammates of Target"**: that series "centers around husbands" (A10).
7. **Five Functions as a world first.** Tanishq ran a function-by-function bridal series in June 2025 (I1). Position Westside's as the clothing companion, set apart by the store fitting.
8. **"First in India" without a hand check** (I3, I6).
9. **Any mention of Zara or Target in consumer copy**, and never "Zara at Westside prices". The comparison belongs in the pitch room only.
10. **Several new handles at once.** Start with one Instagram style handle. Run long Trial Room cuts on Westside's existing YouTube channel, as Target ran its creator series on its main YouTube channel (A10). [I]
11. **Flat commission for every creator.** Target replaced its 2023 commission programme, and its digital chief called the switchover "particularly disruptive" for some creators (earlier file, verified): https://retaildive.com/news/targets-digital-chief-breaks-down-the-retailers-creator-overhaul/819417/
