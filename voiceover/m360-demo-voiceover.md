# m360 OS: demo voiceover script

A founder's voice, first person, about 4 minutes at a relaxed pace (roughly 640 words).
The story runs on problems and real moments, not a feature list. Every example below matches how the app actually works.

The team in the examples is made up for the demo: **Riya** (designer), **Arjun** (strategist), **Meher** (account lead), **Kabir** (video editor), **Tanvi** (new joiner). Swap in real names if you like.

---

## Part 1: the clean script (paste this into ElevenLabs)

Paste one block at a time, so you can re-generate a single scene without redoing the whole thing.

**Scene 1: the hook**

It's seven forty on a Friday evening.
A client messages: "Hey, where are we on the reels?"
And I'm scrolling through six WhatsApp groups, three email threads, and someone's Google Sheet... trying to work out who did what this week.
If you run an agency, you know this feeling.
The work is happening. You just can't see it.

**Scene 2: the idea**

So I built m360.
It's the operating system for my agency.
One place where the whole day runs. The work, the clients, the team, the culture.
And the rule I built it on is simple: results and culture are the only two things that count.

**Scene 3: the day, without chasing anyone**

Here's Riya, one of our designers.
She walks in, taps "Check in, office", and it confirms she's actually at the office. She picks a mood. Today it's "on fire".
At seven, she posts her end of day line. What she shipped, what's next, and what's blocking her.
"Shipped six reel covers. Blocked on the brand's logo files."
If that blocker is still there two days later, m360 flags it to me by itself.
I didn't have to ask. Nobody had to chase anybody.

**Scene 4: a week with a rhythm**

Every week has four fixed moments.
Monday, everyone sets three outcomes that will exist by Saturday.
Wednesday is the twenty percent check. Arjun, our strategist, shows a rough direction early, and we catch a wrong turn before it costs us a full round of revisions.
And if a task does get sent back, m360 counts the revision. So quality isn't a feeling anymore. It's a number.
Friday, we review. Every outcome is marked hit or miss.

**Scene 5: it's a game, and the game is shipping**

Now here's the fun part. It's gamified.
Everything you do earns points. Checking in on time, posting your EOD line, hitting your outcomes, shipping tasks on time.
But output earns about three times what discipline earns. Showing up is the floor. Shipping is what scores.
So last week, Meher topped the leaderboard. Not because she came in earliest, but because she hit all three of her outcomes and closed a campaign with zero revisions.
That earned her a trophy too. "Clean pass."
There are streaks, levels, a podium, and trophies like "Five alive", "Early bird" and my favourite, "Night owl".
People actually check the leaderboard. It's genuinely competitive, in a good way.

**Scene 6: the vibe**

But an agency isn't just output. It's the people.
So there's a whole section called Vibe.
When Kabir stayed back till midnight to finish an edit, Riya gave him kudos. Everyone gets three a week, so they actually mean something.
There's a team feed for wins and polls. Birthdays and work anniversaries show up for everyone, with one tap to wish.
There's team chat, with direct messages and files.
There's even shared music. On a shoot day, the whole team is on one playlist, playing right inside m360.
And every week there's an anonymous pulse. If the team's energy dips, I see it early, and I can fix it before someone burns out.

**Scene 7: clients and new business**

Every client has a brain.
What they love. What they've approved. And the lines we never cross. Like: never use yellow on this brand.
Before a meeting, one tap gives me a prep note.
And for new business, there's Radar. It reads the Indian ad and marketing news every day.
Say a new CMO joins a big brand. Radar shows the story, I tap "Pursue", and m360 has the LinkedIn note, the email and the follow ups ready to go.
Our database of twenty five thousand contacts sits right behind it. I can just ask: "Who do we know at this company?"

**Scene 8: the buddy**

And then there's the buddy. A little flame that lives on your screen, talks to you, and does things for you.
When Tanvi joined, the buddy walked her through the whole place, out loud, on her first morning.
No one had to sit with her. By lunch she knew where everything lived.

**Scene 9: the founder's view**

For me, it all rolls up into one dashboard.
Who's in. What's shipped. What's stuck. How the pipeline looks. How the team is feeling.
Nothing ever gets deleted by accident. Every change is saved and can be rolled back.
I don't have to parent anyone. The system does the checking, so I can do the thinking.

**Scene 10: close**

That's m360.
It's how we run the work, and it's how we keep the culture alive.
Results and culture. The only two things that count.

## Part 3: ElevenLabs settings that work well for this

- **Model:** Eleven Multilingual v2 (most natural for a long founder read), or Eleven v3 if you want more expressive delivery.
- **Voice settings:** Stability around 40 to 50 (lower sounds more excited and alive, higher sounds calmer), Similarity around 75, Style around 20 to 30, Speaker boost on.
- **Pauses:** the "..." in Scene 1 gives a natural beat. For a longer gap between scenes, add `<break time="0.8s" />` at the end of a block (Multilingual v2 supports it).
- **Numbers:** they're already written as words ("seven forty", "twenty five thousand") so they're read the way you'd say them.
- **If you're cloning your own voice:** record 1 to 2 minutes of yourself reading Scene 5 and Scene 6 in your normal excited tone, then use that clone. It'll sound like you're talking about your own product, because you are.
