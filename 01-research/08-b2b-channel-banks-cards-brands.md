## 0. Executive summary (what the founder should take away)

1. **India's card-concierge market is already outsourced, and to foreigners.** HDFC Bank's Infinia concierge T&C names International SOS (whose lifestyle unit is Aspire Lifestyles), and HSBC India's Visa Infinite "Infinite Butler" runs on Aspire's own domain [E: competitive-landscape Blocks 6, 9]. Aspire has run B2B concierge and loyalty programmes in India since 2014 for banks, auto companies, real-estate and hospitality players [E: Block 6]. The operators of ICICI Emeralde, Axis Burgundy/Magnus/Reserve, SBI Aurum, Kotak, IndusInd, IDFC First, StanChart, DBS and Amex India lines are **not established by the evidence** and must be read off each issuer's concierge T&C PDF [VERIFY].
2. **Bank-card concierge is a volume, low-margin business with a cost-cutting buyer.** Ten Lifestyle Group, the listed global benchmark, earned GBP 62.9m net revenue from 349,000 *active* members in FY2024 (GBP 55.3m corporate + GBP 7.6m supplier) [E: Block 10] — roughly GBP 160–180 net revenue per active member per year, and far less per *eligible* cardholder [EST]. DreamFolks lost ~49% of revenue in one year (FY25 Rs 1,292 Cr → FY26 Rs 660.56 Cr) when ICICI, Axis and IDFC First switched or dropped lounge programmes in 2025 [E: Block 12]. Indian issuers have been rationalising card benefits since 2023 [K-high].
3. **Nobody in India sells a UHNW-grade white-label desk.** Aspire and Ten serve mass-affluent card books; Les Concierges (Bengaluru, Rs 8.24 Cr FY25 revenue, 84 staff) serves corporate employee programmes [E: Block 5]; Indulge Global has alliances with EO/YPO chapters and the Campden family-office network but no verified bank, developer or club contract [E: model-ops Block 56; Indulge notes A/B]; RedBeryl has partnered with D'MONDE Members Club (Mumbai) and Ellidore (global expansion, Sep 2024) [E: initial-scouting Block 1; competitive-landscape Block 3]. The open slot is a **"private tier" overlay** for the top 500–20,000 customers of a bank, wealth manager, developer or club — not the mass line.
4. **Winnable within 12–24 months, in order:** (i) private members' clubs and communities (weeks; small money, big credibility); (ii) ultra-luxury and branded residences (3–6 months; Rs 20–75 lakh per project per year [EST]); (iii) wealth managers, private banks' top tier and multi-family offices (4–9 months; Rs 25 lakh–1.5 Cr pilots [EST]); (iv) luxury auto, watch and jewellery brands' clienteling programmes (2–6 months; Rs 10–50 lakh per programme [EST]); (v) corporate C-suite/promoter packages (3–6 months; Rs 10–40 lakh per company [EST]). **Bank card RFPs** (9–18+ months, incumbent Aspire, Rs 1–5 Cr per year but thin margin and switch-off risk [EST]) are a Year-2/3 play, and only for a super-premium overlay.
5. **Pricing models to master:** per-eligible-member-per-month (PMPM), per-active-member, per-request, retainer + success/supplier share, and Amex-style partner-funded benefits. Global card-concierge PMPM for mass-premium books is cents, not dollars; per-request fees in the GBP 15–40 band; dedicated desks are sold per seat [K-med] [VERIFY with an ex-Aspire/Ten salesperson]. In India, an outsourced premium-card concierge probably costs the issuer Rs 30–150 per eligible card per year [EST, K-low].
6. **Contract hygiene matters more than logos:** DreamFolks' collapse shows that non-exclusive, short, no-minimum bank contracts can be switched off in a quarter [E: Block 12]. Insist on term (2–3 years), minimum annual fee, notice periods, change-of-scope pricing and data-portability clauses; cap any one client at ~30–40% of revenue [EST].
7. **Case-study lessons:** Ten won HSBC's Jade wealth proposition in 2018 on global multi-market delivery plus a technology platform, and later a multi-year "fully digital" programme for ~1 million premium clients of a European bank [E: Block 4; tenlifestylegroup.com 2018 release]; Aspire won India by bundling medical/travel assistance (International SOS heritage) with concierge and by riding card-network (Visa/Mastercard) premium-benefit programmes [E + K-med]; Amex funds much of its benefits via partners who pay for access to high spenders [K-high]. A new Indian entrant should copy the overlay structure (Ten-Jade), the assistance bundle (Aspire) and partner-funded benefits (Amex), and avoid the aggregator trap (DreamFolks).
8. **Numbers to verify first:** each issuer's concierge provider (T&C PDFs); Ten's FY2025 contract disclosures; DreamFolks' FY26 annual report and concentration; Aspire Lifestyles India's MCA filings; Les Concierges' client list; RedBeryl–D'MONDE deal terms; Amex India Platinum/Centurion fees and India delivery partner.

### 1.2 The three ways a card concierge gets bought

1. **Network-funded programmes (Visa / Mastercard / Diners / RuPay).** The network appoints and pays a concierge vendor for its premium tiers (Infinite, World Elite, Diners Club Black/Premium, RuPay Select) and issuers switch it on for eligible BINs. Cost to the issuer: nil or a small per-card levy; quality: call-centre standard; contract owner: the network's regional benefits team (Singapore/Mumbai) [K-med]. This is how a single vendor (Aspire) ends up behind many bank logos.
2. **Issuer-outsourced programmes.** The bank's cards/premium-banking team runs an RFP for a branded desk ("HDFC Infinia Concierge", "i-Assist", "Axis Bank Concierge") with issuer-specific scripts, SLAs and MIS; paid PMPM on eligible cards, per call/request, or per dedicated seat [K-med]. Aspire's HDFC and HSBC desks are of this kind [E].
3. **In-house or captive.** Amex's Platinum/Centurion servicing [K-med]; HDFC's own "Infiniti" HNI-banking RM layer sits above the outsourced desk [K-low]. Big private-banking arms (Kotak Private, ICICI Private, HDFC Private) use RMs plus ad-hoc partners rather than a contracted lifestyle desk [K-low].

A new entrant can sell into all three: to networks (as a premium-tier "specialist" vendor), to issuers (as an overlay for the top 0.1–1% of the book), and to captive private-banking desks (as the fulfilment partner behind the RM).

### 1.3 What the T&Cs reveal (and how to use them)

- Indian concierge T&Cs typically state that the service is provided by a **third-party service provider**, that the bank is **not liable** for fulfilment, that the cardholder pays all third-party costs, that the provider may record calls and share data with the bank, and that the bank may **withdraw or modify** the benefit at any time [K-high]. HDFC's Infinia T&C names International SOS as the provider [E].
- For a seller, three clauses matter: (a) **who the counterparty is** (issuer vs network); (b) the **fee-for-service disclosure** ("charges may apply for certain requests") which tells you whether the provider earns per-request revenue from the cardholder; (c) **exclusions** (no political/legal/medical advice, no purchases without cardholder payment) which define the service ceiling and the room above it for a paid premium layer.
- Since 2022 the **RBI Master Direction on Credit Card and Debit Card – Issuance and Conduct** requires issuers to give cardholders clear MITC and to be responsible for outsourced activities related to the card [K-med] [VERIFY the exact clause numbers in the 2022 Master Direction as amended to 2024–25]. This is why issuer procurement asks for audit rights, call recording, data localisation and exit clauses.

### 1.4 Procurement mechanics inside an Indian bank (what it takes to be a vendor)

| Step | What happens | Typical duration [EST] |
|---|---|---|
| Sponsor identification | Head of Cards / Head of Premium Banking / Head of Private Banking or Wealth; the "benefits and partnerships" manager runs the day-to-day | 1–3 months to reach and interest |
| Vendor empanelment | Company KYC, MCA documents, GST, 2–3 years of audited financials (start-ups can be waived with a sponsor), ISO 27001 or equivalent information-security posture, background verification of staff, business-continuity plan, insurance | 1–3 months; a start-up will fail a formal empanelment without a sponsor willing to run a "pilot under marketing budget" |
| RFP / RFQ | Scope, SLAs, MIS, pricing template (PMPM, per request, per seat), penalties, exclusivity, IP/brand rules; evaluated by procurement plus the business | 2–4 months |
| Information-security and outsourcing review | Data classification (cardholder PII), DPDP Act 2023 / DPDP Rules 2025 obligations, RBI outsourcing risk framework (the bank's own vendor-risk policy derived from RBI guidelines on outsourcing of financial services and the 2023 Master Direction on outsourcing of IT services), VAPT of any portal/app, right-to-audit, sub-contracting approval | 1–3 months, overlapping |
| Legal and commercial closure | MSA + SOW; term 1–3 years; termination for convenience with 30–90 days' notice is common; liability caps; indemnities | 1–2 months |
| Go-live | Scripts, IVR/WhatsApp routing, bank-branded email/telephony, staff training, MIS dashboards | 1–2 months |

**Total sales cycle: 6–18 months to first revenue** for an issuer programme [EST, K-med]. Two shortcuts exist: (i) a **marketing-budget pilot** (event-linked or "top 500 customers" programme) signed as a vendor PO rather than an outsourcing contract; (ii) coming in through the **network** (Visa/Mastercard India premium-benefits teams have discretionary budgets for issuer co-marketing) [K-med].

### 1.5 Context 2023–26: benefit rationalisation

- Indian issuers have devalued super-premium benefits since 2023 (Axis Magnus/Reserve changes, lounge rules moving to spend thresholds, capped reward rates) [K-high for the trend; specifics [VERIFY]].
- DreamFolks: ICICI phased out its DreamFolks lounge programmes from 1 July 2025; Axis discontinued similar arrangements around the same time; IDFC First switched providers in Aug–Sep 2025; DreamFolks discontinued domestic airport-lounge services outright from 16 September 2025; domestic lounges were ~90% of its revenue [E: competitive-landscape Block 12]. Management attributes it to the ecosystem moving from unlimited to spend-based access [E]. DreamFolks is now pivoting to card-linked travel/lifestyle benefits and rail lounges [E].
- Read-across: banks are cutting *cost per card* on benefits, so the pitch to a bank cannot be "add a cost". It must be **"replace a cost you already pay with a better-perceived benefit for a narrower, richer segment"** or **"partner-funded, zero-net-cost benefits"** (Section 2.6 and 5.6).

### 2.2 SLAs, exclusivity, term, RFP and sales cycles

- **SLAs commonly written into concierge contracts** [K-med]: telephone answer within 20–30 seconds (80/20 or 90/30 service level); chat/WhatsApp first response within 2–15 minutes; email within 2–4 hours; acknowledgement of complex requests within 1 hour and proposal within 24–48 hours; fulfilment turnaround by category; abandonment rate < 3–5%; CSAT ≥ 90% or NPS targets; complaint escalation within 24 hours; 99.5% platform uptime; monthly MIS (volumes by category, conversion, CSAT, revenue generated). Penalties: 2–10% of monthly fee for SLA misses; step-in and termination for repeated failure.
- **Exclusivity**: issuers rarely grant it; providers ask for category exclusivity ("sole concierge provider for the X tier") and non-solicitation of their staff. Networks typically appoint one vendor per region per tier [K-med].
- **Term**: India bank/vendor MSAs are commonly 1–3 years with auto-renewal and termination for convenience on 30–90 days' notice [K-med]; Ten describes multi-year contracts, typically three to five years, with fixed and variable fee elements [K-med] [VERIFY in Ten's annual report "Business model" section]; the 2024–25 wins are described as "multi-year" [E: Block 4].
- **RFP cycle**: Indian issuers re-tender benefit vendors every 2–3 years or at devaluation events; card-network vendor reviews run on 3–5 year cycles [K-low].
- **Sales cycle**: 6–18 months for an issuer programme; 3–9 months for a wealth manager/private bank pilot; 1–3 months for a club or brand activation; 2–6 months for a developer [EST, Section 4].

### 2.3 What Ten Lifestyle Group discloses about bank contracts (the public benchmark)

Ten Lifestyle Group plc (AIM: TENG; founded 1998 by Alex Cheatle and Andrew Long; IPO November 2017) [K-high] is the only listed pure-play, so its disclosures are the best public view of B2B concierge economics.

- **Scale (FY2024, year to 31 Aug):** net revenue GBP 62.9m = corporate GBP 55.3m + supplier GBP 7.6m; 349,000 active members; 50+ corporate clients including HSBC, Coutts, Royal Bank of Canada, Westpac [E: Block 10; Block 4]. FY2025 annual report and results presentation were published in November 2025 [E: Block 10, https://tenlifestylegroup.com/annual-report-accounts-2025/] — **figures not captured; [VERIFY]** FY2025 net revenue, adjusted EBITDA, active members and contract categories there.
- **Client-size disclosure:** Ten categorises contracts by annualised net revenue — Small (< GBP 0.25m), Medium (GBP 0.25–1m), Large (GBP 1–2m), Extra Large (> GBP 2m) — and reports the count of Large/Extra Large contracts each year [K-med] [VERIFY the bands and counts in the FY2025 strategic report]. A "Large" bank contract is therefore GBP 1–2m (Rs 11–22 Cr) a year; "Extra Large" > GBP 2m.
- **Fee structure:** corporate revenue is "largely fixed" with variable elements tied to member activity or numbers, under multi-year contracts [K-med]; supplier revenue is commissions from hotels (Ten Private Collection), tickets, dining and travel booked through the platform [K-med].
- **Recent wins:** a new multi-year contract with an existing banking client for a fully digital customer-experience programme for around one million premium clients in Europe [E: Block 4, TipRanks/MarketScreener]; an expanded contract with Norway's DNB [E: Block 4, MarketScreener]; HSBC Jade in 2018 [E: https://tenlifestylegroup.com/2018/03/15/ten-provide-services-to-hsbc-wealth-proposition/].
- **Concentration:** Ten's largest client (widely understood to be HSBC across several regions) has been a material share of revenue; the annual report's "principal risks" section discusses client concentration [K-med] [VERIFY the percentage].
- **Margins:** group adjusted EBITDA margin in the mid-to-high teens in FY2024 [K-low] [VERIFY]; the gap between corporate revenue per active member (~GBP 158) and Ten's cost base (20+ service locations, tech investment) is the whole economics of B2B concierge: it works only at scale and with a digital-first funnel [EST].
- **Members vs eligibles:** "millions of members have access" [E: Block 4] but only 349k were *active* — a 5–15% activation ratio on eligible bases is normal for card programmes [K-med]. Any Indian pitch must model eligibles, registrations, actives and requests per active separately.

### 2.4 What DreamFolks discloses about unit economics (the aggregator cautionary tale)

- **Model:** asset-light aggregator between card networks/banks and airport lounges (later spas, golf, meet-and-greet, F&B); founder Liberatha Peter Kallat; IPO August 2022 [K-high]. Paid **per access** by the network/issuer; pays the lounge operator per visitor; keeps the spread [K-high].
- **Numbers:** FY25 revenue Rs 1,292 Cr, net profit Rs 65 Cr; FY26 revenue Rs 660.56 Cr (−48.8%), PAT Rs 11.62 Cr (−82.1%) [E: Block 12]. That is a ~5% net margin at peak and ~1.8% after the shock [EST from E]. Gross margin historically in the ~11–14% band [K-med] [VERIFY in the FY24/FY25 annual reports]. Revenue per lounge access in the high hundreds of rupees [K-low] [VERIFY].
- **Contract shape that killed it:** non-exclusive, short-tenor, no minimum-volume contracts with a handful of issuers/networks; top clients accounted for the large majority of revenue [K-med] [VERIFY concentration disclosures in the RHP (2022) and FY25 annual report]. When issuers re-priced lounge access (spend thresholds) and moved to alternative providers, revenue halved in a year [E].
- **Lessons:** (1) pass-through aggregation earns single-digit margins; (2) benefit inflation gets rationalised; (3) an aggregator with no owned supply and no consumer relationship has no moat; (4) contract term, minimums and exit notice are worth more than the logo.

### 2.6 Indian benchmarks — what a bank actually pays (estimates with reasoning)

There is no public Indian price list; the following is triangulated [EST, K-low] [VERIFY with two ex-issuer benefits managers]:

- **Cost of an outsourced Indian call-centre seat:** Rs 45,000–90,000 per month all-in for a bilingual, graduate agent with supervision and telephony (BPO benchmark) [K-med]. A concierge agent with travel/dining fulfilment skills: Rs 70,000–1.5 lakh per month loaded [EST].
- **Request volumes:** on a super-premium Indian card book, 3–10% of cardholders use the concierge in a year, making 2–6 requests each [K-low]. A 300,000-card programme therefore generates roughly 30,000–150,000 requests a year — 5–25 seats [EST].
- **Implied issuer spend:** Rs 1–4 Cr a year for a large super-premium programme, i.e. **Rs 30–150 per eligible card per year** or Rs 200–600 per request [EST]. This reconciles with cards whose annual fees are Rs 10,000–12,500 and whose total benefit budgets run to a few thousand rupees per card [K-med].
- **What this means for a luxury entrant:** the mass line is a Rs 1–4 Cr, 15–25% gross-margin BPO contract. The only interesting bank product is a **private-tier overlay**: 1,000–20,000 families at Rs 3,000–15,000 per family per year (Rs 30 lakh–3 Cr per issuer), delivered by lifestyle managers rather than agents, with partner-funded benefits to keep the issuer's net cost near zero [EST]. That is Ten-for-Jade, not Aspire-for-Infinia.

## 3. The other B2B buyers

### 3.1 Luxury real-estate developers and branded residences

(i) **Pre-possession (marketing):** the sales/marketing head buys "resident lifestyle concierge" as a differentiator for launches and for the fit-out/handover period; budget from the project marketing line; contracts 1–2 years; often a branded desk in the show-flat/clubhouse [K-med]. Aspire lists real estate among its Indian client categories [E: Block 6]; Quintessentially has historically tied up with developers in London and Dubai [K-med]; Lodha's ultra-luxury towers marketed concierge partnerships in the 2013–2016 period [K-low] [VERIFY in old brochures/press]. (ii) **Post-possession (operations):** the facility-management company (JLL, CBRE, Knight Frank PM, Cushman) or the residents' association pays from CAM charges for a desk; here the buyer is cost-focused and the service becomes front-desk-plus [K-med].

Ultra-luxury supply: ~59 deals ≥ Rs 40 Cr worth ~Rs 4,750 Cr in 2024, ~80% Mumbai (Anarock, via companion note) [K-med] [VERIFY] — a few hundred households per tower cluster, which is exactly the private-tier segment.

- **Risk:** developers cut marketing spend when sales slow; RWAs re-tender on price; on-site staff cost rises with attrition.

### 3.2 Wealth managers, private banks and family offices

### 3.10 Hospitals and medical tourism

- Apollo, Fortis, Max, Medanta, Manipal, Narayana, Kokilaben, Aster run international-patient desks; India's medical-value-travel market is estimated in the US$7–13bn range (2024–25) with the Gulf, Africa, Bangladesh and CIS as sources [K-low] [VERIFY: FICCI–EY / Ministry of Tourism "Heal in India" figures]. Outbound UHNW medical travel (Mayo, Cleveland, MD Anderson, Singapore, London) is unserved except by ad-hoc facilitators [K-med].

For a luxury concierge, the cleaner structure is **per-case fees paid by the family** with hospital partnerships for access, not hospital-paid referral fees (reputational and regulatory risk) [EST].
- **Deal size:** small and per case; treat as a **service line** for members and family offices rather than a B2B revenue segment.

### 4.2 Segment-by-segment pitch and pilot structures

**A. Private members' clubs (D'MONDE, Quorum, BLVD, Jolie's, Soho House if open to it)**
- *Pitch:* "Your members ask your front desk for things your front desk cannot do — a table in Tokyo, a jet on Friday, a Wimbledon debenture. We become your invisible lifestyle desk under your brand; your team escalates to us on WhatsApp; your members see your name, not ours."
- *Pilot:* 90 days; zero retainer (or Rs 1–2 lakh per month for an on-site presence on Thursday–Saturday); a white-labelled WhatsApp line; 10 "money-can't-buy" club experiences co-hosted; conversion offer to full membership at a club-member price; report weekly on requests and NPS. Success metric: 15–30 conversions and 3 co-hosted events.
- *Contract points:* non-exclusive both ways in Year 1; club gets 10–15% of first-year fees on referred members [EST]; data belongs to you, with the member's consent under DPDP.

**B. Ultra-luxury residences (Lodha, DLF, Oberoi, Prestige, Raheja, Piramal, Trump-branded towers)**
- *Pitch to the sales/marketing head:* "You sell Rs 10–100 Cr homes with a clubhouse; buyers compare you to Four Seasons Residences. Give them a genuine lifestyle desk from booking to handover and for the first year of living — and give your sales team a reason to close." Bring three numbers: your service SLA, the cost per household per month (target Rs 2,500–6,000), and the events calendar.
- *Pilot:* one tower, 6–12 months; on-site lifestyle manager 5 days a week plus 24/7 remote pod; a resident app/WhatsApp line under the project brand; monthly resident events funded partly by luxury-brand partners (car, watch, champagne); fee Rs 2–5 lakh per month plus pass-through costs [EST]. Include a hand-over clause so the RWA/FM company can continue at a published rate.
- *Contract points:* term 12 months with a 12-month extension option; brand usage rules; minimum fee; termination 60–90 days; exclusivity limited to the project.

**C. Wealth managers, private banks and multi-family offices (360 ONE, Nuvama, Waterfield, Julius Baer, LGT, Avendus, Kotak Private, HDFC Private)**
- *Pitch to the CEO/Head of Private Banking or client experience:* "Your RMs are being asked to book Amanpuri, find a paediatric cardiologist in London and get a Birkin — and they are doing it badly and for free. We give your top 500 families a named lifestyle manager under your brand, with transparent supplier commissions disclosed to you, and you keep the relationship." Position as a retention and share-of-wallet tool, and as an RM productivity tool.
- *Pilot:* 6–9 months for 200–500 families; fee Rs 10,000–25,000 per family per year or 2–4 dedicated seats at Rs 1.5–2.5 lakh per seat per month [EST]; joint quarterly events; a "family-office desk" for staffing, medical navigation, education logistics and property administration. Success metric: activation ≥ 40% of enrolled families, NPS ≥ 70, RM-reported retention wins.
- *Contract points:* strict data segregation; no revenue share from the client (keep supplier commissions but disclose them); no solicitation of families for direct membership during the term; one-year term with renewal; concentration cap in your own plan.

**D. Luxury auto, watch and jewellery brands (Mercedes-Benz, BMW, Porsche, Lamborghini, Bentley, Rolls-Royce importers and dealer groups; Ethos, Kapoor, Johnson, Zoya, Bulgari/Cartier)**
- *Pitch to the CRM/marketing head:* "Your top 300 owners/clients buy from whoever makes them feel known. We run your owner programme — priority dining and travel, a curated calendar (F1, Wimbledon, Art Basel, Geneva), family lifestyle requests — under your brand, and we bring qualified buyers to your launches." Offer a menu: per-activation, per-programme-year, or per-client subscription.
- *Pilot:* one launch or one quarter; Rs 10–25 lakh; 100–300 clients; measurable outcomes (event attendance, test-drive/showroom visits, referrals); co-funded by a hotel/champagne partner.
- *Contract points:* IP/brand guidelines; approval workflow; performance bonus tied to leads.

**E. Corporates (CXO/promoter packages)**
- *Pitch to the CHRO/CEO office:* "A company-paid lifestyle membership for your top 10 leaders costs less than one executive search and is remembered every week." Bundle: family travel, medical navigation, school admissions, relocation, events. Provide a perquisite-tax note prepared by a CA.
- *Pilot:* 5 executives, 6 months, Rs 5–10 lakh; upgrade path to full memberships.

**F. Card networks and issuers (Year 2 onwards)**
- *Pitch to Visa/Mastercard India premium-benefits teams:* "Your Infinite/World Elite concierge is a call centre. Give the top 5% of eligible cards a specialist lifestyle tier, co-funded by luxury partners, delivered by lifestyle managers, measured on activation and spend uplift." Then take that proof to HDFC/ICICI/Axis/SBI Card/Amex as an **overlay**, never as a replacement for the mass desk in Year 1.
- *Pilot:* 1,000–3,000 cardholders or private-banking families; 6 months; Rs 30–90 lakh [EST]; KPIs: activation, requests per active, CSAT/NPS, incremental card spend on partner categories; partner-funded benefits reduce the issuer's net cost.
- *Contract points:* insist on a minimum fee and a 2–3-year term at scale-up; carve-outs for your own private membership; data-processing agreement under DPDP; call recording and audit rights as standard.

### 4.3 Risks and mitigations (B2B channel)

| Risk | Why it is real | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Concentration | DreamFolks: ~90% of revenue in one benefit type with a few issuers; lost half its revenue in a year [E: Block 12] | Cap any client at 30–40% of revenue; diversify segments; minimum fees |
| Low per-member fees | Ten earns ~GBP 160–180 net per *active* member and pennies per eligible [E-derived]; Indian mass desks cost issuers Rs 30–150 per eligible card per year [EST] | Sell overlays for narrow rich segments, seat-based retainers, and partner-funded benefits; avoid PMPM on mass books |

## 5. Case studies

### 5.1 How Ten won HSBC (and what it teaches)

- **Facts in evidence:** in March 2018 Ten announced it would provide global concierge services to HSBC's exclusive wealth proposition (HSBC Jade) [E: https://tenlifestylegroup.com/2018/03/15/ten-provide-services-to-hsbc-wealth-proposition/]. Ten lists HSBC, Coutts, RBC and Westpac among 50+ corporate clients [E: Block 4/10]. Ten later won a multi-year contract with an existing banking client for a fully digital programme for ~1 million premium clients in Europe [E: Block 4] and expanded its DNB (Norway) contract [E: Block 4].
- **Why Ten won [K-med, analyst synthesis]:** (1) **multi-market delivery** — HSBC needed one concierge standard across Hong Kong, Singapore, UK, UAE, Canada, the US and mainland China; Ten had 20+ service locations and a single platform [E for locations]; (2) **a technology platform** the bank could integrate (app/web, content, dining and travel inventory), funded by the November 2017 IPO proceeds [K-med]; (3) **references** — Coutts (RBS) had used Ten since the late 2000s [K-low] [VERIFY]; (4) **a proposition structure** — Jade was a top-tier overlay (clients with ≥ US$1m with HSBC) above Premier, exactly the "private-tier overlay" recommended above [K-med]; (5) **willingness to sign multi-year with fixed fees** that let Ten invest ahead of revenue.
- **What it cost Ten:** client concentration (HSBC became a material share of revenue), heavy technology capex, and margin pressure until scale [K-med] [VERIFY in annual reports 2018–2021].
- **Lesson for India:** the bank does not buy "luxury"; it buys a **differentiated tier it can market, one vendor across geographies, integration, and reporting**. An Indian entrant can offer one thing Ten cannot: Indian cultural depth and on-ground presence in the India–Gulf–UK corridor for global Indian clients of HSBC/StanChart/DBS/Citi-successor Axis [EST].

### 5.2 How Aspire / International SOS and Les Concierges won Indian banks

- **Aspire:** International SOS already sold **medical and travel assistance** to Indian card issuers and insurers; concierge was added as an adjacent "loyalty services" line and rebranded Aspire in 2013 [E: Block 6]. In India (since 2014) Aspire serves banks, auto, real estate and hospitality players [E]. Its HDFC Infinia desk is documented in HDFC's T&C as International SOS [E: Block 9]; HSBC Visa Infinite's "Infinite Butler" runs on Aspire's domain [E: Block 6]. The likely winning mechanics [K-med]: (a) **bundling assistance + concierge** under one vendor with global 24/7 centres; (b) **card-network programmes** (Visa/Mastercard premium concierge in APAC) that made Aspire the default behind many issuers; (c) global references and infosec maturity that pass bank procurement.
- **Les Concierges:** grew from corporate onsite desks (IT campuses) in Bengaluru from 1998 and added bank-card and BPO work; today Rs 8.24 Cr revenue, 84 staff [E: Block 5]. Lesson: domestic B2B concierge sold as a service contract stays small and priced like BPO; the brand never reached the consumer.
- **Read-across:** to displace Aspire on a mass desk you would need infosec maturity, 24/7 multilingual capacity and price — a losing fight. To *sit above* Aspire you need what it lacks: lifestyle managers, luxury supply, events and a brand the bank's top clients recognise.

### 5.3 RedBeryl × D'MONDE and Ellidore; Indulge × EO/YPO/Campden

- **RedBeryl** (founded 2023 by Manoj Adlakha, ex-American Express India; "thirty years at American Express and Visa") [E: competitive-landscape Block 3; initial-scouting Block 1] sells a benefits bundle (Red Card: ~Rs 5 lakh joining + Rs 1.9 lakh per year; "benefits worth Rs 37 lakh"; 200+ curated partnerships; 3,000 global clubs; 1,800 Michelin restaurants; 85 Indian cities, 25 international destinations) [E: Block 8] [VERIFY]. It announced an exclusive partnership with **D'MONDE Members Club** (Mumbai) and a global expansion partnership with **Ellidore** (September 2024, ANI/Business Standard) [E: initial-scouting Block 1; Block 3, https://aninews.in/news/business/redberyl8482-global-expansion-through-ellidore-partnership20240918153230/]. Terms of both are **unknown**; the structure reads as reciprocal member benefits and international fulfilment rather than paid white-label [EST]. The "3,000 clubs / 1,800 Michelin restaurants" scale suggests a licensed reciprocal-club and dining network rather than owned relationships [inference, K-low] [VERIFY].
- **Indulge Global** lists strategic alliances with EO and YPO chapters and the Campden Group (family-office network) on its Growth Stage page [E: model-ops Block 56, https://indulge.global/growthstage]; a third-party summary claims it "partners with banks, wealth managers and family offices" but this is unsourced [E: onendf.com; Indulge notes A rate it L]. No bank, card, developer, hotel or club contract was found [E: Indulge notes B]. Lesson: the Indian independents use B2B as **distribution** (chapter alliances, club tie-ups), not as **contracted revenue** — the contracted white-label slot is still empty.

### 5.4 Amex's partner-funded benefits model

- **Mechanics [K-high]:** Amex charges high card fees (India Platinum Charge ~Rs 60,000 + GST [K-med] [VERIFY]; Centurion by invitation with reported joining/annual fees in the low lakhs [K-low] [VERIFY]) and delivers a benefits bundle in which **partners fund a large share**: hotel programmes grant elite status and Fine Hotels + Resorts perks (breakfast, upgrades, credits) because Platinum members are high spenders and hotels pay commission on bookings; airlines and retailers fund credits and offers to acquire spend; dining access came in-house via the Resy acquisition (2019) [K-high]; lounge networks are partly owned (Centurion Lounges) and partly bought (Priority Pass) [K-high]. In Amex's 10-K the net cost appears under "Card Member services" after partner contributions, and the revenue engine is discount (merchant) revenue plus net card fees [K-med] [VERIFY in American Express Company Form 10-K, Item 7 segment results].
- **India specifics:** Platinum Charge benefits have included Taj Epicure/Preferred status, Marriott Bonvoy Gold, Hilton Honors Gold and airport lounge access, with Amex's own Platinum Concierge [K-med] [VERIFY current list on americanexpress.com/in]. Centurion India has dedicated relationship managers [K-med].
- **Lesson for a concierge:** build a **partner-funded benefits book** (hotels, jets, clubs, retailers) whose retail value is marketed to members and B2B clients while the cash cost is borne by suppliers wanting access — RedBeryl's "Rs 37 lakh of benefits" is this model in Indian dress [E: Block 8]. In B2B pitches, partner funding is what lets you tell a bank "your net cost is near zero".

### 5.5 DreamFolks (cautionary) and Velocity Black (the exit comp)

- **DreamFolks:** see 2.4 — an aggregator earning single-digit margins on pass-through, with short, non-exclusive, no-minimum contracts, lost ~49% of revenue and ~82% of profit in FY26 when issuers re-priced lounge access [E: Block 12].
- **Velocity Black:** a UK/US B2C digital luxury concierge acquired by Capital One in 2023 (reported ~US$300m) [K-med] [VERIFY] — proof that a card issuer will buy a concierge brand to own the premium-member relationship. For an Indian founder this is the strategic exit thesis: build the brand and member base that HDFC/ICICI/Axis/SBI Card/Amex or a wealth manager would rather buy than build.

## 7. Confidence and gaps

**Established (E or K-high):** HDFC Infinia's concierge is provided by International SOS/Aspire; HSBC India's Visa Infinite concierge is Aspire; Aspire has operated in India since 2014 across banks, auto, real estate and hospitality; Ten's FY2024 net revenue, corporate/supplier split and active-member count; Ten's HSBC Jade (2018), DNB and ~1m-client digital contract announcements; DreamFolks' FY25/FY26 figures and the 2025 issuer exits; Les Concierges' FY25 revenue and headcount; RedBeryl's D'MONDE and Ellidore partnerships and Red Card price points; Indulge's EO/YPO/Campden alliances and the absence of verified bank/developer contracts; the general structure of Indian concierge T&Cs; the Amex partner-funded benefits mechanism.

**Probable but unverified (K-med):** Aspire's role behind Visa/Mastercard premium concierge in APAC; Ten's contract-size categories, 3–5-year terms and fixed-plus-variable fees; DreamFolks' ~11–14% gross margin and concentration; Indian bank procurement steps and durations; SLA norms; per-request and PMPM price bands; developer and wealth-manager buying behaviour; luxury-car volumes; perquisite-tax and TDS treatment.

**Low confidence / unknown:** the concierge operators for ICICI, Axis, SBI Card, Kotak, IndusInd, Yes, IDFC First, StanChart, DBS, Amex India, Mastercard/Visa/RuPay India tiers; what any Indian issuer pays per card or per request; Aspire India's revenue and clients beyond HDFC/HSBC; Les Concierges' current bank clients; Ten's FY2025 figures and HSBC concentration; the commercial terms of RedBeryl–D'MONDE/Ellidore; whether any developer currently contracts a third-party lifestyle concierge; wealth managers' client-count tiers and CX budgets; Amex India Centurion fees and India lifestyle sub-vendors; Velocity Black's exact deal value; the medical-value-travel market size.
