# Walk, breathe, plan: skip the brain games

Across roughly 60 meta-analyses, replications and field trials reviewed for this report, the interventions that reliably survive scrutiny for a 2 to 15 minute work break are unglamorous: **slow breathing at about six breaths per minute for at least five minutes** (subjective stress g ≈ -0.35 across 12 RCTs, with a reliable vagal HRV response during the session), **a short walk** (vigor and mood in a randomised crossover; divergent-thinking gains replicated across at least four labs plus a 2022 meta-analysis), **a written if-then plan for the first task** (d = 0.65 across 94 studies), **recorded progress against a specific target** (d = 0.40 across 138 studies), and **a scheduled rather than self-chosen break** that is genuine disengagement rather than a switch to email. Micro-breaks of ten minutes or less raise vigor and cut fatigue by about a third of a standard deviation but do **not** measurably raise performance on demanding work (d = 0.16, non-significant), so the honest promise of a "Break" section is recovery and fewer stalled starts, not output. Creative gains are real but modest: incubation with a light filler task adds d ≈ 0.29, positive activating mood adds r ≈ .15 to d ≈ .52 versus neutral, and simply telling people to be unusual and clever raises rated originality with β ≈ .47; the famous mind-wandering, boredom, blue-wall, coffee-shop-noise and "literally think outside the box" effects are single-lab or have failed replication. For engagement, the field evidence favours small-squad comparison with weekly resets, forgiving workday streaks, informational (not prize-linked) points, kudos, cue-anchored reminders that fade, and one shared daily puzzle, while individual leaderboards, unforgiving streaks, contingent rewards, variable-ratio rewards and scarcity timers either demotivate or cross into dark patterns. Commercial brain training transfers to nothing beyond the trained task (far-transfer g = 0.001 after bias correction; Lumosity paid $2 million to the FTC), so no game in the product should claim to improve memory, focus or productivity. Three widely cited findings are now retracted or failed (Ariely & Wertenbroch's self-imposed deadlines, Brooks et al.'s "rituals reduce anxiety", Ramirez & Beilock's pre-test worry writing) and should not appear in any copy. The report closes with a ranked, protocol-level build list for a 10 to 15 person studio.

A note on evidence access: the researchers' publisher fetches were largely blocked, so most numbers below were confirmed from abstracts and search-result excerpts rather than full texts. Every figure carries its source; items the notes flagged as unverifiable were left out. Grades used throughout: **A** = meta-analysis or independent multi-lab replication; **B** = one adequately powered RCT or multi-experiment paper without independent replication; **C** = single small study, observational data, or inference from mechanisms; **F** = failed replication, retraction, or no evidence.

## Six slow breaths a minute is the only reset with meta-analytic backing

Among all the breathing protocols now circulating, only one has convergent evidence at two levels. Laborde and colleagues' 2022 meta-analysis found that voluntary slow breathing raises vagally mediated heart-rate variability **during** the practice (RMSSD g = 0.53, LF power g = 1.49) with a small carry-over immediately afterwards ([Solent University record](https://pure.solent.ac.uk/en/publications/effects-of-voluntary-slow-breathing-on-heart-rate-and-heart-rate-/)), and Fincham's 2023 meta-analysis of breathwork RCTs found a small-to-medium reduction in self-reported stress (**12 RCTs, N = 785, g = -0.35**, 95% CI -0.55 to -0.14), with similar effects on anxiety (g = -0.32) and depressive symptoms (g = -0.40) ([Scientific Reports 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-022-27247-y)). The resonance band where the baroreflex amplifies these effects lies between 4.5 and 6.5 breaths per minute ([Lehrer et al. 2020](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/341254440_Heart_Rate_Variability_Biofeedback_Improves_Emotional_and_Physical_Health_and_Performance_A_Systematic_Review_and_Meta_Analysis)), and the HRV-biofeedback meta-analysis by Goessl, Curtiss and Hofmann (24 studies, g = 0.83 versus control) found efficacy was not moderated by number of sessions, which argues that a paced-breathing timer without a sensor captures most of the practical value ([Goessl et al. 2017](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/316802722_The_effect_of_heart_rate_variability_biofeedback_training_on_stress_and_anxiety_a_meta-analysis)). The dose matters: Bentley's 2023 systematic review of 58 studies found that the ineffective interventions were disproportionately those **shorter than five minutes**, fast-paced, interrupted, or performed standing ([Bentley et al. 2023](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Breathing-Practices-for-Stress-and-Anxiety-of-Based-Bentley-D%E2%80%99Andrea-Penna/b9ed440871b48c903451b4807b4a5c475cd643cc)). This single finding rules out the 30 to 60 second "breath reset" that most productivity apps ship.

The more famous protocols sit a rung lower. Cyclic sighing rests on one Stanford RCT (n = 108, 5 min/day for 28 days) in which it produced the largest rise in positive affect and beat mindfulness meditation but was **not** significantly better than box breathing or cyclic hyperventilation ([protocol.us summary](https://www.protocol.us/blog/cyclic-sighing-evidence)); as of 2025 no independent lab has replicated it, and the technique was popularised by its own lead author ([AASP blog 2025](https://appliedsportpsych.org/blog/2025/06/the-commodification-of-breath-parsing-fact-from-fiction-in-the-gilded-age-of-breathwork-and-breathing-influencers/)). Box breathing has one recent RCT (n = 66) showing that both it and prolonged exhalation blunted the heart-rate, state-anxiety and salivary alpha-amylase rise to a social stress test when practised beforehand ([PMC13321280](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13321280/)), which positions it as a pre-pitch buffer rather than a general reset; prolonged exhalation beat box breathing on Stroop performance under pressure in an earlier comparison ([Röttger et al. 2021](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/343451194_The_Effectiveness_of_Combat_Tactical_Breathing_as_Compared_with_Prolonged_Exhalation)). The 4-7-8 pattern has been tested only in multi-week clinical trials on tinnitus and sleep, never acutely in healthy workers ([PMC12895279](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12895279/)).

The break itself, independent of content, buys mood rather than output. Albulescu's 2022 meta-analysis of micro-breaks (22 samples, N = 2,335, breaks of ten minutes or less) found **vigor d = 0.36 and fatigue d = 0.35**, but overall performance d = 0.16 and non-significant, with performance gains confined to low-demand tasks and growing with break length ([PMC9432722](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9432722/)). What fills the break does matter in one specific way: in Helton and Russell's "Rest is best" experiments, post-break vigilance was highest after passive rest and lowest after continuous work, while switching to a different cognitive task during the break helped less than resting ([Helton & Russell 2015](https://interruptions.net/literature/Helton-Cognition15.pdf)). The design implication is that a break spent on Slack or email is not a break. Scheduling also beats intuition: Biwer's comparison of self-regulated, 24/6 and 12/3 break schedules found no difference in task completion, but systematic breakers had better mood and finished in less time, while self-regulators ran longer sessions and reported more fatigue and lower concentration ([Biwer et al. 2023](https://bpspsychub.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/bjep.12593)). Hunter and Wu's analysis of roughly 1,000 workday breaks adds that breaks taken earlier in the day and spent on **preferred** activities recovered more energy, and that frequent short breaks beat infrequent ones ([Hunter & Wu 2016](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Give-me-a-better-break:-Choosing-workday-break-to-Hunter-Wu/eac12e8544efff04a9697e1e0b47f017eee8fb53)).

Movement is the best-supported break content for energy. In Bergouignan's randomised crossover (30 sedentary adults, three 6-hour conditions), **six hourly 5-minute walks** raised energy and vigor and, unlike a single 30-minute morning walk, also improved mood, lowered end-of-day fatigue and reduced food cravings with no cost to cognition ([PMC5094084](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5094084/)); ten minutes of easy stair walking raised vigor more than a 50 mg caffeine capsule in sleep-deprived young women, though the effect faded within an hour ([UGA news](https://news.uga.edu/stairs-more-energy-research/)). Measurable executive-function gains need more: Chang's meta-analysis of acute exercise found a minimum of about 11 minutes, with no cognitive improvement for bouts of ten minutes or less ([PMC8992970](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8992970/)). So a five-minute walk is an energy tool and a twenty-minute lunchtime walk is a cognition tool, and the product should not conflate them.

Two other options deserve a conditional place. A **ten-minute nap** is the best-supported short dose: in Brooks and Lack's comparison of 5, 10, 20 and 30 minute naps after restricted sleep, the 10-minute nap improved sleepiness, fatigue, vigor and cognitive performance immediately, with some gains lasting up to 155 minutes, whereas 5 minutes did little and 30 minutes produced sleep inertia with benefits only 35 to 95 minutes later ([PubMed 16796222](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16796222/)). And a five-minute **easy casual game** restored affect better than guided relaxation and much better than silent rest in Rupp's fatigue experiment (n = 66), where silent rest left people less engaged and more worried ([ScienceDaily on Rupp 2017](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2017/07/170725100704.htm)); the caveat from Pine's adolescent study is that Tetris did **not** reduce stress when difficulty exceeded skill ([Pine et al. 2020](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/2055207620949391)), so the game must be easy and without a fail state.

Several popular resets should be labelled honestly or dropped. Brief mindfulness reduces negative affect by g = 0.21 across 65 RCTs, but the effect falls to **g = 0.04 after publication-bias correction** ([Schumer et al. 2018](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Brief-Mindfulness-Training-for-Negative-A-Review-Schumer-Lindsay/18e7d1b93185a53d7f37a4a39c75e3c4c0f690fc)); nature exposure produces small, test-specific attention effects (around 0.2 to 0.3) that are most consistent at 30 minutes or longer ([2025 meta-analysis](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S027249442500115X)), and a well-powered test of nature images and sounds (n = 202) found no attention restoration at all, only higher relaxation ratings ([Emfield & Neider 2014](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2014.00548/full)), which makes the celebrated 40-second green-roof study a single unreplicated result ([Lee et al. 2015](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/40-second-green-roof-views-sustain-attention:-The-Lee-Williams/662dbfb1a298df2c35a379bb5c07ac437e666753)). The 20-20-20 eye rule did not change reading speed, accuracy or symptoms when the interval was varied in a lab trial ([Johnson & Rosenfield 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36473088/)). Power posing failed a registered replication (n = 200) and was disavowed by its first author; ego depletion came out at **d = 0.04** in a multi-lab preregistered replication ([Hagger et al. 2016](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1745691616652873)); binaural beats show a meta-analytic g = 0.45 ([Garcia-Argibay 2019](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00426-018-1066-8)) but the evidence that they entrain anything is "inconclusive at best" ([Ingendoh et al. 2023](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0286023)), so any benefit is relaxation that calm music provides without the marketing. The 90-minute ultradian focus cycle is a hypothesis from sleep research with no robust waking-performance evidence ([BRAC overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basic_rest%E2%80%93activity_cycle)), and DeskTime's 52/17 ratio is observational app data that its own 2021 re-analysis revised to 112/26 ([DeskTime blog](https://desktime.com/blog/52-17-updated/)).

## Walking and incubation lift ideas; mind-wandering and boredom do not

The creativity literature divides cleanly into two modes, and almost every replicated lever helps one while leaving the other alone or harming it. Divergent generation (listing many unusual ideas) responds to movement, positive activating mood, explicit instructions and incubation; convergent insight (finding the one answer) responds to quiet, caffeine and sleep. A Break section that ignores this split will ship a music track into a copy-editing session and a brainstorm into a debugging one.

Walking is the clearest divergent lever. Oppezzo and Schwartz's four experiments (176 participants) found that walking on an indoor treadmill facing a wall increased divergent-thinking output for **81% of participants** versus sitting, that walking outdoors produced more novel, high-quality analogies for 100% of participants, and that walking did **not** help convergent problems on the Remote Associates Test ([APA PDF](https://www.apa.org/pubs/journals/releases/xlm-a0036577.pdf)). Independent labs have since confirmed the direction (Zhou et al. 2017; Kuo & Yeh 2016, where free walking beat a prescribed rectangular path ([Frontiers 2016](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2016.01580/full))), and Murali and Händel showed that **restricting movement** (fixed path or fixed gaze) impaired divergent thinking whether walking or sitting ([Springer 2022](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00426-021-01636-w)); a 2022 multilevel meta-analysis concludes that both acute and chronic physical activity increase creative ideation ([Rominger et al. 2022](https://sportsmedicine-open.springeropen.com/articles/10.1186/s40798-022-00444-9)). The active ingredient appears to be unrestricted movement, so the protocol should be "wander, no route, no screen", and because effects were measured during and immediately after walking, the idea sprint must follow within minutes.

Incubation works, but not for the reason popular accounts give. Sio and Ormerod's meta-analysis of 117 studies found a positive effect of taking a break before returning to a creative problem (**d = 0.29**), and the benefit was larger when the break was filled with an undemanding task than with a demanding task or no task at all ([Lancaster repository](https://research.lancaster-university.uk/en/publications/does-incubation-enhance-problem-solving-a-meta-analytic-review/)); Gilhooly's group found the effect held and was even stronger when the incubation period came **immediately** after reading the problem, before any attempt to solve it ([Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Don%E2%80%99t-wait-to-incubate:-Immediate-versus-delayed-in-Gilhooly-Georgiou/5a9d9d5c7702843869749dacbfb29467d2d66a13)). Baird et al.'s 2012 claim that mind-wandering during the filler is the mechanism has not survived: a 2023 review states that "numerous researchers have failed to observe a reliable association between mind wandering and post-incubation creativity" ([PMC10414778](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10414778/)), with Steindorf et al. 2021 and van Rooij et al. 2024 among the failures ([Wiley 2024](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/jocb.640)). The related "being bored makes you creative" result (copying phone numbers for 15 minutes) had a partial replication that "clearly failed" once the control group was given an interesting rather than empty task ([Nettinga et al.](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/371242526_Exploring_the_Relation_between_State_and_Trait_Boredom_and_Various_Measures_of_Creativity)); the original result is best read as an incubation effect in disguise. The product should therefore offer a pleasant low-demand filler and never a deliberately boring one.

The cheapest replicated lever is an instruction. Telling people to produce ideas that are unusual, clever and uncommon raised rated creativity with a standardised **β = .47** while lowering fluency (about two fewer uses), one of the oldest findings in the field and confirmed by a 2023 meta-analysis ([Nusbaum, Silvia & Beaty 2014](https://bpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/sites.psu.edu/dist/c/122043/files/2020/08/2014b_Nusbaum_PACA.pdf); [2023 meta-analysis](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0023969023000760)). Because quantity and originality trade off, a two-phase sprint (quantity first, then the explicit "be creative" definition) captures both. Mood is the next lever: Baas, De Dreu and Nijstad's synthesis of 102 effect sizes found positive mood beats neutral (r = .15), with the gain concentrated in **activating** positive states such as happiness rather than relaxed calm, and with fear and anxiety reducing flexibility ([RUG portal](https://research.rug.nl/en/publications/a-meta-analysis-of-25-years-of-mood-creativity-research-hedonic-t/)); Davis's independent meta-analysis put the positive-versus-neutral effect at d = .52 ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S074959780800054X)). Happy, high-arousal music during a divergent task beat silence in one study ([Ritter & Ferguson 2017](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article/file?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0182210&type=printable)), but background music, with or without lyrics, **impaired** compound-remote-associate solving in another lab ([Threadgold et al. 2019](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/acp.3532)): music belongs before generation and nowhere near convergent work. Caffeine shows the mirror image: 200 mg improved convergent problem solving and not divergent thinking in a double-blind study of 88 volunteers ([Zabelina & Silvia 2020](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/339442376_Percolating_ideas_The_effects_of_caffeine_on_creative_thinking_and_problem_solving)).

Group format matters more than any individual trick. Mullen, Johnson and Salas's meta-analysis of 20 studies and more than 800 teams found face-to-face brainstorming groups significantly **less** productive than the same number of people working alone, on both quantity and quality ([Purdue review](https://docs.lib.purdue.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1093&context=jps)), chiefly because of production blocking ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0022103103000404)). Silent brainwriting removes the block: Paulus and Yang found round-robin written ideation beat nominal groups ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228210918_Better_than_brainstorming_Potential_contextual_boundary_conditions_to_brainwriting_for_idea_generation_in_organizations)), and hybrid 6-3-5 with sketches outperformed nominal groups on idea count at equal quality ([Springer chapter](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/978-0-85729-224-7_22.pdf)). DeRosa's meta-analysis of electronic brainstorming found it beats face-to-face but does not beat nominal groups below about eight members ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0747563205000506)), which for a 10 to 15 person agency means silent-first, then share. Imposed constraints are a smaller but intriguing lever: writing rhymes that had to include a given noun raised rated creativity and the gain carried over into subsequent unconstrained writing ([Haught-Tromp 2017](https://www.cct.umb.edu/630/files/HaughtTromp2017-GreenEggsandHam.pdf)). SCAMPER has mixed education-sector evidence (fluency up, originality not always), and "worst possible idea", Six Thinking Hats and Design Thinking as methods have no controlled evidence in the notes.

Two further levers carry grade B or C and should ship as experimental. Open-monitoring meditation (wide, receptive attention, no breath anchor) improved divergent thinking in a single small within-subject study while focused-attention meditation did not ([Colzato et al. 2012](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2012.00116/pdf)); the broader mindfulness-creativity correlation is r = .22 across 20 samples with high heterogeneity ([Lebuda et al. 2016](https://mocca.uark.edu/home/publications/)). Most meditation apps default to breath focus, which is precisely the mode the evidence does not link to idea generation. Sleep onset is the most striking single result: in Lacaux's study (N = 103) a single minute of N1 sleep during a 20-minute reclined break made participants about **2.7 times** more likely to discover a hidden rule than staying awake and about 5.8 times more likely than those who slipped into N2 ([HAL record](https://hal.science/hal-03474706)), echoing the overnight-sleep result of roughly 60% versus 22% rule discovery in Wagner et al. ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/8908590_Sleep_inspires_insight)); but the benefit vanished in deeper sleep, which cannot be seen without EEG, and sleep does not help classical insight problems ([PMC5834438](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5834438/)). An "Edison nap" with an object in hand and a hard 20-minute alarm is a low-cost lottery, not a routine.

The environment hacks mostly fail. The 70 dB coffee-shop-noise effect has no published replication ([Mehta, Zhu & Cheema 2012](https://www.jstor.org/stable/10.1086/665048)); the blue-versus-red colour effect from the same lab has a published failed replication and "no studies since have replicated" it ([Steele 2014](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13423-013-0548-3)); ceiling height is one paper; dim light (150 lux) is one lab's six-experiment series ([Steidle & Werth 2013](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/242023319_Freedom_from_constraints_Darkness_and_dim_illumination_promote_creativity)); psychological-distance prompts trace to Förster, whose work was found manipulated and partly retracted ([retraction record](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/258189205_RETRACTED_Sense_Creative_The_Impact_of_Global_and_Local_Vision_Hearing_Touching_Tasting_and_Smelling_on_Creative_and_Analytic_Thought)); and "sit outside a cardboard box" is a single-lab series with reviewers noting small samples and no replication ([PMC6818493](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6818493)). Nature is the exception: four days of device-free backcountry hiking raised Remote Associates scores about 50% ([Atchley et al. 2012](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0051474)), and brief nature video raised divergent originality in a 2025 online experiment, most for people who found the footage fascinating ([J. Environmental Psychology 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0272494425001823)). Paired with the attention-restoration nulls above, nature footage is a creativity and mood lever, not a focus lever.

Finally, creativity training as a multi-session programme works (Scott, Leritz and Mumford: 70 studies, overall effect about 0.64 to 0.68, transfer tasks 0.51, largest for cognitively framed technique training with realistic exercises ([open PDF](http://www.psychologie.uni-mannheim.de/cip/tut/seminare_wittmann/meta_fribourg/sources/Scott_etal_2004_Effectiveness_of_creativity_training.pdf))), while daily Remote Associates practice raises Remote Associates scores with no evidence of transfer ([Frontiers 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.00026/full)). A daily prompt is defensible as technique practice tied to real briefs, and indefensible as a claim that it makes the team more creative.

## If-then plans and recorded progress beat every productivity hack

For follow-through, one two-minute intervention outperforms everything else in the notes. Gollwitzer and Sheeran's meta-analysis of 94 studies (more than 8,000 participants) put implementation intentions, "if [situation], then I will [action]", at **d = 0.65** on goal attainment, helping people start, shield a goal from distraction, and disengage from failing pursuits ([Univ. Konstanz](https://www.socmot.uni-konstanz.de/publications/implementation-intentions-and-goal-achievement-meta-analysis-effects-and-processes)). The literal format carries the effect: in a 557-person randomised comparison, the if-then wording increased fruit and vegetable intake by 0.50 portions versus 0.31 for a looser "global" plan and 0.01 for control ([PubMed 20204996](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20204996/)). The one procrastination-specific experiment found plan-makers were nearly eight times more likely to keep appointments, with no interaction with procrastination level ([Owens et al. 2008](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Overcoming-Procrastination%3A-The-Effect-of-Owens-Bowman/383297028b6836af44b70930951c16f92a30a056)). The elaborated WOOP version (wish, outcome, obstacle, plan) has two meta-analyses behind it, g = 0.336 across 15,907 participants, with interactive delivery (g = 0.465) beating documents (g = 0.277) ([Wang et al. 2021](https://public-pages-files-2025.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.565202/text); [Cross & Sheffield 2019](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/331842483_Mental_Contrasting_for_Health_Behaviour_Change_a_Systematic_Review_and_Meta-analysis_of_Effects_and_Moderator_variables)); it is a reasonable weekly exercise but adds little over a bare if-then for daily use. One caution shapes the social design: Gollwitzer's 2009 experiments found that **identity-related** intentions noticed by others were acted on less, because recognition gave a premature sense of completion ([Psychological Science](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2009.02336.x)), so shared commitments should be behaviours ("draft the brief by 10:00"), never identities.

Monitoring is the second cheap win. Harkin's meta-analysis of 138 studies (19,951 participants) found that prompting people to monitor progress raised goal attainment by **d = 0.40**, about the size of the goal-setting effect itself ([Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Does-monitoring-goal-progress-promote-goal-A-of-the-Harkin-Webb/71c6265bf7a8ded9084ff21a417cd63d6f4119ea)); Locke and Latham's summary of goal-setting meta-analyses puts specific difficult goals over "do your best" at d = 0.42 to 0.80, shrinking to the lower end for complex tasks ([Locke & Latham 2002](https://www-2.rotman.utoronto.ca/facbios/file/09%20-%20Locke%20&%20Latham%202002%20AP.pdf)), which for agency work means specific process targets ("three directions by noon") rather than quality targets. Amabile and Kramer's 12,000-entry diary study, which found progress in meaningful work the strongest driver of inner work life, is correlational and supports surfacing small wins, not claiming they cause better work ([ATD summary](https://www.td.org/content/td-magazine/recognizing-meaningful-progress)).

Offloading works only when it is specific. Masicampo and Baumeister showed that unfulfilled goals caused intrusive thoughts and worse performance on unrelated tasks, and that writing a **specific plan** for the unfinished goal eliminated both even though the goal stayed unfinished ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/51234294_Consider_It_Done_Plan_Making_Can_Eliminate_the_Cognitive_Effects_of_Unfulfilled_Goals)); Scullin's sleep-lab study (n = 57) found a five-minute bedtime to-do list cut sleep onset by about nine minutes, with more specific lists working better ([ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2018/01/180111224021.htm)). Both are single studies, but they converge on one mechanism and they are what "brain dump" and "shutdown ritual" actually have behind them. The Zeigarnik memory effect, by contrast, fails a 2025 meta-analysis, although the urge to resume an interrupted task (the Ovsiankina effect) holds ([Nature HSSC 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-025-05000-w)), which is the real basis for the "start for five minutes" tactic. Leroy and Glomb's "ready-to-resume" note, a brief plan written before switching tasks that reduced attention residue and improved performance on the next task, is single-lab-group evidence but cheap and mechanistically consistent ([UW News](https://www.washington.edu/news/2018/01/16/task-interrupted-a-plan-for-returning-helps-you-move-on/)).

On focus, the product should quote field numbers rather than folklore. Mark's 2005 field study found interrupted information workers took about **25 minutes and 2.3 intervening tasks** to return, not "23 minutes 15 seconds" ([summary](https://www.highperformanceroutines.com/no-task-left-behind-examining-the-nature-of-fragmented-work/)), and the "multitasking costs 40%" line is an APA paraphrase of sub-second lab switch costs ([APA page](https://www.apa.org/topics/research/multitasking)). The smartphone "brain drain" effect failed a preregistered direct replication ([Ruiz Pardo & Minda 2022](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0001691822002323)) and meta-analyses put it between d = -0.02 and -0.14 ([PMC10525686](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10525686/)), so a "phone in another room" rule is not an evidence-based cognitive enhancer. What does have support, at single-study-field level, is reducing fragmentation: batching notifications three times a day reduced stress and improved concentration and mood in a two-week randomised field experiment (n = 237) ([Fitz et al. 2019](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Batching-smartphone-notifications-can-improve-Fitz-Kushlev/ce19cf66bdc6ced56a2f16d2483d97f9dc59b21d)), and limiting email checks to three times a day lowered daily stress in a within-person crossover (n = 124) ([Kushlev & Dunn 2015](https://www.interruptions.net/literature/Kushlev-ComputHumBehav15.pdf)). Heavy media multitaskers show worse, not better, filtering and switching ([Ophir et al. 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19706386/)).

Three retractions and failures reshape the rituals and deadlines space. Ariely and Wertenbroch's 2002 self-imposed-deadlines paper **was retracted in September 2026** after a failed replication (deadline changes had negligible effects on all performance metrics) and a Data Colada analysis concluding the data were tampered with ([Hyndman & Bisin 2026](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/09567976261460772); [Retraction Watch](https://retractionwatch.com/2026/09/03/procrastination-study-duke-dan-ariely-psychological-science-data-colada-tampering-retraction/)). Brooks et al.'s "rituals improve performance by decreasing anxiety" **was retracted in November 2024** over data-integrity anomalies ([retraction notice](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0749597824000694)); what survives for pre-performance routines comes from sport, where a meta-analysis of 112 effect sizes found moderate-to-large experimental effects under pressure (g = 0.70), unmoderated by routine type ([Rupprecht et al. 2021](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/1750984X.2021.1944271)). Ramirez and Beilock's pre-exam expressive writing did not replicate in the Social Sciences Replication Project ([Nature Human Behaviour 2018](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-018-0399-z)), so a "write your worries before the pitch" screen cannot be sold as performance enhancement. With deadlines gone, pre-commitment rests on health-behaviour commitment contracts (low take-up, real effects among those who opt in ([Giné, Karlan & Zinman 2010](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257%2Fapp.2.4.213))), temptation bundling (gym-locked audiobooks raised visits 51% initially ([PubMed 25843979](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25843979/)); teaching the idea to 6,792 people raised weekly workouts 10 to 14% for 17 weeks ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S074959782030385X))), and fresh-start framing (goal behaviours rise after temporal landmarks ([Dai, Milkman & Riis 2014](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2014/06/Dai_Fresh_Start_2014_Mgmt_Sci.pdf)); framing a savings-increase date as a fresh start raised contributions in a 6,082-employee field experiment ([Beshears et al. 2021](https://katherinemilkman.squarespace.com/s/using-fresh-starts-to-nudge-increased-retirement-savings.pdf))). End-of-day reflection has one field experiment (15 minutes of written lessons gave 23% better training-test scores for new call-centre hires ([HBS summary](https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/reflecting-on-work-improves-job-performance))), but no independent replication, and the notes flag unverified co-author data-integrity controversies, so grade it C.

"Eat the frog" has no productivity trial; the only experimental work shows hard-first ordering builds self-efficacy while people wrongly prefer easy-first ([Schroeder et al. 2020](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0022103120303723)). Chronotype scheduling is weak: more than 80% of studies show no chronotype main effect and a 2023 latent-variable study found no general synchrony boost ([Chronobiology International 2025](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/07420528.2025.2490495); [Collabra 2023](https://online.ucpress.edu/collabra/article/9/1/88337/197502/The-Interplay-of-Time-of-day-and-Chronotype)). The lever that dwarfs all of these is sleep: 14 nights of 6 hours in bed produced deficits equal to one or two nights of total deprivation while subjective sleepiness plateaued, meaning people stop noticing their own impairment ([Van Dongen et al. 2003](https://www.med.upenn.edu/uep/assets/user-content/documents/VanDongen2003CumulativeCost.pdf)), and short-term sleep loss hits simple-attention lapses hardest (g = 0.78) while leaving reasoning accuracy untouched ([Lim & Dinges 2010](https://www.med.upenn.edu/uep/assets/user-content/documents/LimDinges2010MetaAnalysis.pdf)). A non-judgemental "hours slept" gate that steers task choice is better founded than any timing hack. Because procrastination is driven by mood repair, with self-compassion mediating the procrastination-stress link ([Sirois 2014](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/91791/1/ProcrastinationFINAL.pdf)) and self-forgiveness after one slip predicting less procrastination before the next exam ([Wohl et al. 2010](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/222302476_I_forgive_myself_now_I_can_study_How_self-forgiveness_for_procrastinating_can_reduce_future_procrastination)), the reset screen after a missed target must forgive rather than scold.

## Squad comparison and forgiving streaks engage; brain training transfers nothing

Habit formation takes longer than the product cycle expects. In Lally's field study, automaticity plateaued after a **median of 66 days with a range of 18 to 254**, and missing a single day did not materially reduce the chance of forming the habit ([Lally interview](https://www.surrey.ac.uk/news/does-it-really-take-66-days-form-habit-we-asked-expert-dr-pippa-lally); [Steps.org summary](https://steps.org/sources/lally-2010-habit-formation/)). Cue placement matters: people told to floss **after** brushing formed stronger habits than those told to floss before, still visible at eight months ([Judah et al. 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22989272/)), and a UCL review of habit apps found reminders supported repetition but **hindered** habit formation because users attached the behaviour to the notification rather than to a context ([Stawarz et al. 2015](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/1468224/)). Fogg's "85% adherence" and Clear's "habit stacking" have no peer-reviewed tests of their own; what they borrow from is the implementation-intention and context-cue literature. The product should therefore let each person pick a routine boundary as the cue, plan for a ten-week on-ramp, and fade reminders after two weeks.

Streaks are a double-edged scaffold. Silverman and Barasch found that logged streaks of three or more performances become a goal in themselves and raise repetition, but a broken streak lowers subsequent engagement, more so when the person blames themselves, and less so when the streak can be **repaired** ([JCR abstract](https://academic.oup.com/jcr/article-abstract/49/6/1095/6623414)). Duolingo's non-peer-reviewed experiments point the same way (leniency raised engagement; a streak freeze cut churn 21% among at-risk users ([Trophy.so summary](https://trophy.so/blog/the-psychology-of-streaks-how-sylvi-weaponized-duolingos-best-feature-against-them))), while a survey of 2,483 adolescents linked Snapchat streaks to FOMO and problematic use ([Computers in Human Behavior Reports 2023](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2772503023000476)). The defensible version counts workdays only, repairs one miss per week automatically, and attributes misses to circumstances in copy.

Points and leaderboards do what the Self-Determination literature predicts. Deci, Koestner and Ryan's meta-analysis of 128 experiments found expected tangible rewards undermined intrinsic motivation (d = -0.36, performance-contingent d = -0.28), unexpected rewards were neutral (d = 0.01), and positive verbal feedback **enhanced** it (d = 0.33) ([Deci et al. 1999](https://home.ubalt.edu/tmitch/642/articles%20syllabus/Deci%20Koestner%20Ryan%20meta%20IM%20psy%20bull%2099.pdf)); Cerasoli's 183-study meta-analysis adds that incentives predict quantity while intrinsic motivation predicts quality, and that performance-tied incentives crowd out the latter ([PubMed 24491020](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24491020/)). In practice, points, levels and leaderboards raised output quantity without changing intrinsic motivation in Mekler's experiment ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/281539797_Towards_understanding_the_effects_of_individual_gamification_elements_on_intrinsic_motivation_and_performance)); a semester-long gamified course with badges, a leaderboard and coins **lowered** intrinsic motivation and, through it, final grades ([Hanus & Fox 2015](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0360131514002000)); and a leaderboard on a 12-minute ideation task acted like an impossible assigned goal, which the authors warn narrows effort ([Landers et al.](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/281325758_Gamification_of_task_performance_with_leaderboards_A_goal_setting_experiment)). Leaderboard position drives competence satisfaction at the top and frustration at the bottom ([Internet Research](https://www.emerald.com/intr/article/33/7/1/178330/How-leaderboard-positions-shape-our-motivation-the)), so a 12-person studio with a permanent bottom rank is a textbook demotivation design.

Small-group comparison is the social lever with field-RCT backing. In STEP UP (Deloitte employees, 24 weeks plus follow-up), the competition arm (clusters of three with a weekly leaderboard email) added 920 steps per day during the intervention versus 689 for support and 637 for collaboration, and **only competition persisted** after the game ended (+569) ([Penn Today](https://penntoday.upenn.edu/news/competition-works-better-support-or-collaboration-increase-daily-step-counts)); in BE FIT, family-level points with loss framing lifted step-goal achievement to 53% versus 32% ([JAMA Internal Medicine 2017](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/2655242)). Zhang's 790-student trial found attendance 90% higher with social comparison than without, while social support alone did nothing (p = 0.68) ([PubMed 27617191](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27617191/)). Low-stakes affirmation also helps: runners who received Strava kudos subsequently ran more often in a 329-person longitudinal study ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0378873322000909)). The synthesis for a 10 to 15 person team is two or three squads of three to four, a weekly squad ranking with a Monday reset, and personal views that show only self-relative progress.

The ethical line is clear from the gaming literature. Variable-ratio rewards are the mechanism behind loot boxes, whose spending correlates with problem-gambling severity in a large survey and a 2019 replication ([Zendle & Cairns 2018](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0206767); [PMC6405116](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6405116/)), and HCI taxonomies classify "play by appointment", grinding, artificial scarcity and obligating friends as dark patterns that create frustration, coercion or regret without consent ([CHI EA 2022](https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3491101.3519837); [Level Up or Game Over](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.05039)). The product should have no random reward containers, no countdown scarcity, no status-loss notifications, no mechanics that require teammates to act, and a one-click way to hide streaks, points and rankings without penalty. Real-world retention makes the stakes concrete: across popular mental-health apps, median day-15 retention was 3.9% and day-30 retention 3.3%, with breathing apps at 0.0% and peer-support (8.9%) and tracker (6.1%) formats retaining best ([Baumel et al. 2019](https://www.jmir.org/2019/9/e14567/PDF)). The apps that do get used deliver small effects (perceived stress g = 0.35 across 22 RCTs ([Health Psychology Review 2024](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/17437199.2024.2379784)); mindfulness apps g = 0.46 on stress at post-test ([Gál et al. 2021](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/344672612_The_efficacy_of_mindfulness_meditation_apps_in_enhancing_users'_well-being_and_mental_health_related_outcomes_a_meta-analysis_of_randomized_controlled_trials))), and the one RCT isolating gamification found a gamified app retained 21% more participants than a CBT journal app while raising resilience ([Litvin et al. 2020](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0237220)). Gamification is an adherence tool, not the source of benefit.

On "brain games" the verdict is settled. Melby-Lervåg, Redick and Hulme's meta-analysis (145 comparisons) found working-memory training produces short-term gains on near-transfer tasks and **no convincing far transfer** to reasoning, reading or arithmetic against treated controls ([PMC4968033](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4968033)); Simons and colleagues' review found extensive evidence for trained-task improvement and little for everyday cognition, with no study meeting all best-practice criteria ([PubMed 27697851](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27697851/)); and Sala and Gobet's second-order meta-analysis put far transfer with active controls at **g = 0.001** ([Collabra 2019](https://pure.fujita-hu.ac.jp/en/publications/near-and-far-transfer-in-cognitive-training-a-second-order-meta-a/)). Lumosity paid $2 million to settle FTC charges that its "10 to 15 minutes, three to four times a week" claims were unsupported, and must now hold competent scientific evidence for any future claim ([FTC press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2016/01/lumosity-pay-2-million-settle-ftc-deceptive-advertising-charges-its-brain-training-program)). The ACTIVE trial's speed-of-processing result (29% lower dementia risk at ten years, non-clinical outcome, no active control ([Edwards et al. 2017](https://alz-journals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1016/j.trci.2017.09.002))) concerns older adults and is irrelevant to a working-age team's product claims. Action video games show g = 0.34 in training studies ([Bediou et al. 2018](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Meta-Analysis-of-Action-Video-Game-Impact-on-and-Bediou-Adams/db92415ee7a5b787ae00c07894187d56aaa6cd2f)) that shrink to minimal after proper bias correction ([Hilgard et al. 2019](https://online.ucpress.edu/collabra/article/5/1/30/113031/Overestimation-of-Action-Game-Training-Effects)); music training (g = 0.06 ([Sala & Gobet 2020](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13421-020-01060-2))) and bilingualism ([Paap et al. 2025](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/13670069231214599)) are null when controlled. What does transfer broadly is **exercise**: g = 0.29 across 36 studies in adults over 50, with 45 to 60 minute moderate sessions ([Northey et al. 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28438770/)), and a year of aerobic walking grew hippocampal volume about 2% while stretching controls lost 1.4% ([ScienceDaily on Erickson 2011](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2011/01/110131153249.htm)). Crosswords have one RCT in mild cognitive impairment where they beat a commercial cognitive-games platform at 12 and 78 weeks ([NEJM Evidence 2022](https://evidence.nejm.org/doi/abs/10.1056/EVIDe2200276)), which justifies word puzzles as the shareable break format with the least embarrassing evidence, not as cognitive protection for a 30-year-old art director. The one measurement worth shipping is a 3-minute Psychomotor Vigilance Test, which with a 355 ms lapse threshold tracked total and partial sleep deprivation as well as the 10-minute version ([Basner et al. 2011](https://www.med.upenn.edu/uep/assets/user-content/documents/Basner2011-ValidityandsensitivityofabriefPVT.pdf)), though a later study questioned its convergent validity ([PMC8885985](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8885985/)); it is a fatigue mirror, never a ranking.

## The build list: twenty-three screens, ranked by evidence

Durations are the tested doses, not guesses. Every protocol assumes the Break section opens with a one-tap "Ready-to-resume" note (rank 9) when it interrupts work, and closes with a return cue.

**1. Reset (paced breathing).** Screen 1: "Sit back, feet flat, hands loose. Breathe through your nose. Follow the circle: in for 4, out for 6." Show an expanding/contracting shape on a 10-second cycle (six breaths per minute; a 5/5 split is an acceptable alternative inside the 4.5 to 6.5 bpm band). Default 5:00 with the stop button hidden for the first 3:00 and a note that sessions under five minutes were the ones found ineffective. Screen 2 (end): "Stay seated for 30 seconds before you stand." Label: "Best-supported stress reset: meta-analyses of RCTs." Do not offer a 1:00 version as a reset; offer it only as a "settle" before the pre-pitch screen.

**2. One If-Then.** Screen 1: "Name the one task you most want to start today (one line)." Screen 2: "Complete this sentence exactly: *If it is [time] and I am [place or right after cue], then I will [first physical action under 5 minutes].*" Enforce the literal structure with three fields. Screen 3 (optional): "*If [the interruption that usually gets me], then I will [specific response].*" Store privately by default; if shared to the squad board, show only the behaviour line, never an identity statement. Show the plan back at the chosen time.

**3. Energise (free walk).** Screen 1: "Walk for 5 to 10 minutes. Any route, your pace, no destination, no phone reading. Stairs count." Start a timer and a return chime. Screen 2 (return): "Energy now? (1 to 5)" charted against the person's own week. Copy: "Short walks reliably lift energy and mood for under an hour; for a sharper brain you need 11 to 20 minutes." Suggest hourly repeats on heavy days.

**4. Walk & dump.** Screen 1 (0:30): show the live generation prompt, e.g. "Give me 25 wrong, weird or wonderful ways to launch [brief]." Screen 2 (6:00 to 8:00): "Walk. Any route, no fixed loop, no screen. Say ideas aloud into a voice memo as they come." Screen 3 (3:00, seated, within five minutes of stopping): "Type everything. Then one more minute to add." Finish with "Star the two most unusual." Copy: "For generating, not deciding. Do not use before a judgement or editing task."

**5. Scheduled break timer.** Settings: 25/5 or 50/10 (both inside the tested range). The break fires automatically; the person does not choose when. Break screen: "Eyes off the screen. No Slack, no email. Pick something you like: walk, music, chat, stretch." Longer break suggested before lunch and after a heavy session ("Big session? Take 10 or more."). The timer calls the Ready-to-resume note before each break. Copy: "Scheduled breaks beat self-chosen ones for mood and efficiency; breaks restore energy, not output." Never show a 90-minute cycle or a 52/17 preset.

**6. Progress ledger.** Screen 1: "Yesterday I moved forward on: ____ (one concrete thing)." Screen 2: "Today's specific target: ____ by ____." Validation requires a deliverable noun and a time. Weekly view shows the person's own ledger; the squad board shows this week's targets as behaviours. Copy: "Recording progress is as powerful as setting the goal."

**7. Shutdown / Close the loops.** Screen 1 (2:00 to 3:00): "Every unfinished thing on your mind, one line each. For each: next concrete step, and when." Items without a next step get "park until [date]". Screen 2 (0:30): "Tomorrow's first task at [time] is ____." Screen 3 (2:00): "What did I learn today? What would I do differently? One thing that went well." Screen 4: a single fixed button, "Shutdown complete", that mutes work notifications until the next workday. Copy: "Specific next steps quiet open loops; this doubles as the bedtime list that sped sleep onset by about nine minutes."

**8. Silent 6-3-5 lite.** Group screen for 3 to 6 people, async-friendly. Round 1 (3:00): "Write 3 ideas on your card. No talking." Rounds 2 to 4 (3:00 each): cards rotate; "Add 3 new ideas that build on or twist what is there. Sketching allowed." Final (2:00): "Star the 2 you would least expect." Show a per-round countdown and the rule "No talking until the end" on every screen. Copy: "Talking brainstorms produce fewer and worse ideas than the same people writing silently."

**9. Ready-to-resume note.** Triggered on any break, meeting or switch. "Where exactly am I in [task]? (one line)", "The next concrete step when I come back is: ____", "Anything I must not forget: ____". Then: "Notifications are batched until [next batch]. Email next at [10:00 / 13:00 / 16:00]." Label as "promising, one research group".

**10. Two-gear sprint.** Screen 1 (3:00): "List as MANY angles as you can for [brief]. Quantity only. Do not judge." Screen 2 (3:00): "Now give ideas that are *unusual, clever, uncommon, surprising*. Quality over quantity; fewer is fine." Optional priming line before screen 2: "For three minutes, think the way an eccentric poet would." Peer "most surprising" votes score originality.

**11. Park it.** Screen 1 (1:00 to 1:30): show the brief. "Read this. Do not start solving. Notice what the real question is." Screen 2 (5:00 to 8:00): a genuinely low-demand, non-verbal, pleasant filler (sort coloured shapes, trace a slow line, simple tile matching), nothing that needs working memory or language. Screen 3 (3:00): "Back to the brief. Ideas, go." Copy: "A light filler beats both resting and hard work for incubation (d ≈ 0.3). Mind-wandering is not the mechanism and we do not claim it is."

**12. Easy casual game.** A 3:00 to 5:00 match-three or sorting game with low difficulty and no fail state; the game ends on the timer. Pair with the daily puzzle for the temptation-bundling effect (the game appears only inside Break). Copy: "Five minutes of an easy game restored mood better than guided relaxation in a lab test. It is a mood tool, not brain training."

**13. Lift then list.** Screen 1 (2:00 to 3:00): an upbeat, high-arousal instrumental track or a short genuinely funny clip, or the prompt "Write three things that went well this week." Screen 2 (3:00): generation sprint on the live problem. Music auto-mutes if the person opens any "Crack it" screen. Copy: "Happy, energetic mood lifts idea generation; background music hurts puzzle-type problem solving."

**14. Batch & schedule.** A settings panel, not a break: notifications delivered at three fixed times, email windows at 10:00, 13:00 and 16:00 by default, both adjustable. Copy: "Batching three times a day reduced stress and improved concentration in a two-week field experiment."

**15. Pre-pitch box breathing.** Screen: "Before the call: in 4, hold 4, out 4, hold 4. Follow the square." 2:00 to 5:00. Optional add-on: a personal fixed routine ("three slow breaths, say the opening line once, check the first slide") performed identically each time; type does not matter, consistency does. Label: "One RCT (n = 66) for the breathing; sport meta-analysis for the routine."

**16. Window break.** Full-screen slow nature footage with natural sound for 3:00. "Just look. Let your eyes wander; notice what catches you." Then an optional 2:00 idea sprint. Offer "take this at a window or outside" as the first choice. Copy: "Lifts mood and idea originality; does not reliably restore attention in short doses."

**17. WOOP the week.** Four timed sub-steps, sequence enforced: Wish (0:30, 3 to 6 words, challenging but feasible); Outcome (1:00, no typing, "imagine the best result as vividly as you can"); Obstacle (1:00, "what inside you most often gets in the way"); Plan (1:00, "If [obstacle], then I will [action]"). Show the plan back at the start of each block. Check wording against Oettingen's official WOOP materials before shipping.

**18. Energy check.** "Hours slept: [slider]." Under 6: "Plan shallow or collaborative work for your hardest hour; protect tonight's sleep." "Daylight yet? If not, 10 minutes outside before your first deep block." "13:00 to 15:00: book admin and meetings, not your hardest creative work." No judgement, no score, no history visible to anyone else.

**19. Daily shared puzzle.** One puzzle per day, identical for everyone, 1:00 to 3:00, word-based (a crossword mini or a three-word remote-associates triad: "cottage / swiss / cake"). A 30-second timer per triad with a hint at 20 seconds; afterwards "Flash or worked out?" as a self-awareness tag only. Silence enforced for the triad. The result card is spoiler-free (guess count or time bucket) and posts to the team feed with kudos reactions; show the team distribution, never a ranked list. No second puzzle on demand.

**20. Reaction check.** A 3:00 PVT-B: random 2 to 10 second intervals, tap on the stimulus; display lapses over 355 ms and mean 1/RT against the person's own baseline. Copy: "How rested are you today? This mirrors sleep, not ability." Never compared across people.

**21. Reset after a slip.** Shown after a missed target. "You did not get to [task]. That happens to everyone; forgive yourself for it (one sentence)." "What is the 5-minute first step now?" (returns to One If-Then). "Pair it with something you enjoy for the duration: [playlist / coffee / podcast]."

**22. Ten-minute nap.** Only if the office has a dim, quiet space. "Lie back, eyes closed, 10 minutes of sleep opportunity. Hard wake-up." Copy: "10 minutes helped; 20 to 30 left people groggy for half an hour or more."

**23. Experimental shelf (opt-in, labelled "experimental / single study").** Edison nap: show the problem for 2:00, then "Recline, dim the lights, hold a light object over the chair arm, let yourself drift; when it drops or images appear, open your eyes and write for 3 minutes," hard alarm at 20:00, once a day after lunch. Open sky: 5:00 to 8:00 audio-guided open monitoring ("Let attention be wide. Label each sound, thought, itch lightly and let it pass. No breath anchor."), then a 3:00 sprint. Green Eggs: the brief plus a random constraint ("must include the word hinge", "no colour", "for someone who hates us"), 3:00 constrained, then 2:00 unconstrained. Cyclic sighing: 5:00 of double nasal inhale and long mouth exhale, labelled "one RCT, no independent replication". Best possible week (Friday, 3:00): "Imagine the end of next week went as well as it realistically could. Write it in the present tense."

The following should not be built, or should carry a visible "folklore" tag if anyone insists: any game marketed as brain, memory or focus training; a 30 to 60 second breathing "reset"; a 90-minute focus-cycle preset or 52/17 preset; binaural-beat or ASMR audio sold as focus or stress tools; power-pose or "willpower tank" prompts; mind-wandering or boredom-induction exercises; blue UI themes, coffee-shop noise generators or "dim the lights" prompts for ideation; "imagine it is a year away" construal prompts; self-imposed deadlines with penalties as a default (retracted); "rituals reduce anxiety" copy (retracted); "write your worries before the pitch" sold as performance enhancement (failed replication); "phone in another room" as a cognitive rule; "23 minutes to refocus" or "multitasking costs 40%" copy; "eat the frog" or chronotype scheduling as productivity science; 4-7-8 breathing as an acute reset; cold water on the wrists as a stress reset; and 20-20-20 labelled as evidence-based (keep it as a comfort prompt).

Measure the product honestly: the KPI is 30-day active use (the consumer benchmark is 3.3%), plus self-rated energy before and after each break type and idea counts with peer-rated originality after each "Generate" screen, so the team learns its own effect sizes rather than borrowing the lab's.

## Conclusion

The striking pattern across four separate literatures is that the interventions that replicate are cheap, dull and slightly embarrassing to market, while the ones with memorable names and TED-talk numbers are the ones that fall over: two of the most cited productivity papers were retracted within the last two years, the 40-second nature view and the 70 dB coffee shop have never been reproduced, and an entire commercial category built on brain training has a far-transfer effect of zero. The practical consequence for a creative agency is that the Break section should be built as a small set of protocols with a dose (five minutes of six-breaths-per-minute, ten minutes of free walking, two minutes of if-then planning, a scheduled break that is actually a break) wrapped in engagement mechanics that borrow from health-behaviour field trials rather than from mobile games.

Two implications go beyond the build list. First, the divergent/convergent split should be a first-class concept in the team OS, not a Break detail: walking, happy music, nature footage and silent brainwriting belong before idea generation, and silence, caffeine and a fresh night's sleep belong before decisions and edits; a team that routes its rituals by mode will get more from the modest effects available than one that treats "creativity" as a single dial. Second, because every effect here is measured on lab tasks minutes after the manipulation, the product's most valuable feature may be its own measurement: a studio of twelve logging energy and idea originality against break type for a quarter will know more about what works for its people than any of the papers cited above, and will have the standing to tell its clients so.
